The other chapters in this part rent things: a room, an RV, a car, a jacket. A services marketplace rents a person's time. The dog walker, the house sitter, the handyman and the babysitter are the inventory, and they do the work inside the customer's home, often alone, often with a key, and often in charge of something that can't be repaired or replaced at actual cash value: a dog, a cat, a child, an elderly parent. That changes what onboarding has to check (a criminal record matters more than a VIN), what a guarantee is promising (a vet bill, not a dent), what the calendar is counting (a caregiver's hours and a boarding facility's capacity, not one asset's nights), and what the platform's control over the work implies about whether the worker is its employee.

This chapter covers what is different when the listing is a person doing a service: how the platforms do it across pet care (Rover, Wag!, Yourgi), home services (Angi, Thumbtack, Taskrabbit) and family care (Care.com, Sittercity); the split between lead generation and booked-and-paid models; two-tier supply, where independent caregivers sit beside licensed businesses that run their own software; background checks under the FCRA and what they can't prove; boarding and kennel licensing; where a guarantee stops and insurance starts, with the vet emergency as the worked case; in-home access and visit evidence; recurring care; and guarantee credits and rewards as a liability. It doesn't repeat the gates, calendars, fees, claims, reviews and cancellations the earlier chapters cover; it extends them. Whether a caregiver is an employee, and the gig-worker law around it, belongs to [Gig Work & Worker Law](/books/user-generated-platforms/gig-work-and-worker-law).

- **The person is checked, not the asset.** Every platform runs a criminal background check, an identity check or both, and none of them claims the check proves the person is safe. The check is a consumer report with legal duties attached, which is where this chapter starts.
- **Lead or booking decides what the platform knows.** A platform that sells a lead to a pro never sees the price, the schedule or whether the work happened; a platform that takes the booking and the payment sees all of it, and can guarantee it.
- **Guarantees are written not to be insurance.** Wag! says so in capitals; Rover calls its program a commercial agreement backed by its own general liability policy above a retention; Taskrabbit says it "does not insure"; Yourgi pays in coupons. The construction (discretion, lifetime caps, minimums, excess of the customer's own insurance) is the industry's answer to state insurance law.
- **Supply comes in two tiers.** Individuals board a few dogs in their homes; licensed resorts and kennels board dozens under a state or local license and run their own software. A marketplace that mixes them needs both a license gate and an integration.
- **Most of the revenue is repeat business.** Rover reported that repeat bookings were 83% of its 2022 bookings. A weekly walk or a recurring cleaning is a schedule, not a sequence of unrelated reservations.

## Leads or Bookings

## Two-Tier Supply

A pet care marketplace has two kinds of supplier for the same search:

- **Independents:** a person who walks dogs, drops in, house-sits in the owner's home, or boards a few pets in their own. Rover's and Wag!'s 10-Ks list boarding in the caregiver's home as a core service. The independent's capacity is their own time and how many animals they'll take at once.
- **Licensed businesses:** a resort, kennel, daycare or grooming salon, with a facility, staff, a license, and its own booking system. Yourgi lists "local pet resorts" next to individual Pros. Home services have the same split (a solo handyman and a licensed plumbing company with dispatchers), and the field operations side of that business is the [Field Operations](/books/field-operations/field-operations-business) volume.

The two tiers break different assumptions in the core model. The core `bookings` table allows one confirmed booking per listing per period, which is right for a house and wrong for a boarding sitter who takes three dogs from three families on the same nights, and wrong for a resort with forty runs. Capacity is a counter, not an exclusion ([Counters for Pooled Inventory](/docs/architecture/reservations-and-holds#counters-for-pooled-inventory)): pets per night for a sitter's home, runs by size for a resort, visit slots for a walker. A walker's drop-ins, by contrast, do exclude each other, because one person can't be in two homes at once, so time exclusion moves from the listing to the caregiver.

### Integrating a Facility's System

A resort's inventory lives in its own software. Gingr publishes an API reference, and PetExec documents an API for developers building on its customers' accounts; neither public page describes an availability or capacity endpoint, so a marketplace typically derives open capacity from the facility's reservation list or asks the facility to hold an allotment. <!-- source-gap: facility-availability-api | missing | whether Gingr or PetExec expose an availability or capacity endpoint | closes with: the vendors' public API reference listing such an endpoint | checked: 2026-10-28 --> The patterns from [External Calendars](/books/user-generated-platforms/availability-and-booking#external-calendars) apply with one difference: iCal blocks a whole listing, while a facility needs counts by unit type (runs by dog size, suites, cat condos), and a booking has to land in the facility's system with the pet's vaccination records before check-in. My default is an allotment the facility controls, written back to its system on every booking, with the facility's confirmation as the step that moves the marketplace booking from requested to confirmed.

### Licensing: Boarding in Someone's Home

The license gate is per jurisdiction and per tier, and the hard case is the independent who boards several animals in their own home, because the law may treat them as a facility:

- **Colorado.** The Pet Animal Care Facilities Act defines a pet animal facility as any place "used in whole or in part" for keeping pet animals for, among other purposes, boarding, grooming or handling (C.R.S. 35-80-102). The Department of Agriculture's rules define a boarding/training facility as one that "cares for, or houses more than three pet animals at one time, or trains pet animals in the absence of the owner" for compensation, and a pet handler as one without a permanent facility that transports or trains pets in the owner's absence (8 CCR 1202-15, Part 1). Each location is licensed separately, licenses expire every March 1, and the legislature's staff describe operating or advertising without a license as a deceptive trade practice under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act. My reading is that a sitter in Denver housing a fourth paying dog at once is a boarding facility under the rule. Neither the statute nor the rule addresses care in the owner's own home. <!-- source-gap: co-in-home-sitting | missing | whether Colorado's PACFA requires a license for pet sitting in the owner's home | closes with: a Colorado Department of Agriculture statement or rule on in-home sitting | checked: 2026-10-28 -->
- **Massachusetts.** A "commercial boarding or training kennel" boards, holds, provides day care, overnight stays or training for animals the operator doesn't own, for payment, without the owner present; an individual who temporarily cares for others' animals outside the normal course of business is excluded (M.G.L. c.140 §136A). Kennels are licensed by the city or town after an animal control officer's inspection (§137A). Ollie's Law (§174G) directs the state to make licensing, inspection and enforcement rules for commercial boarding and training kennels, including those "located at a private residence," and requires kennels to report injuries to animals or people to the licensing authority. <!-- source-gap: ma-ollies-law-rules | status | whether the Department of Agricultural Resources rules under Ollie's Law are final and their citation | closes with: the final regulation in the Code of Massachusetts Regulations | checked: 2026-10-28 -->
- **Texas and Oregon.** I found no state boarding license in either. Texas's licensing department licenses dog and cat breeders, not boarding; Oregon defines a boarding kennel (ORS 609.035) but licenses dogs through counties. Local ordinances are where boarding rules would live, and I couldn't source a Dallas, Houston or Portland boarding permit. <!-- source-gap: tx-or-boarding-license | missing | that Texas and Oregon have no state boarding license and which local permits apply in Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston and Portland | closes with: the cities' or counties' kennel or boarding ordinances | checked: 2026-10-28 -->

Home services carry trade licenses (plumbing, electrical, pest control), which the [Licenses & Permits](/books/field-operations/licensing-and-permits#a-licensing-model) chapter models. The marketplace version is the same credential record with one change: the platform verifies it against the state lookup ([Verifying Against State Lookups](/books/field-operations/licensing-and-permits#verifying-against-state-lookups)) and gates the service, not the account, so a sitter who passes the boarding threshold in Colorado can keep walking dogs while the boarding offer waits for a license. That is [gate the act, not the account](/books/user-generated-platforms/supply-onboarding#the-onboarding-gates) with a jurisdictional rule.

## Vetting: Background Checks

A background report from a consumer reporting agency is a consumer report, and using one to decide whether someone can work brings the FCRA's employment-purpose duties: a clear and conspicuous disclosure "in a document that consists solely of the disclosure," the person's written authorization (15 U.S.C. 1681b(b)(2)), and before any adverse action "based in whole or in part on the report," a copy of the report and the summary of rights (1681b(b)(3)), followed by the adverse action notice. The FTC's employer guidance adds that the disclosure "cannot be in an employment application." The mechanics are in [Adverse Action Notices](/books/property-tech/applications-and-screening#adverse-action-notices) and, for workers, [Recruiting and Onboarding](/books/field-operations/sales-teams-and-commissions#recruiting-and-onboarding); [Sanctions Screening and Background Checks](/books/user-generated-platforms/marketplace-fraud#sanctions-screening-and-background-checks) covers the narrower use on guests.

Two twists apply to services marketplaces:

- **The worker isn't an employee, and the duties may apply anyway.** In a 1998 staff letter the FTC said a trucking operation using consumer reports to decide whether to engage drivers must follow the employment-purpose provisions, and its 2011 staff report says "employment purposes" may apply where an entity uses people "who are not technically employees." Courts have split on contractors. <!-- source-gap: fcra-contractor-caselaw | secondary | that courts have split on whether FCRA employment-purpose duties cover independent contractors (e.g. Smith v. Mutual of Omaha, S.D. Iowa 2018) | closes with: the opinions themselves | checked: 2026-10-28 --> My default is to run the full disclosure, authorization and two-step adverse action flow for every provider, because the cost is a few notices and the alternative is a class action over the theory.
- **The customer may be the one ordering the check.** Sittercity doesn't check every sitter: the sitter or a parent buys a Checkr check, a Sittercity admin can request one, and only the person checked sees the report, while the parent sees a badge that lasts 90 days. Sittercity's own guidance adds that records vary by state, county and city, that there is "no single source of all crime everywhere," and that a result reflects one point in time. Care.com runs an initial check on every individual caregiver, renews it annually while they're active, and sells optional checks of criminal, social media and driving history. When a family pays for a check, the platform has to decide who the user of the report is, who owes the adverse action notice, and what the family may see; a badge with an expiry, not the report, is the answer both make.

### What a Check Can't Prove

The platforms say it themselves. Care.com's safety page says checks are "not always 100% accurate" and "may not reveal a person's complete criminal or sex offender history"; Taskrabbit's terms say it can't "assume any responsibility or liability for the accuracy" of check results; Yourgi's say it doesn't independently verify them. The law adds limits of its own:

- **Seven years, except convictions.** A consumer report may not include civil suits, judgments and arrest records older than seven years, or other adverse items older than seven years "other than records of convictions of crimes" (1681c(a)). The limits don't apply to a job at $75,000 a year or more (1681c(b)(3)), which no caregiver's earnings will reach, so the seven-year rule applies to arrests.
- **Public records need care.** An agency reporting adverse public records for employment must notify the consumer at the time or keep strict procedures so the record is "complete and up to date" (1681k(a)).
- **State and local fair-chance laws** narrow what may be considered and when; the jurisdiction is the provider's, and the [Criminal History and Fair-Chance Laws](/books/property-tech/applications-and-screening#criminal-history-and-fair-chance-laws) section shows the shape of the rules.
- **A check is about the past.** It says nothing about who else is in the provider's home when a dog is boarded there, or who comes along on a job.

Care.com's history is the industry's reference case. After press reports in March 2019 that the site had listed caregivers with police records who were later accused of crimes on the job, <!-- source-gap: care-com-2019-reports | secondary | the March 2019 press investigation of caregivers with police records on Care.com | closes with: the original Wall Street Journal article | checked: 2026-10-28 --> Care.com's April 2019 press release said it offered "three tiers of background checks that families can purchase," and its first-quarter 10-Q said it had decided "to implement additional screening on individual caregivers" in its US marketplace, disclosed a securities class action over its disclosures about screening, and a proposed settlement with district attorneys of about $4.9 million over auto-renewal and sex offender registry disclosures. By IAC's 2020 10-K, after IAC bought it, Care.com required every individual caregiver on its US matching platform to complete a check by a third-party consumer reporting agency, while warning that "no assurances can be provided regarding the future behavior of any caregiver." The FTC's 2024 action against Care.com was about something else (inflated job counts, unsupported earnings claims and a hard-to-cancel subscription, with $8.5 million for refunds), which is a reminder that the marketing claims about supply are regulated too.

### Continuous Monitoring

A check at signup goes stale. Care.com renews annually; Sittercity's badge expires after 90 days; Checkr sells continuous monitoring that reruns county, national database and sex offender registry searches every 30 days and reports arrests from booking data. Three rules make monitoring safe to run:

- **Consent covers it.** The disclosure and authorization have to describe ongoing checks, and a new authorization is collected if the original didn't.
- **Every new record is a new decision.** A monitoring hit that would remove a provider goes through pre-adverse action, a waiting period (Checkr's guidance calls 7 calendar days "generally considered an adequate waiting period") and the final notice, never an automatic deactivation. Since June 2025 Checkr pauses a pending adverse action when the person disputes the report, and cancels it if the report changes.
- **Bookings in flight are a safety question.** Between a serious hit and the final decision, the provider's future bookings may need to be paused or reassigned, with the customer told only what the law and fairness allow. That pause is a restriction with a reason, recorded like any other ([Account Standing](/books/user-generated-platforms/accounts-and-identity#account-standing)).

## Control and Classification

Whether a caregiver is an employee is a legal determination the [Gig Work & Worker Law](/books/user-generated-platforms/gig-work-and-worker-law) chapter covers, along with the federal rule's status. What's specific to services is California's referral agency exemption: Labor Code 2777 takes referral agencies out of the ABC test (leaving the older *Borello* test) for providers of listed services, which include "animal services, dog walking, dog grooming," minor home repair, moving, errands and furniture assembly, and defines animal services as "daytime and nighttime pet care including pet boarding." It excludes "in-home care," among others, so child and senior care don't get it. Rover's and Wag!'s 10-Ks both cite it in their classification risk factors.

The exemption's conditions read like a product spec for what the platform may not control. The provider must be free from the agency's control, deliver services under their own name with their own tools, keep their own clients, set or negotiate their own hours and terms, accept or reject clients without penalty, and set their own rates "without deduction by the referral agency." Taskrabbit's terms echo the last one word for word ("without deduction by Taskrabbit") and say it doesn't "supervise, scope, direct, control or monitor Taskers' work." Massachusetts applies the ABC test with criminal and civil remedies, including debarment (M.G.L. c.149 §148B). So the controls a services platform builds have a legal cost:

- **Rates:** the provider sets the price; the customer's fee is added on top rather than taken out, where the exemption matters.
- **Acceptance:** declining requests can't carry a penalty that looks like discipline; ranking by responsiveness is a judgment call for counsel, and [Supply Sanctions](/books/user-generated-platforms/availability-and-booking#supply-sanctions) has to be reviewed with that in mind.
- **Standards:** safety rules (vaccination requirements, leash rules, lockbox handling) are easier to defend as conditions of the platform's guarantee and the customer's instructions than as work rules. Visit evidence is collected to pay and to resolve disputes, and is never framed as supervision.

### Why They Aren't Insurance

California defines insurance as "a contract whereby one undertakes to indemnify another against loss, damage, or liability arising from a contingent or unknown event" (Insurance Code §22). New York's definition turns on a benefit that depends on a "fortuitous event," one "to a substantial extent beyond the control of either party," and treats making guaranty contracts "as a vocation and not as merely incidental" to another business as doing an insurance business (Insurance Law §1101). New York's insurance regulator has used the control test the other way, finding a cancel-for-any-reason refund not to be insurance because the trigger is "substantially under the control" of the customer. A pet's injury in a sitter's care is fortuitous from the owner's point of view, which is why the services guarantees are built to look incidental and discretionary: lifetime caps, minimum contributions, excess-of-insurance positioning, "at its discretion," coupons instead of cash, and the platform's own liability policy behind the program. The [Insurance Law: Who May Sell It](/books/user-generated-platforms/deposits-and-protection#insurance-law-who-may-sell-it) section covers producer licensing when a platform does sell real insurance; Wag! did that for pet insurance through an insurance comparison site whose subsidiary is a licensed producer.

The provider's own coverage is a separate layer. A pet sitter's general liability policy covers third-party injury and property damage; care, custody and control coverage is what covers the client's pets and property in the sitter's care; and a dishonesty bond covers theft, usually only after proof the accused committed a crime, with the bonding company then seeking restitution from them. A platform that asks providers for proof of insurance stores the certificate as a credential with an expiry ([Bonds and Certificates of Insurance](/books/field-operations/licensing-and-permits#bonds-and-certificates-of-insurance)).

### The Vet Emergency

The case every pet platform designs for is a sick or injured animal while the owner is unreachable. The pieces:

- **Authorization before the booking.** Yourgi's terms have the pet parent authorize the provider, their emergency contact or Yourgi to obtain veterinary care if they can't be reached, and make the owner responsible for the cost. The authorization is a versioned document acknowledged per booking, with the owner's vet of record and, where the platform allows it, a spending ceiling.
- **An escalation ladder.** Owner, then emergency contact, then the platform's support line, then the nearest emergency vet, each attempt timestamped. Wag! advertises support around the clock for this reason.
- **The guarantee's clock.** Wag!'s 72-hour notice, 7-day documentation and 30-day cost windows start at the injury, so the incident record captures the time it happened, not the time someone filed a form.
- **Who pays the vet now.** The provider is at the clinic with no money of the owner's; the guarantee reimburses later and excludes pre-existing conditions. The platform needs a policy for paying the clinic directly, reimbursing the provider, or charging the owner's card on file under the authorization, and the ledger needs all three.

## In-Home Trust and Safety

The service happens where the platform can't see it, so the system's job is to make the visit observable after the fact and to control the things that let a stranger into a home.

- **Meet-and-greet first.** My default is a recorded meet-and-greet before the first booking with a new provider, where the owner and provider agree on care instructions, access and the emergency plan. The thread is the record ([Messaging as the Transaction Record](/books/user-generated-platforms/messaging-and-reviews#messaging-as-the-transaction-record)), and the instructions become a structured part of the booking, because "didn't follow your care instructions" is a Yourgi Guarantee trigger and needs instructions to compare against.
- **Access is a credential.** A key, lockbox code, door code or smart-lock grant is a secret: stored encrypted ([Envelope Encryption](/docs/security/encryption-at-rest#envelope-encryption)), revealed to the assigned caregiver only inside the booking's window, logged on every reveal, and handed back or rotated when the booking or series ends. A substitute caregiver gets a new grant, never a forwarded code.
- **Visit proof is evidence and payroll.** Wag!'s 10-K describes report cards with photos and videos and GPS mapping of walks; check-in, check-out, route, photos and notes are the visit's record. They pay the provider (a visit with no check-in isn't paid automatically), answer the owner, and become evidence for guarantee claims and chargebacks ([Evidence for Disputes and Chargebacks](/books/field-operations/proof-of-service#evidence-for-disputes-and-chargebacks)). A location fix is evidence, not truth ([Evidence, Not Truth](/docs/architecture/location-tracking#evidence-not-truth)), and the record keeps accuracy and device time beside server time, as [The Handoff](/books/user-generated-platforms/damage-and-claims#the-handoff) does for vehicles.
- **Incidents are their own record.** An escape, a bite, an injury, an illness, a death or a safety threat is reported once, with the time it happened, the animal or person involved, who was notified and when, and what followed (vet visit, guarantee case, report to a licensing authority where the law requires one, as Massachusetts does for kennels). A bite history follows the animal, because the next provider needs it, and the owner's statement of the pet's behavior at booking is evidence when it was wrong.
- **People, not just pets.** Child and senior care add a person who can't consent to the arrangement. Those platforms keep less: who the caregiver is, the booking and the safety reports, not a dossier on the child. The child-safety obligations elsewhere in this volume ([Child Safety: Detection & Reporting](/books/user-generated-platforms/child-safety)) apply to the messaging around them.

## Recurring Care

A daily walk is a subscription to a person. Wag!'s 10-K separates pre-scheduled from on-demand walks, and Rover's repeat bookings were most of its volume. The recurrence engine is the one in [Recurring Schedules](/books/field-operations/recurring-schedules#rfc-5545-as-the-rule-language) (an RFC 5545 rule, a generation horizon, exceptions), and [Missed Visits and Make-Ups](/books/field-operations/recurring-schedules#missed-visits-and-make-ups) covers what happens when a visit can't be done. What a marketplace adds:

- **The series belongs to a pair.** It's the owner and this caregiver, and the trust in the arrangement is personal. When the caregiver can't do a visit, the substitute is a different person entering the home, so the default is to ask the owner, with an opt-in to a pre-approved backup who has already met the pet. A substitute visit carries its own access grant, its own caregiver of record and its own payout.
- **Billing per visit or per period.** Per-visit billing charges each completed visit and refunds nothing; per-period billing (a week of walks) needs credits for missed visits and a policy for a caregiver's absence. [Four Billing Models](/books/field-operations/billing-recurring-service#four-billing-models) covers the choice; on a marketplace each visit is also a payout to a person, so the payout follows the visit even when the customer pays by period.
- **The platform's own subscription is different.** Wag! Premium is a monthly or annual membership for discounts, waived booking fees and priority support; it renews under automatic-renewal law ([The Law of Automatic Renewal](/docs/payments/subscriptions#the-law-of-automatic-renewal)), the issue in the FTC's Care.com case. The care series is a schedule of bookings; the membership is a subscription; don't make one object do both.

## Credits and Rewards Are a Liability

Services platforms pay customers in platform money more than other marketplaces do: guarantee remedies as coupons, rewards on spend, and goodwill credits after a bad visit. Yourgi's program is a clear published example. Rewards accrue on eligible bookings at 2.5% up to $750 of annual spend (Bronze), 3.75% from $750 to $1,250 (Silver) and 5.0% above $1,250 (Gold); points "carry no cash value," expire "at the end of the twelfth month from the date earned," may be revoked "at any time and for any reason," and convert to a credit redeemable with participating providers. Its guarantee remedies are coupon codes valid 12 months. Each of those is a different liability:

| Balance | How it arises | Accounting | Consumer and property law |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Purchased** (gift cards) | Customer pays cash | A liability until redeemed; breakage recognized as redemption patterns allow ([Breakage](/docs/payments/accounting#breakage)) | Federal gift card rule: funds can't expire within 5 years of issue or last load, dormancy fees limited (Reg E §1005.20); California bars expiration and requires cash redemption under $15 from April 1, 2026 (Civil Code §1749.5); unclaimed property dormancy varies by state |
| **Earned** (rewards on spend) | Part of the price the customer paid | A material right: part of each booking's price is deferred and recognized when the points are redeemed or expire (ASC 606-10-55-42) | Loyalty and promotional cards are excluded from the federal rule if they disclose they're promotional and their expiry; California exempts awards and loyalty certificates given without value in exchange |
| **Remedial** (guarantee coupons) | Settling a customer's claim | A cost or revenue reduction when issued (Rover's 10-K treats coupons and refunds as a reduction of revenue), with the expected redemption estimated | A credit given to settle a claim isn't plainly "without value in exchange"; whether it can expire is a question for counsel by state |
| **Promotional** | Marketing | Recognized as redeemed | Promotional disclosures; state rules vary |

What the build needs is the record that makes each treatment possible: every grant with its source, program version, amount, issue date, expiry, cash value or none, and the customer's jurisdiction; every redemption, expiry, revocation and escheat as a ledger entry; and a redemption order that's stated in the terms (my default is oldest-expiring first). State unclaimed property dormancy for gift certificates ranges from three years (California, Texas) to five (Colorado, whose act covers stored value cards, and New York), so a purchased balance's jurisdiction is stored when it's issued. Breakage estimates never expire a balance in the product that the terms and the law say is still spendable. [Unclaimed balances](/books/payments/payouts-and-settlement#payout-failures) owed to providers are the Payments volume's side of the same problem.

Tiers are computed, not stored: the tier is a view over the trailing year's eligible spend, so a refund moves a customer back across a threshold the next time it runs, and the rate in force on each booking is frozen on the booking.

## How to Build It

The chapter widens `verifications` once more, adds providers, capacity, care subjects, background checks, licenses, series and visits, access grants, incidents and a credit ledger:

```sql
-- Supply Onboarding's widening, plus the two kinds this chapter adds.
ALTER TABLE verifications
  DROP CONSTRAINT verifications_kind_check,
  ADD CONSTRAINT verifications_kind_check CHECK (kind IN ('identity', 'notability',
    'organization', 'phone', 'email', 'payment_instrument', 'driver_license', 'business',
    'tax_id', 'bank_account', 'background_check', 'facility_license'));

CREATE TABLE service_providers (         -- the supply side of a services listing
  account_id       UUID PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES accounts (id),
  tier             TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (tier IN ('independent', 'business')),
  business_name    TEXT,
  facility_address JSONB,                -- where boarding happens; a home for independents
  service_area     JSONB,                -- radius or polygon for visits
  external_system  TEXT,                 -- 'gingr', 'petexec', ... for businesses
  external_ref     TEXT,                 -- location id in that system
  sync_mode        TEXT CHECK (sync_mode IN ('allotment', 'reservation_list', 'manual'))
);

CREATE TABLE capacity_counters (         -- pooled inventory: pets a night, runs, slots
  provider_id   UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES service_providers (account_id),
  unit          TEXT NOT NULL,           -- 'pet_night', 'run_large', 'cat_condo', ...
  day           DATE NOT NULL,
  capacity      INT NOT NULL CHECK (capacity >= 0),  -- license and provider limits applied
  held          INT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 CHECK (held >= 0),
  PRIMARY KEY (provider_id, unit, day),
  CHECK (held <= capacity)
);

ALTER TABLE bookings
  ADD COLUMN exclusive BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT TRUE;  -- false when capacity_counters decide
ALTER TABLE bookings                     -- Postgres's default name for the core exclusion
  DROP CONSTRAINT bookings_listing_id_service_period_excl,
  ADD CONSTRAINT bookings_listing_period_excl EXCLUDE USING GIST
    (listing_id WITH =, service_period WITH &&)
    WHERE (status IN ('confirmed', 'in_progress') AND exclusive);

CREATE TABLE care_subjects (             -- the pet, or the person cared for
  id               UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  owner_account_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES accounts (id),
  kind             TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('dog', 'cat', 'other_pet', 'child',
                     'adult')),
  name             TEXT NOT NULL,
  attributes       JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}',  -- breed, weight, size class; minimal for people
  care_notes       TEXT,                 -- feeding, medication, behavior, as the owner states
  medical_ref      TEXT,                 -- vaulted; vet of record and conditions
  created_at       TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW()
);

CREATE TABLE subject_vaccinations (
  subject_id    UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES care_subjects (id),
  vaccine       TEXT NOT NULL,           -- 'rabies', 'dhpp', 'bordetella', ...
  administered_on DATE NOT NULL,
  expires_on    DATE NOT NULL,
  evidence_asset_id UUID REFERENCES media_assets (id),
  verified_by   TEXT CHECK (verified_by IN ('vet_record', 'facility', 'owner_attested')),
  PRIMARY KEY (subject_id, vaccine, administered_on)
);

CREATE TABLE booking_subjects (
  booking_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES bookings (id),
  subject_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES care_subjects (id),
  instructions     TEXT,                 -- frozen care instructions for this booking
  PRIMARY KEY (booking_id, subject_id)
);

CREATE TABLE care_authorizations (       -- emergency vet consent, per booking
  id               UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  booking_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES bookings (id),
  kind             TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('emergency_vet', 'medication', 'transport')),
  spending_limit   BIGINT,               -- minor units; null means the terms' default
  vet_of_record    JSONB,
  emergency_contact JSONB NOT NULL,
  document_version TEXT NOT NULL,
  authorized_at    TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL
);

CREATE TABLE background_checks (         -- one consumer report request and its decision
  id                  UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  account_id          UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES accounts (id),
  verification_id     UUID REFERENCES verifications (id),  -- set when it passes
  vendor              TEXT NOT NULL,
  vendor_ref          TEXT NOT NULL,
  package             TEXT NOT NULL,     -- 'basic_criminal', 'enhanced', 'mvr', 'monitoring_hit'
  ordered_by          TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (ordered_by IN ('platform', 'customer', 'self')),
  disclosure_version  TEXT NOT NULL,     -- the stand-alone disclosure shown
  authorized_at       TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  monitoring_consent  BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE,
  jurisdiction        TEXT NOT NULL,     -- ISO 3166-2; fair-chance rules resolve from here
  status              TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (status IN ('pending', 'clear', 'consider',
                        'pre_adverse', 'disputed', 'adverse', 'approved', 'expired')),
  pre_adverse_sent_at TIMESTAMPTZ,       -- with the report copy and summary of rights
  final_decision_after TIMESTAMPTZ,      -- the waiting period, stored
  adverse_sent_at     TIMESTAMPTZ,
  criteria_version    TEXT,              -- the adjudication rules applied
  badge_valid_until   TIMESTAMPTZ,       -- what customers see, when a badge is shown
  created_at          TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW()
);

CREATE TABLE provider_credentials (      -- facility licenses, trade licenses, insurance
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  provider_id     UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES service_providers (account_id),
  kind            TEXT NOT NULL,         -- 'co_pacfa_boarding', 'ma_kennel', 'liability_coi', ...
  jurisdiction    TEXT NOT NULL,
  number          TEXT,
  location        JSONB,                 -- licenses are per location
  valid_during    DATERANGE NOT NULL,
  verified_by     TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (verified_by IN ('state_lookup', 'document', 'attested')),
  verified_at     TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL
);

CREATE TABLE booking_series (            -- a recurring arrangement between two people
  id               UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  listing_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES listings (content_item_id),
  guest_account_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES accounts (id),
  provider_id      UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES service_providers (account_id),
  rrule            TEXT NOT NULL,        -- RFC 5545
  dtstart          TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  visit_window     INTERVAL NOT NULL,    -- 'within 2 hours of the start'
  billing_basis    TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (billing_basis IN ('per_visit', 'per_period')),
  substitute_policy TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (substitute_policy IN ('ask', 'approved_backups',
                     'skip')),
  status           TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (status IN ('active', 'paused', 'ended')),
  guarantee_version INT NOT NULL         -- frozen like a protection plan
);

CREATE TABLE visits (                    -- one occurrence of care, the unit of pay and proof
  id               UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  booking_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES bookings (id),
  series_id        UUID REFERENCES booking_series (id),
  caregiver_id     UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES accounts (id),  -- a substitute is a new caregiver
  substitute_for   UUID REFERENCES accounts (id),
  owner_approved_at TIMESTAMPTZ,         -- required when substitute_for is set
  scheduled        TSTZRANGE NOT NULL,
  status           TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (status IN ('scheduled', 'checked_in', 'completed',
                     'missed', 'cancelled')),
  check_in         JSONB,                -- server time, device time, position, accuracy
  check_out        JSONB,
  route_ref        TEXT,                 -- the walk's track, in the evidence store
  report           TEXT,                 -- the report card's notes; photos in content_media
  EXCLUDE USING GIST (caregiver_id WITH =, scheduled WITH &&)
    WHERE (status IN ('scheduled', 'checked_in')),  -- one home at a time
  CHECK ((substitute_for IS NULL) OR (owner_approved_at IS NOT NULL))
);

CREATE TABLE access_grants (             -- keys and codes to a home
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  booking_id      UUID REFERENCES bookings (id),
  series_id       UUID REFERENCES booking_series (id),
  grantee_id      UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES accounts (id),
  kind            TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('physical_key', 'lockbox', 'door_code',
                    'smart_lock', 'concierge')),
  secret_ref      TEXT,                  -- envelope-encrypted; never in this table
  reveal_during   TSTZRANGE NOT NULL,
  returned_at     TIMESTAMPTZ,           -- key back, code rotated or grant revoked
  CHECK ((booking_id IS NULL) <> (series_id IS NULL))
);

CREATE TABLE access_reveals (            -- append-only: who saw the code, when, from where
  grant_id    UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES access_grants (id),
  revealed_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW(),
  device_ref  TEXT
);

CREATE TABLE care_incidents (
  id               UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  booking_id       UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES bookings (id),
  visit_id         UUID REFERENCES visits (id),
  subject_id       UUID REFERENCES care_subjects (id),
  kind             TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('escape', 'bite', 'injury', 'illness',
                     'death', 'property_damage', 'safety_threat')),
  occurred_at      TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,  -- starts the guarantee's clocks
  reported_at      TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW(),
  owner_notified_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  vet_visit        JSONB,
  authority_report_due TIMESTAMPTZ,      -- where a licensing authority must be told
  authority_reported_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  protection_case_id UUID REFERENCES protection_cases (id)
);

CREATE TABLE credit_grants (             -- platform money owed to a customer
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  account_id      UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES accounts (id),
  source          TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (source IN ('purchase', 'reward', 'guarantee',
                    'goodwill', 'promotion')),
  program_version TEXT NOT NULL,
  booking_id      UUID REFERENCES bookings (id),          -- the booking that earned it
  protection_case_id UUID REFERENCES protection_cases (id),  -- the claim it settled
  amount          BIGINT NOT NULL CHECK (amount > 0),
  currency        CHAR(3) NOT NULL,
  cash_value      BOOLEAN NOT NULL,      -- true only for purchased balances
  jurisdiction    TEXT NOT NULL,         -- the customer's, at issue
  issued_at       TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW(),
  expires_at      TIMESTAMPTZ            -- null where law or terms forbid expiry
);

CREATE TABLE credit_entries (            -- every movement of a grant's balance
  id         UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  grant_id   UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES credit_grants (id),
  kind       TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('issue', 'redeem', 'reverse_redeem', 'expire',
               'revoke', 'escheat')),
  amount     BIGINT NOT NULL,            -- signed
  booking_id UUID REFERENCES bookings (id),
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW()
);

CREATE VIEW reward_tiers AS              -- tier from trailing-year eligible spend
SELECT b.guest_account_id AS account_id,
       SUM(bl.amount) AS eligible_spend,
       CASE WHEN SUM(bl.amount) > 125000 THEN 'gold'
            WHEN SUM(bl.amount) > 75000 THEN 'silver'
            ELSE 'bronze' END AS tier      -- thresholds are program data in practice
FROM bookings b
JOIN booking_lines bl ON bl.booking_id = b.id
WHERE b.status = 'completed'
  AND bl.kind IN ('rental', 'add_on')    -- tips and taxes excluded
  AND b.created_at >= NOW() - INTERVAL '12 months'
GROUP BY b.guest_account_id;
```

Design decisions behind it:

- **Capacity and exclusion are both kept, on different things.** A boarding booking claims counters (pets a night at a sitter's home, runs at a resort) and sets `exclusive` false; a visit excludes on the caregiver's time, because one person can't be in two homes. Counters are capped by the lower of the provider's setting and any license threshold, so a Colorado sitter without a PACFA license can't be sold a fourth concurrent dog.
- **Care subjects are first-class.** The pet's vaccinations, care notes and bite history follow the animal across providers and bookings; the booking freezes the instructions given for it. For people, the record holds what the care needs and no more, and medical details live in the vault.
- **A background check is a consumer report workflow, not a boolean.** Disclosure version, authorization, the pre-adverse notice, the stored waiting period, disputes and the final notice are columns; the passing check becomes a `verifications` row with a validity range so the provider gate reads one model. Monitoring hits arrive as new checks with `package = 'monitoring_hit'` and go through the same flow.
- **Licenses gate offers.** A credential is per location and jurisdiction with a validity range; a boarding offer checks it at publish and at booking, as asset eligibility does for vehicles ([Asset Eligibility](/books/user-generated-platforms/supply-onboarding#asset-eligibility)).
- **The visit is the unit of proof and pay.** Check-in and check-out with device and server time, the route, the report and photos make a visit payable and are the evidence when a claim arrives; a missed visit is a status with a reason, not a deleted row.
- **Substitutes need consent in the schema.** A visit by someone other than the series' caregiver can't exist without the owner's approval timestamp, and it gets its own access grant.
- **Secrets are revealed, never stored in the clear.** Access codes are envelope-encrypted references with a reveal window and an append-only reveal log, so "who had the code on the day the TV went missing" has an answer.
- **Incidents start clocks.** `occurred_at` drives the guarantee's notice and document deadlines and any report owed to a licensing authority, stored as computed deadlines ([Deadlines Are Computed and Stored](/docs/architecture/rules-as-data#deadlines-are-computed-and-stored)).
- **Credits are a ledger.** Every grant has a source, a program version, a jurisdiction and an expiry the law allows; every movement is an entry; balances are sums. Finance estimates breakage from the entries; the product never expires a balance early because of the estimate.
- **Events:** `background_check.consider`, `background_check.adverse_sent`, `credential.expiring`, `visit.missed`, `visit.substitute_requested`, `incident.reported`, `access.revealed` and `credit.expiring` go through the outbox ([The Transactional Outbox](/docs/architecture/events#the-transactional-outbox)) to notifications, standing, payouts and support.

## Auditing

- **Every background check end to end:** the disclosure shown, the authorization, the report reference, the criteria applied, both notices with their delivery records, the waiting period, any dispute and the outcome, kept for the FCRA's limitation period and per [Retention Schedules by Record Type](/docs/architecture/data-retention#retention-schedules-by-record-type).
- **License and insurance gates over time:** which credential allowed each boarding booking, so a licensing authority's question about a provider has the record as it was on that night.
- **Visit evidence:** check-ins, routes, reports and photos per visit, retained through the guarantee's claim windows and the chargeback window.
- **Access:** every grant, reveal, return and rotation per home.
- **Incidents and their notifications:** who was told, when, and the report to an authority where one was due.
- **Guarantee decisions and the version in force,** including credits issued as remedies and their link to the claim.
- **Credit and reward ledgers:** issues, redemptions, expiries, revocations and escheat reports by jurisdiction, reconcilable to the liability on the balance sheet.

## Tradeoffs

- **Lead generation or bookings.** Selling leads is cheaper to build and avoids holding customer money, guarantee liability and visit evidence; it also gives up the data to guarantee anything, rank on outcomes or stop leakage. Booking and payment are worth the build when the platform wants to stand behind the work.
- **Licensed businesses or independents.** Resorts bring licensed capacity, staff and their own insurance, but they arrive with software the platform must integrate and margins that resist a take rate. Independents bring neighborhood coverage and a classification question. A marketplace that starts with one tier should model both from the start, because the capacity model differs.
- **Guarantees paid in credit.** Coupons keep the remedy inside the platform and cost less than cash, and customers can read them as a refusal; they also create a liability the accounting and the law treat by source. A cash reimbursement with a clear cap is simpler to explain and to account for.
- **Control.** Every quality control a services platform adds (required response times, uniform pricing, mandatory training) improves the customer's experience and weakens the independent contractor position. When the controls are what the customer is paying for, an employee or agency model may be the honest one, and the [Gig Work & Worker Law](/books/user-generated-platforms/gig-work-and-worker-law) chapter covers the tests.
- **Child and senior care.** The referral agency exemption doesn't cover in-home care, the safety stakes are higher, and checks alone don't make a caregiver safe. A family care marketplace that books and controls the work is close to a staffing agency, with that licensing and employment law; the subscription directory model Care.com and Sittercity use keeps the platform a directory with a background check product.

## What This Means for the System You Build

- **Treat the provider as the inventory:** time exclusion on the caregiver, capacity counters on homes and facilities, and the core model's one-booking-per-period rule only where it still fits.
- **Pick leads or bookings deliberately,** knowing a lead marketplace can only guarantee what the customer can prove.
- **Run the full FCRA flow for contractors,** with stand-alone disclosure, authorization, pre-adverse notice, a stored waiting period and the final notice, and route monitoring hits through the same flow.
- **Don't let a badge say more than the check proved,** and expire it.
- **Gate boarding offers on location licenses** where state or local law reaches into homes (Colorado above three animals, Massachusetts kennels including private residences).
- **Write guarantees so they stay guarantees:** caps, minimums, excess of insurance, discretion, a frozen program version, and real insurance sold only through a licensed producer.
- **Model the vet emergency before it happens:** a per-booking authorization, an escalation ladder, an incident record whose time starts the guarantee's clocks, and a payment path for the clinic.
- **Treat access codes as secrets** with reveal windows, logs and rotation, and give every substitute their own grant with the owner's approval.
- **Make the visit the unit of proof and payout,** linking to the field operations evidence model rather than inventing a new one.
- **Keep credits in a ledger by source,** with jurisdiction and lawful expiry on each grant, rewards as material rights, and breakage as finance's estimate, never the product's deletion.
