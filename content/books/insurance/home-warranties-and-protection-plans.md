Home warranties and consumer protection plans are service contracts run at industrial scale. A home warranty promises to repair or replace a home's systems and appliances (the furnace, the water heater, the dishwasher) for an annual price and a service fee per visit; a protection plan does the same for a phone, a television or a refrigerator. The contracts are simple; the operation behind them is not. frontdoor, the largest home warranty company, handles about 3.8 million service requests a year through about 17,000 contractor firms; Assurant runs about 1,150 repair and partner locations; Asurion cites more than 700 locations. The claim is not a check but a dispatch: a technician, a diagnosis, a part, a repair or a replacement, and the decision in the middle about which.

This chapter covers how states license home warranty companies (Florida, California and Texas worked), consumer product protection plans at scale and their economics, claims as repair-or-replace logistics, how the revenue is recognized against seasonal claims, and FTC enforcement. The general service contract regime (the obligor, administrator and reimbursement insurer, the NAIC model and refunds) is in [Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts); a field service company's own repair warranties and the equipment warranties on what it installs are in Field Operations' [Warranties and Guarantees](/books/field-operations/service-agreements#warranties-and-guarantees).

## Home Warranty Licensing

States regulate home warranties in three ways: as a separate licensed business under the insurance department, as a service contract under a non-insurance regulator, or not specifically. frontdoor's 10-K describes the patchwork from the regulated side: in certain states, "both our home warranty business and the new home builder warranty business are regulated by applicable state insurance regulatory authorities," naming the California Department of Insurance, the District of Columbia Department of Insurance, Securities & Banking, the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation and the Texas Department of Insurance, with other bodies that may also regulate them such as the Virginia Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services and the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation; and it provides "insurance for our home warranty claims in Texas via our wholly-owned captive insurance company."

**Florida (ch. 634, Part II).** A **home warranty** is "any contract or agreement whereby a person undertakes to indemnify the warranty holder" against the cost of repairing or replacing a home's structural components or appliances; a **home warranty association** is any organization "other than an authorized insurer, issuing home warranties" (634.301). The part doesn't prohibit a builder's or an appliance maker's usual performance guarantee if "no identifiable charge is made for such guarantee," never allows indemnification for consequential damages, and doesn't apply to a service warranty association's contract covering only systems and appliances (no structural components), which is regulated under Part III instead. The financial rules (634.3077):

- an unearned premium reserve of at least 25% of gross written premium on Florida contracts in force, in cash or eligible investments;
- net assets of at least one-sixth of written premium, or less with at least $500,000 of net assets and a 40% reserve;
- no reserve if contractual liability insurance covers 100% of claim exposure, provided the insurer pays losses and unearned premiums directly to holders if the association can't, "shall assume full responsibility for the administration of claims" in that case, gives 60 days' written notice to the office before cancelling or nonrenewing, covers every contract issued while the policy was in effect, and pays 100% of claims as incurred or as due;
- or a net worth of at least $100 million (own or parent's), with a parent guarantee and $750,000 of net assets in some cases.

**California.** A **home protection contract** is one in which "a person, other than a builder, seller, or lessor of the home" agrees for a set period and fee to repair or replace home components, systems or appliances damaged by wear, deterioration or inherent defect, provides a service system for those repairs, and doesn't cover consequential damage; a **home protection company** is "any person licensed pursuant to this part which issues home protection contracts" (Cal. Ins. Code 12740). The part sits in the Insurance Code and the licensing is the Department of Insurance's, which is why frontdoor names it.

**Texas.** Texas regulates home warranties as residential service contracts. Its service contract security statute sets a minimum deposit of $25,000 for residential service contract providers under the reserve option (Occ. Code 1304.151(b-4)), and frontdoor names both the Texas Department of Insurance and the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation among its regulators, and insures its Texas home warranty claims through its own captive. <!-- source-gap: tx-residential-service-companies | missing | the Texas statute that licenses residential service companies (Occupations Code ch. 1303), its current regulator and its financial requirements | closes with: the statute text opened on texas.public.law or statutes.capitol.texas.gov, or the TDLR program page | checked: 2026-11-10 --> I couldn't open the Texas residential service company chapter itself, so a system should load Texas's licensing and reserve rules from the statute once confirmed.

The design consequence is a per-state rule set for the same national product: in one state a licensed association with a reserve ratio, in another a licensed home protection company under the insurance department, in another a residential service contract registered with a licensing agency, and in a few no specific regime. The contract template, the reserve calculation, the complaint channel and the cancellation rule all key on the state and the regime.

## Consumer Product Protection Plans at Scale

A protection plan on a phone or an appliance is a service contract (or, where it covers theft and loss, an insurance policy; [Who May Sell It](/books/insurance/embedded-insurance#who-may-sell-it-four-licensing-models)) sold in very large numbers through a small number of partners. The economics are visible in the filings: Assurant describes its client agreements as exclusive and multi-year, "generally between three and five years," with up-front commission payments to clients "typically supported by various protections, such as letters of credit, letters of guarantee and real estate"; Allstate's protection plans carry four of every five Allstate policies.

What scale changes for the system:

- **The partner's catalog is the risk schedule.** Every SKU maps to a plan, a price band and a coverage schedule; Extend's documentation has a guide on keeping products "mapped appropriately with Extend Protection Plans" so offers can display. A product with no mapping gets no offer; a product mapped to the wrong band is mispriced on every sale.
- **Coverage starts after the manufacturer's warranty for breakdown,** and immediately for accidental damage where the plan covers it, so each contract has two coverage clocks.
- **Mobile plans run monthly.** Device protection billed on a carrier's monthly bill behaves like a subscription: coverage continues while paid, lapses on nonpayment, and the claim checks the payment status on the loss date.
- **Fulfillment is replacement inventory.** A covered phone is often replaced, not repaired; the claim is an order for a refurbished or new device, with a deductible or service fee collected first and the damaged device returned (and often refurbished and resold, a salvage stream like an auto total loss).
- **Fraud follows volume.** Claims shortly after enrollment, repeated claims on the same device, and devices that are never returned are the patterns any plan watches ([Fraud & SIU](/books/insurance/insurance-fraud)).

## Claims as Repair-or-Replace Logistics

A home warranty claim is a dispatch. The contract decides what's covered; the operation decides who goes, when, and what they're allowed to do.

1. **Service request.** The holder reports a failure (online or by phone) and pays the service fee. The request is a `claims` row with an exposure for the item (the HVAC system, the water heater).
2. **Coverage check.** The contract is in force, the item is on the plan, the failure is a covered cause (wear, deterioration or defect, never consequential damage under Florida's and California's definitions), and any waiting period has passed.
3. **Dispatch.** The request goes to a contractor by trade and location, preferred contractors first (frontdoor's preferred contractors did about 84% of its service requests in 2025), with an appointment window.
4. **Diagnosis.** The technician diagnoses the failure and reports: the failed component, the cause, whether it is repairable, and the repair estimate.
5. **Authorization.** The administrator approves a repair up to the contract's limits, or decides on replacement, or on cash in lieu of repair or replacement where the contract allows it. Above a threshold, a second approval or a second opinion applies.
6. **Parts and return visit.** Many repairs need a part; the claim waits on parts and a second visit, which is where the days go.
7. **Completion and payment.** The contractor invoices against the authorization and is paid; the holder pays anything not covered (code upgrades, permits, items over limits).

Home warranty claims "take approximately three months to settle, on average," frontdoor reports, and they are seasonal: "Seasonal fluctuations are primarily driven by a higher number of HVAC work orders," with about 29% and 30% of 2025 revenue in the second and third quarters. The repair-or-replace decision is the expensive one.

### Worked Numbers: Repair, Replace or Cash

My contract terms and numbers. A home warranty priced at $600 a year, a $125 service fee per request, and a $2,000 coverage limit per HVAC system. A ten-year-old air conditioner fails in July.

| Option | Cost to the warranty company | Holder pays | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repair: replace the failed capacitor and contactor | $380 | $125 service fee | Covered; done on the first visit |
| Repair: replace the compressor | $1,850 parts and labor | $125 | Covered, under the $2,000 limit; needs a part and a second visit |
| Replace the condenser unit ($5,800 installed) | $2,000, the limit | $125 + $3,800 over the limit | Code-required upgrades are excluded under my terms |
| Cash in lieu of the compressor repair | $1,400 offered | $125 already paid | My terms set cash below the $1,850 repair cost; the holder may take the cash and put it toward a new unit |

On the first branch the year's contract is still profitable ($600 + $125 against $380); on the second it isn't ($725 against $1,850); and the holder's choice on the last two decides whether a ten-year-old system is replaced at all. The decision rule, the limit and the cash-in-lieu formula are contract terms, so they are versioned with the contract form and stored on the authorization.

## Revenue, Reserves and Seasonality

frontdoor recognizes revenue "at the agreed upon contractual amount over time using the input method," measured against expected service costs under each contract, because "Home warranty contracts are typically one year in duration" and costs are not spread evenly over the year. Its claim accruals "are made using internal actuarial projections" and "are established based on estimates of the ultimate cost to settle claims," with a third-party actuary reviewing them. Two different measures sit side by side: the regulator's unearned premium reserve (Florida's 25% of gross written premium in force) and the company's GAAP deferred revenue under the input method.

### Worked Numbers: Earning a Home Warranty by Expected Cost

My numbers. A $600 annual contract starting January 1, with expected service costs by quarter of 18%, 29%, 30% and 23% (a summer-heavy pattern like the one frontdoor reports).

| Quarter | Pro rata by time | By expected cost (input method) | Florida unearned premium reserve held on the contract |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | $150 | $108 | 25% of gross written premium in force: $150 all year while in force |
| Q2 | $150 | $174 | $150 |
| Q3 | $150 | $180 | $150 |
| Q4 | $150 | $138 | $150 |
| Total | $600 | $600 | Released when the contract expires |

The ledger needs both. The GAAP schedule recognizes revenue by the cost curve (and the curve is an estimate, updated as experience comes in, so the schedule for new contracts changes while old contracts keep theirs); the statutory reserve is a percentage of in-force gross premium per state. A refund on cancellation uses neither: it follows the state's refund rule on the contract price ([Cancellation and Refunds](/books/insurance/service-contracts#cancellation-and-refunds)). Generic recognition schedules are in [Recognition Schedules](/docs/money/accounting#recognition-schedules).

## FTC Enforcement and Consumer Protection

Home warranties and protection plans meet federal law in three places:

- **Magnuson-Moss.** A seller that sells a service contract within 90 days of the sale can't disclaim implied warranties on the product (15 U.S.C. 2308), and warranty terms can't tie coverage to branded parts or service (16 CFR 700.10). The FTC's July 3, 2024 warning letters to eight companies, for conditioning warranties on specified parts or service and for "warranty void if removed" stickers, show where staff attention is ([Magnuson-Moss and the FTC](/books/insurance/service-contracts#magnuson-moss-and-the-ftc)).
- **Telemarketing.** frontdoor's 10-K names the FCC's TCPA and the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule among the rules on its sales practices; home warranty and vehicle service contract marketing by phone is the classic target of both ([Leads, Aggregators & Consent](/books/insurance/leads-and-consent)).
- **The CFPB.** Assurant's 10-K notes that the CFPB "does not have direct jurisdiction over insurance products" but "may take the position that it has the authority to regulate certain non-insurance consumer services we provide"; frontdoor lists the CFPB among its federal regulators. Service contracts are exactly those non-insurance services.

State enforcement is by the regulator for each regime (the insurance department in Florida and California, the licensing agency in Texas) and by attorneys general under consumer protection law. Recurring complaint themes are denials for preexisting conditions or lack of maintenance, slow dispatch in peak season, and the gap between the replacement cost and the coverage limit, which is why the claim records above keep the reason, the dates and the limit applied on every decision.

## How to Build It

These are deltas against [The Core Data Model](/books/insurance/insurance-business#the-core-data-model) and the service contract tables in [Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#how-to-build-it): a home warranty is a `policies` row with `legal_form = 'home_warranty'` and its `obligor_id` (the association or company), the home is a `risks` row of kind `dwelling` and each covered system or appliance a `risks` row of kind `covered_product`; the service fee is collected as a `payments` row against the claim; each request is a `claims` row with an `exposures` row per item; contractors are `parties` with a `vendor` role. Contractor payments are `claim_payments`. This chapter adds the dispatch:

```sql
CREATE TABLE service_dispatches (          -- one technician visit on one exposure
  id                 UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  exposure_id        UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES exposures (id),
  contractor_party_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),
  contractor_tier    TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (contractor_tier IN ('preferred', 'network',
                       'out_of_network')),
  trade              TEXT NOT NULL,         -- 'hvac', 'plumbing', 'appliance', 'electrical'
  visit_no           INT NOT NULL,          -- 1 = diagnosis; later visits for parts
  appointment_window TSTZRANGE NOT NULL,    -- the window offered to the holder
  arrived_at         TIMESTAMPTZ,
  diagnosis          JSONB,                 -- component, cause, repairable, estimate
  decision           TEXT CHECK (decision IN ('repair', 'replace', 'cash_in_lieu',
                       'not_covered', 'parts_ordered', 'second_opinion')),
  approved_amount    BIGINT,
  limit_applied      BIGINT,                -- the contract's per-item limit, as applied
  denial_reason      TEXT,                  -- 'preexisting', 'maintenance', 'consequential',
                                            -- 'not_listed', 'code_upgrade'
  parts_eta          DATE,
  completed_at       TIMESTAMPTZ,
  UNIQUE (exposure_id, visit_no)
);

CREATE TABLE revenue_curves (              -- expected cost by period, for the input method
  product_id         UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES products (id),
  version            INT NOT NULL,
  period_shares_bp   INT[] NOT NULL,        -- {1800, 2900, 3000, 2300} for quarters
  valid_for_sales    DATERANGE NOT NULL,    -- contracts sold in this window use this curve
  PRIMARY KEY (product_id, version)
);
```

Design choices that matter:

- **A dispatch is a visit, not a claim.** A compressor replacement with a diagnosis visit, a parts wait and a return visit is three rows on one exposure, so days-to-complete and first-visit fix rate come straight from the table.
- **The decision is recorded with its limit and reason** on the dispatch where it was made, so the repair-or-replace economics and every denial are reviewable.
- **Revenue curves are versioned by sale date.** A contract earns by the curve in force when it was sold; updating the curve for new sales never restates old contracts.
- **State regime per contract,** read from the obligor's authority in the contract's state ([The Parties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#the-parties)), selects the reserve, the template and the refund rule.

## Auditing

- **Licensed in every state sold,** under that state's regime, with the reserve, ratio, contractual liability insurance or net worth test met as of each reporting date.
- **No consequential damages paid or promised** where the state's definition excludes them, and the exclusion in the contract template.
- **Every denial with a reason and evidence** (the technician's diagnosis, maintenance history), and every limit applied matches the contract form version.
- **Service fees collected and refunded** by rule when a request is cancelled or found not covered, if the contract promises it.
- **Revenue recognized by the curve in force at sale,** reconciled to deferred revenue, and the statutory reserve computed separately per state.
- **Telemarketing consent** for every outbound sales call, kept with the lead.

## What This Means for the System You Build

- **Treat the claim as dispatch logistics:** visits, trades, contractor tiers, parts waits and the repair-or-replace decision as data.
- **Record each decision with the contract's limit and the reason,** on the visit where it was made.
- **Keep a per-state regime** for licensing, reserves, templates and refunds under one national product.
- **Run two measures of unearned:** the GAAP input-method curve and each state's statutory reserve.
- **Map every partner SKU to a plan** before an offer can show, and give protection plans two coverage clocks.
