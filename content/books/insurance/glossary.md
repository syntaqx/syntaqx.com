Insurance borrows its words from contract law, accounting, actuarial science and a century of state regulation, and the same word shifts with context: a reserve is an adjuster's estimate on one claim and an actuary's estimate for a whole accident year, an exposure is a claimant against a coverage in a claims system and the unit premium is charged on (a car-year, $100 of payroll) in a rating manual, a binder is a temporary contract on one desk and an MGA's authority on another, and a premium is written, earned, billed and collected at four different times. Entries are grouped by the part of the volume that explains them, alphabetized within each group, and each is one line and a link to the chapter that owns the idea; a word with two meanings appears once per meaning, qualified. Terms another volume's glossary already defines with the same meaning, such as the [Payments](/books/payments/glossary), [Banking & Lending](/books/banking-and-lending/glossary), [Field Operations](/books/field-operations/glossary) and [Workplace & Collaboration](/books/workplace-and-collaboration/glossary) glossaries, keep their definitions. The volume's own names for the core records (insurer, insurer authority, party, party role, producer, producer license, appointment, product, form, rate table, submission, quote, policy, policy term, policy transaction, policy version, risk, coverage, premium entry, billing account, invoice, installment, payment, commission, claim, exposure, reserve, claim payment, recovery, claim task, reinsurance contract, cession, document, notice, regulatory filing, complaint) are the ones in [The Core Data Model](/books/insurance/insurance-business#the-core-data-model).

## The Business

- **Admitted insurer:** an insurer holding a certificate of authority in a state, filing its rates and forms there and covered by the state's guaranty association ([Excess & Surplus Lines](/books/insurance/excess-and-surplus-lines#when-business-goes-non-admitted)).
- **Agency captive:** a captive insurer owned or controlled by insurance agencies or brokerages that insures only risks placed through them ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#captives-and-protected-cells)).
- **Attorney-in-fact:** the company that runs a reciprocal exchange under powers of attorney its subscribers sign, paid a fee on written premium and bearing none of the underwriting risk ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#reciprocal-exchanges-and-the-attorney-in-fact)).
- **Binding authority:** delegated permission for an agent, MGA or coverholder to bind coverage for an insurer within written guidelines (classes, states, limits, policy period, premium volume) ([MGAs, Programs & Fronting](/books/insurance/mgas-programs-and-fronting#binding-authority-and-program-administrators)).
- **Bordereau:** the periodic report of every policy transaction or claim from the party that did the work (an MGA, coverholder or ceding insurer) to the party that bears the risk, reconciled to policies and cash ([MGAs, Programs & Fronting](/books/insurance/mgas-programs-and-fronting#the-bordereau)).
- **Capacity provider:** a reinsurer, collateralized fund or other capital source that takes the risk a fronting carrier or MGA program cedes ([MGAs, Programs & Fronting](/books/insurance/mgas-programs-and-fronting#capacity-providers-and-collateral)).
- **Captive:** an insurer owned by the businesses whose risks it insures; pure, association, industrial insured, agency and sponsored captives are the main kinds ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#captives-and-protected-cells)).
- **Chain of Security:** Lloyd's three layers of policyholder protection: syndicate assets held in trust, members' funds at Lloyd's and the Central Fund ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#lloyd-s)).
- **Closed block:** the participating policies in force when a mutual life insurer converts to stock, run with allocated assets for their dividends alone ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#mutual-holding-companies-and-demutualization)).
- **Combined ratio:** the loss and LAE ratio plus the expense ratio; below 100% means an underwriting profit before investment income ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#the-ratios-that-run-the-business)).
- **Coverage:** one insuring agreement on a policy version and risk, with its limits, deductibles, premium and forms ([The Insurance Business](/books/insurance/insurance-business#the-core-data-model)).
- **Coverholder:** a firm with delegated authority to bind insurance on behalf of Lloyd's syndicates ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#lloyd-s)).
- **Demutualization:** a mutual insurer's conversion to a stock company, exchanging members' interests for consideration equal to statutory surplus under a plan approved by members and the regulator ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#mutual-holding-companies-and-demutualization)).
- **Earned premium:** the part of written premium for coverage already provided, pro rata by time unless the earning rule says otherwise ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#written-earned-and-unearned)).
- **Excess profits law:** a state statute that caps an insurer's underwriting profit on a line over a review period and returns the excess to policyholders, as Florida does for motor vehicle insurance ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#an-excess-profits-law-worked)).
- **Expense ratio:** underwriting expenses over written premium ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#the-ratios-that-run-the-business)).
- **Extraordinary dividend:** an insurer's dividend to its shareholders that, with the prior twelve months', exceeds a statutory threshold (in Iowa, the greater of 10% of surplus or prior-year net income) and needs regulator non-disapproval ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#stock-companies-and-holding-companies)).
- **Float:** money an insurer holds for others, chiefly unearned premium and loss reserves, invested until it is paid out ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#float-and-investment-income)).
- **Fraternal benefit society:** a society operating under a lodge system that insures its members, tax-exempt under 26 USC 501(c)(8) ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#fraternal-benefit-societies)).
- **Fronting carrier:** a licensed insurer that issues policies for an MGA's program and cedes most or all of the risk to capacity providers for a fee, while staying liable to policyholders ([MGAs, Programs & Fronting](/books/insurance/mgas-programs-and-fronting#fronting-and-hybrid-fronting)).
- **Fronting fee:** what a fronting carrier keeps for lending its licenses, filings and balance sheet, usually a percentage of ceded premium ([MGAs, Programs & Fronting](/books/insurance/mgas-programs-and-fronting#fronting-and-hybrid-fronting)).
- **Hybrid fronting:** fronting in which the issuing carrier keeps a meaningful share of the risk rather than ceding nearly all of it ([MGAs, Programs & Fronting](/books/insurance/mgas-programs-and-fronting#fronting-and-hybrid-fronting)).
- **Insurer authority:** an insurer's certificate of authority or surplus lines eligibility in one state, for named lines, effective-dated ([The Insurance Business](/books/insurance/insurance-business#the-core-data-model)).
- **Intercompany pooling:** an arrangement in which insurers in a group cede their business to a lead company and take back fixed shares of the pool ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#stock-companies-and-holding-companies)).
- **Loss ratio:** losses incurred over earned premium ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#the-ratios-that-run-the-business)).
- **Managing agent (Lloyd's):** a company that runs one or more Lloyd's syndicates for their members and employs the underwriters ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#lloyd-s)).
- **Managing general agent (MGA):** under the NAIC model act, an agent that manages an insurer's business and underwrites premium of at least 5% of the insurer's surplus while settling claims over $10,000 or negotiating reinsurance; in industry use, any agency with delegated underwriting authority ([MGAs, Programs & Fronting](/books/insurance/mgas-programs-and-fronting#what-the-law-calls-an-mga)).
- **Market share snapshot:** a sourced, immutable figure for an entity's premium and share by statement line, geography and data year ([The Insurance Landscape](/books/insurance/insurance-landscape#how-to-build-it)).
- **Mutual holding company:** a holding company owned by a former mutual's policyholders that must hold a majority of the voting shares of the stock insurer it became ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#mutual-holding-companies-and-demutualization)).
- **Mutual insurer:** an insurer owned by its policyholders as members, with no shareholders, which may return surplus as policyholder dividends ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#mutuals-and-what-members-are-owed)).
- **NAIC group code:** the NAIC's identifier for a group of affiliated insurers, under which market share is reported ([The Insurance Landscape](/books/insurance/insurance-landscape#how-the-platforms-do-it)).
- **Named insured:** the party who owns a P&C policy and to whom notices are sent ([The Insurance Business](/books/insurance/insurance-business#who-s-who)).
- **Net writings leverage:** net premiums written over policyholders' surplus, NAIC's name for the industry's premium-to-surplus ratio ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#surplus-and-leverage)).
- **Policy transaction:** a change to a policy (new business, endorsement, cancellation, reinstatement, renewal, nonrenewal, audit, rewrite) with its effective date, processed time and written premium change; the only way a policy changes ([The Insurance Business](/books/insurance/insurance-business#the-core-data-model)).
- **Policy version:** the policy as of an effective date range, produced by a policy transaction, with effective and recorded time ([Endorsements, Renewals & the Policy Transaction Model](/books/insurance/endorsements-and-renewals#bitemporal-policy-versions)).
- **Policyholder dividend:** surplus a mutual or participating insurer returns to policyholders, declared each year and never guaranteed ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#mutuals-and-what-members-are-owed)).
- **Policyholders' surplus:** an insurer's statutory capital, assets less liabilities on the statutory balance sheet ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#surplus-and-leverage)).
- **Premium entry:** a written premium, tax, fee or surcharge amount from one policy transaction, with its earning window, from which earned and unearned premium are derived ([The Insurance Business](/books/insurance/insurance-business#the-core-data-model)).
- **Premium-to-surplus ratio:** net premiums written over policyholders' surplus, a measure of underwriting leverage ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#surplus-and-leverage)).
- **Pro rata:** returning or charging premium in proportion to the time remaining or covered ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#written-earned-and-unearned)).
- **Program administrator:** an agency that underwrites and administers a defined program (a class of business, states and product) on an insurer's paper ([MGAs, Programs & Fronting](/books/insurance/mgas-programs-and-fronting#binding-authority-and-program-administrators)).
- **Protected cell:** a pool of an insurer's assets and liabilities segregated by statute from the rest of the company, so the cell's assets pay only the cell's liabilities ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#captives-and-protected-cells)).
- **Reciprocal exchange:** an unincorporated association whose subscribers exchange insurance with one another through an attorney-in-fact ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#reciprocal-exchanges-and-the-attorney-in-fact)).
- **Reserve leverage:** loss and LAE reserves over policyholders' surplus ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#surplus-and-leverage)).
- **Risk:** a thing or exposure insured, typed by line (vehicle, driver, dwelling, location, class code exposure, insured life, covered product) ([The Insurance Business](/books/insurance/insurance-business#the-core-data-model)).
- **Risk retention group (RRG):** a liability insurer owned by its insureds, chartered in one state under the Liability Risk Retention Act and able to write members in every state without guaranty fund protection ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#risk-retention-groups)).
- **Statement line:** the annual statement's line of business code (04 homeowners, 16 workers' compensation, 17.1 other liability and so on) that industry figures are reported by ([The Insurance Landscape](/books/insurance/insurance-landscape#by-line)).
- **Stock insurer:** an insurer owned by shareholders, usually through a holding company ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#stock-companies-and-holding-companies)).
- **Surplus lines insurer:** a non-admitted insurer eligible to write risks placed through a licensed surplus lines broker, on its own rates and forms, without guaranty fund coverage ([Excess & Surplus Lines](/books/insurance/excess-and-surplus-lines#when-business-goes-non-admitted)).
- **Syndicate:** one or more Lloyd's members providing capital and accepting risks for a year of account, run by a managing agent ([Who Carries the Risk](/books/insurance/who-carries-the-risk#lloyd-s)).
- **Underlying combined ratio:** a combined ratio that excludes catastrophe losses and prior-year reserve development, each on the reporting company's own definition ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#the-ratios-that-run-the-business)).
- **Underwriting cycle:** the alternation of hard markets (rising prices, tighter capacity) and soft markets (competition, falling prices) in underwriting results ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#the-underwriting-cycle)).
- **Unearned premium reserve:** the liability for written premium not yet earned ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#written-earned-and-unearned)).
- **Written premium:** the premium recorded when a policy transaction is processed, for the coverage it adds or removes ([How Insurers Make Money](/books/insurance/how-insurers-make-money#written-earned-and-unearned)).

## Regulation and Solvency

- **Accreditation:** the NAIC program under which a state's financial regulation of its domestic insurers meets standards (laws, practices, staffing, licensing) that other states rely on instead of re-examining ([The State System](/books/insurance/the-state-system#accreditation)).
- **Adverse Consumer Outcome:** in the NAIC AI bulletin, a decision subject to insurance regulatory standards that adversely affects a consumer ([Data, AI & Privacy Rules](/books/insurance/data-ai-and-privacy-rules#the-naic-ai-model-bulletin)).
- **Adverse impact ratio:** a group's favorable-outcome rate divided by the highest group's rate, one of the metrics New York's Circular Letter No. 7 names ([Data, AI & Privacy Rules](/books/insurance/data-ai-and-privacy-rules#worked-numbers-an-adverse-impact-ratio)).
- **AIS Program:** the written program for the responsible use of AI systems in regulated insurance decisions that the NAIC AI Model Bulletin expects every insurer to maintain ([Data, AI & Privacy Rules](/books/insurance/data-ai-and-privacy-rules#the-naic-ai-model-bulletin)).
- **Annual statement:** the NAIC-format statutory financial statement each insurer files for each legal entity, due March 1 ([Statutory Accounting & the Annual Statement](/books/insurance/statutory-accounting#the-annual-statement)).
- **Annual statement line:** the NAIC line-of-business classification (homeowners line 4, workers' comp line 16) every statutory figure is reported by ([Statutory Accounting & the Annual Statement](/books/insurance/statutory-accounting#the-annual-statement)).
- **Authorized Control Level RBC (ACL):** the capital amount the RBC formula produces, against which every RBC action level is a multiple ([Solvency & Capital](/books/insurance/solvency-and-capital#the-formula)).
- **Company Action Level:** total adjusted capital from 150% to under 200% of ACL, or 200% to 300% with a trend test trigger; the insurer must file an RBC plan within 45 days ([Solvency & Capital](/books/insurance/solvency-and-capital#the-action-levels)).
- **Complaint register:** the record of all complaints since the last exam, by line, nature, disposition and time to process, that the Unfair Trade Practices Act requires ([Market Conduct & Unfair Practices](/books/insurance/market-conduct#complaints-and-their-clocks)).
- **Covered agreement:** a federal agreement with a foreign government on prudential insurance measures, under which FIO may preempt inconsistent state measures ([The State System](/books/insurance/the-state-system#covered-agreements)).
- **Covered claim:** a claim under a liquidated insurer's policy that a guaranty association pays, up to the state's cap ([When Insurers Fail](/books/insurance/when-insurers-fail#the-p-c-guaranty-system)).
- **Deemer:** a statutory period after which a filing is deemed approved if the regulator hasn't acted (Florida's 90 days, California's 60 and 180) ([Filing Rates & Forms](/books/insurance/rate-and-form-filing#the-regimes)).
- **Depopulation (takeout):** moving policies from a residual market back to private insurers through approved offers ([Stressed & Residual Markets](/books/insurance/stressed-markets#citizens-eligibility-rates-and-depopulation)).
- **Domiciliary state:** the state where an insurer is incorporated, which alone regulates its solvency, holding company transactions and receivership ([The State System](/books/insurance/the-state-system#the-commissioner-and-the-department)).
- **FAIR plan:** a state property insurance placement facility funded by member insurers, for property owners the voluntary market won't insure ([Stressed & Residual Markets](/books/insurance/stressed-markets#property-plans-elsewhere)).
- **Federal Insurance Office (FIO):** the Treasury office that monitors insurance, runs the terrorism program and can preempt state measures only under covered agreements ([The State System](/books/insurance/the-state-system#the-federal-insurance-office)).
- **File-and-use:** a rate regime in which a filed rate may be used after a waiting period or on filing, subject to later disapproval ([Filing Rates & Forms](/books/insurance/rate-and-form-filing#the-regimes)).
- **Flex rating:** a regime in which rate changes within a band take effect without prior approval and larger ones need it ([Filing Rates & Forms](/books/insurance/rate-and-form-filing#new-york-flex-rating)).
- **Guaranty association:** a state association that pays covered claims of liquidated insurers up to statutory caps, funded by assessments on solvent insurers ([When Insurers Fail](/books/insurance/when-insurers-fail#the-p-c-guaranty-system)).
- **Insurance Data Security Model Law (#668):** the NAIC model requiring an information security program, third-party oversight, 72-hour notice of cybersecurity events and an annual certification ([Data, AI & Privacy Rules](/books/insurance/data-ai-and-privacy-rules#data-security-model-668-and-part-500)).
- **IRIS ratios:** the NAIC's 13 P&C and 12 life financial ratios computed from annual statements, with published industry means and medians ([Solvency & Capital](/books/insurance/solvency-and-capital#iris-ratios)).
- **Liquidation:** the receivership stage in which the liquidator takes the insurer's assets, policies cancel after 30 days and guaranty associations pay covered claims ([When Insurers Fail](/books/insurance/when-insurers-fail#the-stages)).
- **Market conduct annual statement (MCAS):** the NAIC's annual collection of claims and underwriting data in 13 lines for market analysis ([Market Conduct & Unfair Practices](/books/insurance/market-conduct#the-market-conduct-annual-statement)).
- **Market conduct examination:** a regulator's review of an insurer's treatment of consumers, from data requests and samples to a public report ([Market Conduct & Unfair Practices](/books/insurance/market-conduct#how-market-regulation-works)).
- **McCarran-Ferguson Act:** the 1945 federal law leaving insurance regulation and taxation to the states unless a federal law specifically relates to insurance ([The State System](/books/insurance/the-state-system#where-the-authority-comes-from)).
- **Model law:** a draft statute or regulation the NAIC adopts for states to enact; it binds no one until a state does ([The State System](/books/insurance/the-state-system#how-a-model-becomes-law)).
- **NAIC:** the National Association of Insurance Commissioners, the standard-setting body of the 56 jurisdictions' chief insurance regulators ([The State System](/books/insurance/the-state-system#the-naic)).
- **Nonadmitted asset:** an asset statutory accounting excludes from the balance sheet because it can't pay claims, reducing surplus ([Statutory Accounting & the Annual Statement](/books/insurance/statutory-accounting#admitted-and-nonadmitted-assets)).
- **ORSA:** the Own Risk and Solvency Assessment, a confidential internal assessment of material risks and capital filed annually by insurers above $500 million (groups above $1 billion) ([Solvency & Capital](/books/insurance/solvency-and-capital#orsa)).
- **Permitted practice:** a departure from statutory accounting a domiciliary regulator approves for one insurer ([Statutory Accounting & the Annual Statement](/books/insurance/statutory-accounting#what-sap-is)).
- **Prior approval:** a rate regime in which a rate may not be used until approved or deemed approved ([Filing Rates & Forms](/books/insurance/rate-and-form-filing#the-regimes)).
- **Priority of distribution:** the statutory order in which a liquidated insurer's estate pays claims, policyholders ahead of general creditors ([When Insurers Fail](/books/insurance/when-insurers-fail#the-priority-of-distribution)).
- **Proposition 103:** California's 1988 initiative imposing prior approval of property-casualty rates, a rollback and compensated intervenors ([Filing Rates & Forms](/books/insurance/rate-and-form-filing#california-proposition-103)), and requiring auto rates to follow three mandatory factors in decreasing order of importance ([What You May Rate On](/books/insurance/rating-factors-and-limits#california-s-proposition-103)).
- **RBC ratio:** total adjusted capital divided by Authorized Control Level RBC; regulatory action begins below 200% ([Solvency & Capital](/books/insurance/solvency-and-capital#risk-based-capital)).
- **Receivership:** state court proceedings (conservation, rehabilitation, liquidation) in which the commissioner takes over a troubled insurer ([When Insurers Fail](/books/insurance/when-insurers-fail#the-stages)).
- **Rehabilitation:** the receivership stage in which the receiver runs the insurer to restore or restructure it, with a plan due within a year ([When Insurers Fail](/books/insurance/when-insurers-fail#the-stages)).
- **Residual market:** a mechanism (state insurer, FAIR plan, assigned risk plan, JUA, reinsurance facility) for risks the voluntary market won't take ([Stressed & Residual Markets](/books/insurance/stressed-markets#the-residual-market-as-a-system)).
- **SERFF:** the NAIC's System for Electronic Rates and Forms Filing, through which rate, rule and form filings reach the states ([Filing Rates & Forms](/books/insurance/rate-and-form-filing#serff-and-the-parts-of-a-filing)).
- **Statutory accounting principles (SAP):** the conservative, solvency-focused accounting basis of the NAIC's Accounting Practices and Procedures Manual that insurers file on ([Statutory Accounting & the Annual Statement](/books/insurance/statutory-accounting#what-sap-is)).
- **Total adjusted capital (TAC):** the capital measure compared against RBC levels, based on statutory surplus with prescribed adjustments ([Solvency & Capital](/books/insurance/solvency-and-capital#the-formula)).
- **Trend test:** the RBC test that treats a company at 200% to 300% as at the Company Action Level when its combined ratio exceeds 120% (P&C) or its margin trends below 1.9 times ACL (life) ([Solvency & Capital](/books/insurance/solvency-and-capital#the-trend-tests)).
- **Unfair Trade Practices Act:** the NAIC model (#880) and state laws defining misrepresentation, unfair discrimination, rebating and record failures as unfair practices when flagrant or a general business practice ([Market Conduct & Unfair Practices](/books/insurance/market-conduct#the-unfair-trade-practices-act)).
- **Uniform Data Standard (UDS):** the NCIGF file format in which receivers and guaranty associations exchange open claims (A records), unearned premium (B records) and payments ([When Insurers Fail](/books/insurance/when-insurers-fail#the-data-handoff-uds)).
- **Use-and-file:** a rate regime in which a rate may be used first and filed within a set period, subject to refund if unjustified ([Filing Rates & Forms](/books/insurance/rate-and-form-filing#the-regimes)).

## Distribution

- **1033 waiver:** a regulator's written consent under 18 USC 1033(e) letting a person convicted of a felony involving dishonesty or breach of trust work in the business of insurance ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#who-must-be-licensed)).
- **Account current:** an agency's periodic statement to a carrier of every agency-bill transaction and the net premium due after commission ([Commissions & Agency Accounting](/books/insurance/commissions-and-agency-accounting#agency-bill-and-direct-bill)).
- **Agency bill:** billing in which the agency invoices and collects from the insured and remits premium net of its commission to the carrier ([Commissions & Agency Accounting](/books/insurance/commissions-and-agency-accounting#agency-bill-and-direct-bill)).
- **Agency management system (AMS):** an agency's system of record for clients, policies across carriers, activities, accounting and commissions, kept current by carrier downloads ([Comparative Raters & Agency Systems](/books/insurance/comparative-raters-and-agency-systems#agency-management-systems)).
- **Appointment:** an insurer's filing with a state naming a producer as its agent, required in most states before the producer acts for that insurer ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#appointments)).
- **Book roll:** moving an agency's block of policies from one carrier to another at renewal ([Comparative Raters & Agency Systems](/books/insurance/comparative-raters-and-agency-systems#downloads)).
- **Bridging:** handing a comparative rater's quote, with its data and quote reference, into the chosen carrier's portal to bind and issue ([Comparative Raters & Agency Systems](/books/insurance/comparative-raters-and-agency-systems#how-comparative-rating-works)).
- **Broker:** a producer who represents the buyer in placing insurance, rather than an insurer ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#who-the-agent-works-for)).
- **Broker of record letter:** an insured's letter moving its account to a new broker or agent, effective on a date ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#who-the-agent-works-for)).
- **Captive agent:** an agent who sells only one insurer's products, often as an independent contractor running a branded office ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#the-channels)).
- **Chargeback (commission):** commission returned to the carrier when premium is returned on a cancellation or reduction, at the original transaction's rate ([Commissions & Agency Accounting](/books/insurance/commissions-and-agency-accounting#clawbacks-and-chargebacks)).
- **Commission schedule:** the versioned rates in an agency contract by insurer, product, state and new or renewal business ([Commissions & Agency Accounting](/books/insurance/commissions-and-agency-accounting#commission-schedules)).
- **Comparative rater:** software that takes a risk's details once and returns quotes from many carriers side by side ([Comparative Raters & Agency Systems](/books/insurance/comparative-raters-and-agency-systems#how-comparative-rating-works)).
- **Compensation disclosure:** a producer's statement to a buyer of how it is paid, required of producers paid by the customer and, in New York, of all producers ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#who-the-agent-works-for)).
- **Consent evidence:** the record proving a consumer's consent to calls or texts: the language shown, sellers named, page, time, action and number ([Leads, Aggregators & Consent](/books/insurance/leads-and-consent#consent-evidence)).
- **Contingent commission:** a commission from a carrier that depends on the claims experience (and often the growth or volume) of the producer's book, computed after a measurement period ([Commissions & Agency Accounting](/books/insurance/commissions-and-agency-accounting#contingent-and-profit-sharing-commissions)).
- **Designated responsible licensed producer (DRLP):** the licensed individual an agency names as responsible for its compliance in a state, required for its business entity license ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#business-entity-licenses-and-the-drlp)).
- **Direct bill:** billing in which the carrier bills and collects from the insured and pays the agency its commission by statement ([Commissions & Agency Accounting](/books/insurance/commissions-and-agency-accounting#agency-bill-and-direct-bill)).
- **Direct Bill Commission Statement (DBCS):** a carrier's commission statement delivered to an agency's system by download for reconciliation ([Commissions & Agency Accounting](/books/insurance/commissions-and-agency-accounting#commission-statements-and-downloads)).
- **Direct channel:** a carrier selling through its own website, app and call center with licensed employees ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#the-channels)).
- **Download (agency):** carrier-to-agency delivery of policy, claim, commission and document data into an agency management system ([Comparative Raters & Agency Systems](/books/insurance/comparative-raters-and-agency-systems#downloads)).
- **Exempt commercial purchaser:** a large commercial buyer with a risk manager that, after disclosure and a written request, may buy surplus lines coverage without a diligent search (15 USC 8205, 8206) ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#surplus-lines-brokers-and-the-home-state-rule)).
- **Home state (surplus lines):** the insured's principal place of business or residence, or where most taxable premium is, the only state that may tax a nonadmitted placement ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#surplus-lines-brokers-and-the-home-state-rule)).
- **Independent agent:** a licensed agency appointed by several carriers that quotes and places business with any of them ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#the-channels)).
- **Just-in-time appointment:** an appointment filed when a producer's first business with the insurer arrives, rather than when the contract is signed ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#appointments)).
- **Lead (insurance):** a consumer's quote request and contact details, with a consent record, sold to carriers or agents who will call or text ([Leads, Aggregators & Consent](/books/insurance/leads-and-consent#lead-markets)).
- **Line of authority:** the class of insurance a producer license allows: life, accident and health, property, casualty, variable, personal lines, credit or a limited line ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#lines-of-authority)).
- **National Producer Number (NPN):** the unique NAIC identifier for an individual or business entity producer, the same in every state ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#nipr-the-npn-and-the-pdb)).
- **NIPR:** the National Insurance Producer Registry, the NAIC affiliate that runs the Producer Database and the gateway for licensing, appointments and terminations ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#nipr-the-npn-and-the-pdb)).
- **Nonresident license:** a producer license from a state other than the home state, granted on the strength of the home-state license where states reciprocate ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#resident-and-nonresident-licenses)).
- **One-to-one consent:** the FCC's 2023 rule limiting written consent to a single seller, vacated by the Eleventh Circuit in January 2025 and repealed in July 2025 ([Leads, Aggregators & Consent](/books/insurance/leads-and-consent#the-one-to-one-rule-and-its-vacatur)).
- **Premium trust account:** the account in which a producer holds premium and return premium as a fiduciary, with a balance never below what it owes insurers and insureds ([Commissions & Agency Accounting](/books/insurance/commissions-and-agency-accounting#trust-accounts-and-fiduciary-funds)).
- **Prior express written consent:** a signed written agreement authorizing a seller to deliver telemarketing calls or texts by autodialer or artificial voice to a designated number, required under the TCPA rules ([Leads, Aggregators & Consent](/books/insurance/leads-and-consent#prior-express-written-consent)).
- **Producer:** a licensed agent, agency, broker or MGA that sells insurance, identified by its National Producer Number ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#who-must-be-licensed)).
- **Producer Database (PDB):** NIPR's database linking state licensing systems, with every producer's licenses, lines and appointments, updated daily ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#nipr-the-npn-and-the-pdb)).
- **Producer of record:** the producer under whose license and appointment a policy transaction was written ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#who-the-agent-works-for)).
- **Profit-sharing commission:** a contingent commission computed as a share of the margin between a target loss ratio and the producer's actual loss ratio ([Commissions & Agency Accounting](/books/insurance/commissions-and-agency-accounting#contingent-and-profit-sharing-commissions)).
- **Surplus lines broker:** a licensed broker who places risks with nonadmitted insurers, files each placement and collects and remits the surplus lines tax ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#surplus-lines-brokers-and-the-home-state-rule)).
- **Termination (appointment):** an insurer ending a producer's appointment, reported to the state within 30 days with whether it was for cause ([Producer Licensing & Appointments](/books/insurance/producer-licensing#terminations)).
- **Wholesaler:** a broker that places business for retail agents with markets they can't reach, usually surplus lines or specialty programs ([Agents, Brokers & Channels](/books/insurance/agents-brokers-and-channels#the-channels)).

## Rating and Underwriting

- **Adverse action (insurance):** under the FCRA, a denial or cancellation of, an increase in any charge for, or an unfavorable change in insurance in connection with underwriting; an initial rate can be one ([Consumer Reports & Third-Party Data](/books/insurance/consumer-reports-and-third-party-data#the-fcra-for-insurers)).
- **Base rate:** the price per coverage and territory of the reference risk, before relativities ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#what-a-rate-manual-contains)).
- **C.L.U.E.:** LexisNexis's contributory auto and property claims history exchange, a consumer report ([Consumer Reports & Third-Party Data](/books/insurance/consumer-reports-and-third-party-data#loss-history-exchanges)).
- **Credit-based insurance score:** a score built from credit report data to predict insurance losses rather than repayment, banned or restricted in some states and lines ([What You May Rate On](/books/insurance/rating-factors-and-limits#credit-based-insurance-scores)).
- **Deductible factor:** the relativity applied to a base rate for a chosen deductible ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#what-a-rate-manual-contains)).
- **Extraordinary life circumstances:** events (catastrophe, serious illness, death in the family, divorce, identity theft, job loss, deployment) for which the NCOIL credit model requires reasonable exceptions to credit-based rating ([Consumer Reports & Third-Party Data](/books/insurance/consumer-reports-and-third-party-data#disputes-re-rating-and-exceptions)).
- **Increased limits factor:** the relativity applied to a base rate for limits above the base limit ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#the-rating-algorithm-and-its-order)).
- **Inspection report (underwriting):** a property inspection used to accept or cancel a risk within the state's underwriting period ([Underwriting Rules, Referrals & Models](/books/insurance/underwriting#inspections-and-underwriting-periods)).
- **Loss cost multiplier (LCM):** the factor an insurer files to turn an advisory organization's loss costs into its rates, built from its expense and profit provisions and, where allowed, its own loss experience ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#loss-costs-and-loss-cost-multipliers)).
- **Loss cost (prospective loss cost):** an advisory organization's filed expected loss and LAE per exposure unit, without expenses or profit ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#loss-costs-and-loss-cost-multipliers)).
- **Motor vehicle record (MVR):** a state's record of a driver's license status, violations and convictions, released to insurers under the DPPA ([Consumer Reports & Third-Party Data](/books/insurance/consumer-reports-and-third-party-data#motor-vehicle-records)).
- **Neutral score rate:** the rate an applicant would have received without the credit score, the baseline for adverse action after Safeco v. Burr ([Consumer Reports & Third-Party Data](/books/insurance/consumer-reports-and-third-party-data#adverse-action-after-safeco-v-burr)).
- **Pay-per-mile:** auto insurance priced as a base premium plus a filed rate per measured mile ([Telematics & Usage-Based Insurance](/books/insurance/telematics#pay-per-mile)).
- **Permissible loss ratio:** 100% minus the expense and profit provisions, whose reciprocal is the expense multiplier ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#loss-costs-and-loss-cost-multipliers)).
- **Prefill:** populating a quote or application with drivers, vehicles or property attributes from a data vendor ([Consumer Reports & Third-Party Data](/books/insurance/consumer-reports-and-third-party-data#prefill-and-property-data)).
- **Price optimization:** varying rates on factors other than risk of loss, such as willingness to pay, barred by bulletin in thirteen jurisdictions ([What You May Rate On](/books/insurance/rating-factors-and-limits#price-optimization)).
- **Rating algorithm:** the filed order of lookups, multiplications, additions, minimums and rounding that turns rate tables into a premium ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#the-rating-algorithm-and-its-order)).
- **Rating worksheet:** every input, table version, factor and intermediate result of a rating, stored with the quote or transaction ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#how-to-build-it)).
- **Referral:** an underwriting issue that blocks a quote, bind or issue until someone with enough authority approves it ([Underwriting Rules, Referrals & Models](/books/insurance/underwriting#authority-and-referrals)).
- **Relativity:** a multiplicative rating factor for a characteristic that differs from the reference risk ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#what-a-rate-manual-contains)).
- **Safe Driver Insurance Plan (SDIP):** North Carolina's driving record surcharge plan, assigning points to convictions and at-fault accidents with a percentage rate increase per point level ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#driving-record-surcharges)).
- **Telematics:** measuring driving behavior or mileage from a phone, plug-in device or connected car for rating ([Telematics & Usage-Based Insurance](/books/insurance/telematics#from-sensor-to-rating-factor)).
- **Territory:** a geographic rating cell to which a garaging or property location is assigned by the filed rule ([How a Rate Is Built](/books/insurance/how-a-rate-is-built#territories)).
- **Underwriting guidelines:** an insurer's written rules for which risks it accepts and on what terms, filed with the regulator in some states ([Underwriting Rules, Referrals & Models](/books/insurance/underwriting#eligibility-and-underwriting-guidelines)).
- **Underwriting period:** the first days of a policy (often 60) in which state law lets an insurer cancel more freely, for instance after an inspection ([Underwriting Rules, Referrals & Models](/books/insurance/underwriting#inspections-and-underwriting-periods)).
- **Unfair discrimination:** rating or underwriting distinctions not justified by expected loss or barred by law; with excessive and inadequate, one of the three rate standards ([What You May Rate On](/books/insurance/rating-factors-and-limits#unfair-discrimination-testing)).
- **Usage-based insurance (UBI):** auto insurance priced in part on measured driving or mileage ([Telematics & Usage-Based Insurance](/books/insurance/telematics#how-the-platforms-do-it)).

## Policy Administration

- **Aggregate transaction:** a single transaction that reverses later transactions, applies an out-of-sequence change and reapplies the reversed ones ([Endorsements, Renewals & the Policy Transaction Model](/books/insurance/endorsements-and-renewals#out-of-sequence-endorsements)).
- **Amendatory endorsement:** a mandatory endorsement that rewrites parts of a policy form to comply with one state's law ([Products, Coverages & Forms](/books/insurance/products-and-coverages#state-amendatory-endorsements)).
- **Binder:** a temporary written contract evidencing coverage pending issuance of the policy, limited in some states (90 days in California) ([Quote, Bind & Issue](/books/insurance/quote-bind-and-issue#binders)).
- **Binding:** the moment coverage exists, when the insurer or an authorized agent accepts a risk from a stated time ([Quote, Bind & Issue](/books/insurance/quote-bind-and-issue#the-quote-lifecycle)).
- **Conditional renewal:** an offer to renew on changed terms (reduced limits, higher deductibles, in New York commercial lines a premium increase over 10%), noticed like a nonrenewal ([Endorsements, Renewals & the Policy Transaction Model](/books/insurance/endorsements-and-renewals#conditional-renewal)).
- **Coverage term:** a parameter of a coverage (a limit, a deductible, an option) with its allowed values ([Products, Coverages & Forms](/books/insurance/products-and-coverages#the-product-model)).
- **Declarations page:** the policy-specific schedule of insured, period, risks, coverages, limits, deductibles, premium, interests and forms ([Products, Coverages & Forms](/books/insurance/products-and-coverages#declarations)).
- **Edition date:** the month and year that, with the form number, identifies a version of a policy form (NC 00 12 10 26) ([Products, Coverages & Forms](/books/insurance/products-and-coverages#forms)).
- **Endorsement:** a midterm change to a policy, or the form that adds, removes or changes coverage ([Endorsements, Renewals & the Policy Transaction Model](/books/insurance/endorsements-and-renewals#midterm-changes)).
- **Form attachment rule:** a rule that infers which form editions a policy carries from its state, product version, coverages and risks ([Products, Coverages & Forms](/books/insurance/products-and-coverages#forms)).
- **Lapse:** the end of coverage because a renewal wasn't paid, as distinct from a cancellation ([Cancellation, Nonrenewal & Notice Law](/books/insurance/cancellation-and-nonrenewal#reinstatement-and-lapse)).
- **Mortgagee bill:** billing a homeowners premium to the mortgage servicer, which pays from escrow ([Billing & Premium Finance](/books/insurance/billing-and-premium-finance#escrowed-premiums)).
- **Nonrenewal:** an insurer's decision not to renew a policy at expiration, subject to notice periods and prohibited bases ([Cancellation, Nonrenewal & Notice Law](/books/insurance/cancellation-and-nonrenewal#notice-periods-by-state-and-reason)).
- **Out-of-sequence endorsement:** a change whose effective date is earlier than that of a transaction already processed ([Endorsements, Renewals & the Policy Transaction Model](/books/insurance/endorsements-and-renewals#out-of-sequence-endorsements)).
- **Pay plan:** the schedule on which a policy's premium is billed (paid in full, two-pay, quarterly, monthly), with its down payment and fees ([Billing & Premium Finance](/books/insurance/billing-and-premium-finance#billing-accounts-and-plans)).
- **Preemption:** when one of two concurrent jobs from the same base version binds first and the other must be rebased and requoted ([Endorsements, Renewals & the Policy Transaction Model](/books/insurance/endorsements-and-renewals#preemption)).
- **Premium finance company:** a state-licensed lender that pays a premium to the insurer and collects installments from the insured, holding a power of attorney to cancel on default ([Billing & Premium Finance](/books/insurance/billing-and-premium-finance#premium-finance)).
- **Proof of mailing:** evidence that a notice was mailed to the required address on a date, which the law treats as proof of notice ([Cancellation, Nonrenewal & Notice Law](/books/insurance/cancellation-and-nonrenewal#proof-of-mailing-and-delivery)).
- **Quote:** a priced, time-limited offer to insure on the facts given, not a contract ([Quote, Bind & Issue](/books/insurance/quote-bind-and-issue#the-quote-lifecycle)).
- **Reinstatement (policy):** a transaction that puts a cancelled policy back in force, with or without a gap in coverage ([Cancellation, Nonrenewal & Notice Law](/books/insurance/cancellation-and-nonrenewal#reinstatement-and-lapse)).
- **Renewal offer:** the notice of a renewal's terms and premium, due a set number of days before expiration ([Endorsements, Renewals & the Policy Transaction Model](/books/insurance/endorsements-and-renewals#renewals)).
- **Rewrite:** replacing a policy or the rest of a term with a new term or policy instead of endorsing it ([Endorsements, Renewals & the Policy Transaction Model](/books/insurance/endorsements-and-renewals#rewrites)).
- **Short rate:** a return premium method that keeps more than pro rata when the insured or a finance company cancels, limited in some states (in Florida, at least 90% of pro rata) ([Cancellation, Nonrenewal & Notice Law](/books/insurance/cancellation-and-nonrenewal#return-premium-pro-rata-and-short-rate)).

## Claims

- **Advance charges:** towing, storage and impound fees a salvage auction pays to release a total loss vehicle and recovers from the sale ([Subrogation, Salvage & Recovery](/books/insurance/subrogation-and-salvage#salvage)).
- **Anti-subrogation rule:** an insurer may not subrogate against a person insured under the same policy ([Subrogation, Salvage & Recovery](/books/insurance/subrogation-and-salvage#subrogation-rights)).
- **A&O (adjusting and other):** loss adjustment expense not tied to a specific claim's defense, chiefly the cost of the claims operation ([Exposures, Reserves & Payments](/books/insurance/reserves-and-payments#case-reserves-by-cost-type)).
- **Appraisal clause:** the policy provision under which each side names an appraiser, the appraisers pick an umpire, and a written award by any two fixes the amount of loss ([Property Claims](/books/insurance/property-claims#appraisal)).
- **Assignment of benefits (AOB):** a policyholder signing insurance proceeds over to a contractor; Florida voided it for policies issued on or after January 1, 2023 ([Property Claims](/books/insurance/property-claims#assignment-of-benefits-and-florida-s-ban)).
- **Case reserve (case outstanding):** the adjuster's estimate of what remains to be paid on a known claim, by exposure and cost type ([Exposures, Reserves & Payments](/books/insurance/reserves-and-payments#case-reserves-by-cost-type)).
- **Civil remedy notice:** Florida's 60-day presuit notice of an insurer's alleged bad faith, which the insurer can cure ([Bad Faith & Litigation](/books/insurance/bad-faith-and-litigation#statutory-remedies)).
- **Claim diary:** the claim's tasks with due dates, generated from cited claim clock rules and the events that trigger them ([Claim Handling Law & Clocks](/books/insurance/claim-handling-law#the-diary-as-a-rules-engine)).
- **Comparable vehicle:** a vehicle of the same make, same or newer year, similar body, options and mileage, available locally within 90 days, used to value a total loss ([Auto Claims & Total Loss](/books/insurance/auto-claims-and-total-loss#valuation-rules)).
- **Coverage position:** the insurer's recorded stance on a coverage issue for an exposure (covered, reserved, partially denied, denied), with its deadline and the letter that carried it ([First Notice, Coverage & Triage](/books/insurance/first-notice-and-triage#coverage-positions-reservations-of-rights-and-denials)).
- **Credible referral:** in California, an SIU referral with at least one red flag, which the SIU must investigate ([Fraud & SIU](/books/insurance/insurance-fraud#detection)).
- **DCC (defense and cost containment):** loss adjustment expense tied to specific claims, such as defense counsel and experts ([Exposures, Reserves & Payments](/books/insurance/reserves-and-payments#case-reserves-by-cost-type)).
- **Deductible recovery:** the insured's share of a subrogation recovery, shared proportionately under the model regulation ([Subrogation, Salvage & Recovery](/books/insurance/subrogation-and-salvage#deductible-recovery)).
- **Direct repair program (DRP):** a network of repair shops that accept an insurer's rates and guidelines in exchange for referrals; the claimant still chooses the shop ([Auto Claims & Total Loss](/books/insurance/auto-claims-and-total-loss#estimating-and-repair)).
- **Excess exposure:** the risk that a liability verdict exceeds the policy limits, which makes a demand within limits a bad faith decision point ([Bad Faith & Litigation](/books/insurance/bad-faith-and-litigation#first-party-and-third-party-bad-faith)).
- **Exposure (claims):** one claimant against one coverage on a claim, the unit reserves and payments attach to ([Exposures, Reserves & Payments](/books/insurance/reserves-and-payments#exposures)).
- **First notice of loss (FNOL):** the first report of a claim to the insurer or its agent, which starts the claim clocks ([First Notice, Coverage & Triage](/books/insurance/first-notice-and-triage#first-notice-channels-and-data)).
- **First-party bad faith:** an insurer's unreasonable denial or delay of its own insured's claim, a tort in California and Texas ([Bad Faith & Litigation](/books/insurance/bad-faith-and-litigation#first-party-and-third-party-bad-faith)).
- **General business practice:** the frequency standard under which repeated claim handling failures become an unfair claims practice ([Claim Handling Law & Clocks](/books/insurance/claim-handling-law#the-unfair-claims-settlement-practices-act)).
- **Incident:** what was damaged or hurt on a claim (a vehicle, a property, an injury), to which exposures attach ([First Notice, Coverage & Triage](/books/insurance/first-notice-and-triage#the-claim-file-opened)).
- **Independent adjuster (IA):** a licensed adjuster from an outside firm retained by an insurer, paid as adjusting expense ([First Notice, Coverage & Triage](/books/insurance/first-notice-and-triage#segmentation-and-assignment)).
- **Inter-company arbitration:** resolution of subrogation disputes between insurers through Arbitration Forums programs instead of court ([Subrogation, Salvage & Recovery](/books/insurance/subrogation-and-salvage#demands-and-inter-company-arbitration)).
- **LAE (loss adjustment expense):** the cost of investigating, defending and settling claims, split into DCC and A&O ([Exposures, Reserves & Payments](/books/insurance/reserves-and-payments#case-reserves-by-cost-type)).
- **LEDES:** the Legal Electronic Data Exchange Standard formats for invoices, budgets and timekeeper data, maintained by the LEDES Oversight Committee; insurers take them from defense counsel ([Bad Faith & Litigation](/books/insurance/bad-faith-and-litigation#litigation-management)).
- **Matching:** the rule extending a repair to undamaged items so replacements conform to a reasonably uniform appearance ([Property Claims](/books/insurance/property-claims#matching)).
- **Model 900 (Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act):** NAIC's 1990 model listing fourteen unfair claims practices, enforced against flagrant violations or a general business practice ([Claim Handling Law & Clocks](/books/insurance/claim-handling-law#the-unfair-claims-settlement-practices-act)).
- **Model 902:** NAIC's property and casualty claims model regulation setting 15, 21, 30 and 45-day clocks, total loss valuation and deductible sharing ([Claim Handling Law & Clocks](/books/insurance/claim-handling-law#the-model-regulation-s-clocks)).
- **Proof of loss:** the insured's documentation of a claim, often sworn, from which many decision clocks run ([Claim Handling Law & Clocks](/books/insurance/claim-handling-law#the-rules-at-a-glance)).
- **Public adjuster:** a licensed adjuster hired by the policyholder for a fee, capped at 10% in Texas and after declared emergencies in Florida ([Property Claims](/books/insurance/property-claims#public-adjusters)).
- **Recovery allocation:** the split of a recovery among insurer, insured, reinsurers and fees, summing to the amount received ([Subrogation, Salvage & Recovery](/books/insurance/subrogation-and-salvage#recovery-accounting)).
- **Red flag:** a written, line-specific indicator of possible fraud that supports an SIU referral ([Fraud & SIU](/books/insurance/insurance-fraud#detection)).
- **Reopened claim:** a closed claim reopened for more costs on a loss already disclosed; in Florida noticed within one year of the loss ([Exposures, Reserves & Payments](/books/insurance/reserves-and-payments#closing-and-reopening)).
- **Reported claims (case incurred):** cumulative paid plus case outstanding at a date ([Exposures, Reserves & Payments](/books/insurance/reserves-and-payments#case-reserves-by-cost-type)).
- **Reservation of rights:** a letter telling the insured the insurer is investigating or defending while reserving the right to deny on stated grounds; in Florida due within 30 days of learning the defense ([First Notice, Coverage & Triage](/books/insurance/first-notice-and-triage#coverage-positions-reservations-of-rights-and-denials)).
- **Right of recourse:** the insured's 35-day window to show a comparable can't be bought for the total loss value, which reopens the valuation ([Auto Claims & Total Loss](/books/insurance/auto-claims-and-total-loss#valuation-rules)).
- **Safe harbor (Florida bad faith):** no third-party bad faith action if the insurer tenders the lesser of limits or the demand within 90 days of notice ([Bad Faith & Litigation](/books/insurance/bad-faith-and-litigation#florida-s-2023-changes)).
- **Salvage:** the damaged property an insurer takes and sells after paying a total loss; its net proceeds reduce the loss ([Subrogation, Salvage & Recovery](/books/insurance/subrogation-and-salvage#salvage)).
- **Salvage title:** a title brand for a vehicle damaged beyond a state's threshold or paid as a total loss ([Auto Claims & Total Loss](/books/insurance/auto-claims-and-total-loss#when-a-car-is-a-total-loss)).
- **Segmentation:** sorting a claim or exposure into a handling path (fast track, injury, total loss, complex, catastrophe, suspicious) at submission ([First Notice, Coverage & Triage](/books/insurance/first-notice-and-triage#segmentation-and-assignment)).
- **Social inflation:** claim costs rising faster than economic inflation because of larger verdicts, more litigation and litigation funding, as insurers' filings describe it ([Bad Faith & Litigation](/books/insurance/bad-faith-and-litigation#legal-spend-and-social-inflation-in-the-filings)).
- **Special investigations unit (SIU):** an insurer's unit, in-house or contracted, that investigates suspected fraud and reports it to the state ([Fraud & SIU](/books/insurance/insurance-fraud#state-requirements)).
- **Subrogation:** the insurer's right, after paying its insured, to recover from the party responsible for the loss ([Subrogation, Salvage & Recovery](/books/insurance/subrogation-and-salvage#subrogation-rights)).
- **Supplemental claim:** a claim for additional loss from the same peril already adjusted; in Florida noticed within 18 months of the loss ([Exposures, Reserves & Payments](/books/insurance/reserves-and-payments#closing-and-reopening)).
- **Third-party bad faith:** a liability insurer's failure to settle within limits when a prudent insurer would have, exposing it to the excess verdict ([Bad Faith & Litigation](/books/insurance/bad-faith-and-litigation#first-party-and-third-party-bad-faith)).
- **Time-limited demand:** an offer to settle a liability claim, usually for the limits, if accepted by a deadline; regulated in California and Georgia ([Bad Faith & Litigation](/books/insurance/bad-faith-and-litigation#time-limited-demands)).
- **Total loss:** a vehicle whose repair cost plus salvage exceeds its value, or that a state's title rule brands; settled at actual cash value with taxes and fees ([Auto Claims & Total Loss](/books/insurance/auto-claims-and-total-loss#when-a-car-is-a-total-loss)).
- **UTBMS:** the Uniform Task-Based Management System of task, activity and expense codes used on legal invoices ([Bad Faith & Litigation](/books/insurance/bad-faith-and-litigation#litigation-management)).
- **Verified policy:** a policy resolved from the policy system at FNOL, as against an unverified one captured from the reporter, which typically can't be paid on ([First Notice, Coverage & Triage](/books/insurance/first-notice-and-triage#verifying-coverage)).

## Reserves, Cat Risk and Reinsurance

- **Accident year:** the year a loss occurred, the usual origin period of a P&C loss triangle ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#what-a-reserve-contains)).
- **Actual cash value (ACV):** replacement cost less depreciation; many policies pay it first and the holdback on replacement ([Homeowners & Renters](/books/insurance/homeowners-and-renters#replacement-cost-acv-and-roofs)).
- **Adverse development cover (ADC):** excess of loss reinsurance on reserves for specified accident years, paying if they develop beyond an attachment ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#adverse-development-covers-and-loss-portfolio-transfers)).
- **Aggregate exceedance probability (AEP):** the annual probability that the sum of all catastrophe losses in a year exceeds an amount ([Catastrophe Models & Their Regulators](/books/insurance/catastrophe-models#what-a-model-does)).
- **Aggregate excess of loss:** reinsurance that pays when total subject losses over a period exceed an aggregate retention ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#excess-of-loss)).
- **Appointed Actuary:** the qualified actuary a P&C insurer's board appoints to sign its Statement of Actuarial Opinion on reserves ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#the-statement-of-actuarial-opinion)).
- **Average annual loss (AAL):** the expected catastrophe loss per year, the sum over modeled events of annual rate times mean loss ([Catastrophe Models & Their Regulators](/books/insurance/catastrophe-models#a-worked-event-loss-table)).
- **Basis risk:** the difference between what an index or parametric cover pays and the sponsor's actual loss ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#triggers)).
- **Bornhuetter-Ferguson method:** a reserving method that sets IBNR to expected losses times the share of ultimate not yet reported, weighting experience by maturity ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#bornhuetter-ferguson)).
- **Broad IBNR:** all unpaid claims other than case reserves: development on known claims, reopenings, pure IBNR and claims in transit ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#what-a-reserve-contains)).
- **Cash call:** a treaty clause letting the cedent bill a large loss immediately instead of in the periodic account ([Reinsurance Operations](/books/insurance/reinsurance-operations#accounts-and-bordereaux)).
- **Cat code:** the catastrophe event a claim is assigned to, used to count, staff, reserve and recover the event's losses ([Catastrophe Events](/books/insurance/catastrophes#cat-codes-on-claims)).
- **Catastrophe bond:** reinsurance issued as a security by a special purpose insurer, with the limit held in a collateral trust and principal lost if a trigger is met ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#catastrophe-bonds)).
- **Catastrophe excess of loss:** reinsurance that pays the part of one loss occurrence's total losses above a retention, up to a limit ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#excess-of-loss)).
- **Cede, cedent:** to transfer risk to a reinsurer; the insurer that does so ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#treaty-and-facultative)).
- **Ceding commission:** the percentage of ceded premium a proportional reinsurer pays the cedent for acquisition and administration costs, flat or sliding with the loss ratio ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#ceding-commission)).
- **Certified reinsurer:** a reinsurer from a qualified jurisdiction, rated by the commissioner, that posts collateral by its rating from 0% (Secure-1) to 100% (Vulnerable-6) ([Reinsurance Operations](/books/insurance/reinsurance-operations#credit-for-reinsurance)).
- **Chain ladder (development technique):** a reserving method projecting losses to ultimate with age-to-age factors from earlier accident years ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#chain-ladder)).
- **Collateralized reinsurance:** a reinsurance contract whose full limit is funded in trust by investors ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#sidecars-and-collateralized-reinsurance)).
- **Commutation:** a lump-sum settlement of all future obligations under a reinsurance contract ([Reinsurance Operations](/books/insurance/reinsurance-operations#recoverables-and-collections)).
- **Credit for reinsurance:** the extent to which a cedent may count reinsurance on its statutory balance sheet, set by the reinsurer's category and collateral ([Reinsurance Operations](/books/insurance/reinsurance-operations#credit-for-reinsurance)).
- **Cumulative development factor (CDF):** the product of age-to-age factors from an age to ultimate ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#chain-ladder)).
- **Defense and cost containment (DCC), adjusting and other (A&O):** the NAIC's split of loss adjustment expense since 1998: defense litigation and medical cost containment, and all other claim adjusting expense ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#loss-adjustment-expense)).
- **Event loss table (ELT):** a catastrophe model's output listing each simulated event with its annual rate and the portfolio's loss ([Catastrophe Models & Their Regulators](/books/insurance/catastrophe-models#what-a-model-does)).
- **Exceedance probability (EP) curve:** the annual probability that losses exceed each amount, on an occurrence (OEP) or aggregate (AEP) basis ([Catastrophe Models & Their Regulators](/books/insurance/catastrophe-models#what-a-model-does)).
- **Exposure snapshot:** a frozen set of insured locations with values, characteristics and terms sent to a catastrophe model ([Catastrophe Models & Their Regulators](/books/insurance/catastrophe-models#exposure-data)).
- **Facultative reinsurance:** reinsurance of a single risk, negotiated individually ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#treaty-and-facultative)).
- **Florida Commission on Hurricane Loss Projection Methodology:** the 12-member panel whose accepted hurricane and flood models Florida insurers must use, unmodified, in residential rate filings ([Catastrophe Models & Their Regulators](/books/insurance/catastrophe-models#florida-s-commission)).
- **Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund (FHCF):** Florida's mandatory state reinsurance fund reimbursing 45%, 75% or 90% of residential hurricane losses above a statutory retention ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#the-florida-hurricane-catastrophe-fund)).
- **Hours clause:** a reinsurance contract's definition of one loss occurrence as losses within a set number of hours from one cause ([Catastrophe Events](/books/insurance/catastrophes#cat-codes-on-claims)).
- **IBNER:** incurred but not enough reported: future development on known claims ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#what-a-reserve-contains)).
- **IBNR:** incurred but not reported; in the broad sense, all unpaid claims other than case reserves ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#what-a-reserve-contains)).
- **Indemnity trigger:** a cat bond or reinsurance trigger that pays on the sponsor's own losses ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#triggers)).
- **Industry index trigger:** a trigger that pays on an industry loss estimate, such as Verisk PCS's ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#triggers)).
- **Industry loss warranty (ILW):** reinsurance that pays based on the industry's total insured loss from an event rather than the buyer's own ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#triggers)).
- **Inuring order:** the contractual order in which reinsurance recoveries reduce the losses subject to other covers ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#inuring-order)).
- **Layer:** an excess of loss cover between an attachment and attachment plus limit, written as "limit xs attachment" ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#excess-of-loss)).
- **Loss development triangle:** losses by origin period and development age, each diagonal a valuation date ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#the-development-triangle)).
- **Loss portfolio transfer (LPT):** a cession of existing reserves and the assets backing them to a reinsurer ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#adverse-development-covers-and-loss-portfolio-transfers)).
- **Occurrence exceedance probability (OEP):** the annual probability that the largest single catastrophe event exceeds an amount ([Catastrophe Models & Their Regulators](/books/insurance/catastrophe-models#what-a-model-does)).
- **Parametric trigger:** a trigger that pays on a measured physical parameter such as wind speed or magnitude ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#triggers)).
- **PCS catastrophe:** an event Verisk Property Claim Services designates and numbers when it expects more than US$25 million of US insured damage affecting many policyholders and insurers ([Catastrophe Events](/books/insurance/catastrophes#how-an-event-becomes-a-catastrophe)).
- **Per risk excess of loss:** reinsurance that pays the part of a loss on one risk above a retention ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#excess-of-loss)).
- **Probable maximum loss (PML):** the loss at a stated return period on a stated EP curve ([Catastrophe Models & Their Regulators](/books/insurance/catastrophe-models#what-a-model-does)).
- **Provision for reinsurance:** a Schedule F liability reducing surplus for unsecured, overdue or under-collateralized recoverables ([Reinsurance Operations](/books/insurance/reinsurance-operations#schedule-f)).
- **Quota share:** proportional reinsurance ceding a fixed percentage of every policy's premium and losses ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#proportional-reinsurance)).
- **Rate on line (ROL):** an excess of loss layer's premium divided by its limit ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#excess-of-loss)).
- **Reciprocal jurisdiction reinsurer:** a reinsurer from the EU, the UK, an accredited US state or a qualifying jurisdiction that may post no collateral under the 2019 NAIC revisions ([Reinsurance Operations](/books/insurance/reinsurance-operations#credit-for-reinsurance)).
- **Reinstatement premium protection (RPP):** a cover that pays the reinstatement premium a cedent would owe after a loss ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#reinstatements)).
- **Reinstatement (reinsurance):** restoration of an excess of loss layer's limit after a loss, usually for an additional premium pro rata as to amount ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#reinstatements)).
- **Reinsurance recoverable:** an amount a reinsurer owes the cedent on paid or unpaid losses ([Reinsurance Operations](/books/insurance/reinsurance-operations#recoverables-and-collections)).
- **Retention (attachment):** the amount of loss a cedent bears before an excess of loss layer responds ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#excess-of-loss)).
- **Return period:** the reciprocal of an annual exceedance probability: a 1% probability is the 1-in-100-year loss ([Catastrophe Models & Their Regulators](/books/insurance/catastrophe-models#what-a-model-does)).
- **Risks attaching, losses occurring:** the two bases deciding which losses belong to a reinsurance contract: policies written in its period, or losses happening in it ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#treaty-and-facultative)).
- **Schedule F:** the annual statement schedule of a P&C insurer's reinsurance by counterparty, with recoverables, collateral and the provision for reinsurance ([Reinsurance Operations](/books/insurance/reinsurance-operations#schedule-f)).
- **Schedule P:** the annual statement's ten-year loss and expense development schedules by line, "the actuarial portion of the Annual Statement" ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#schedule-p)).
- **Sidecar:** a vehicle funded by investors that takes a quota share of a reinsurer's or insurer's book for a period ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#sidecars-and-collateralized-reinsurance)).
- **Statement of Actuarial Opinion:** the Appointed Actuary's annual opinion on whether carried reserves make a reasonable provision for unpaid losses and LAE ([Loss Reserving & Development](/books/insurance/loss-reserving#the-statement-of-actuarial-opinion)).
- **Surplus share:** proportional reinsurance ceding a per-risk share set by the risk's size in multiples (lines) of the cedent's retention ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#proportional-reinsurance)).
- **Terrorism Risk Insurance Program (TRIP):** the federal program sharing 80% of certified terrorism losses above a 20% insurer deductible, authorized through December 31, 2027 ([Cat Bonds, ILS & State Cat Funds](/books/insurance/alternative-capital#the-terrorism-risk-insurance-program)).
- **Treaty reinsurance:** reinsurance covering a defined class of business automatically for a period ([Reinsurance Structures](/books/insurance/reinsurance-structures#treaty-and-facultative)).

## Personal Lines

- **Community Rating System (CRS):** the NFIP program discounting premiums 5% to 45% in communities that exceed floodplain management minimums ([Flood & the NFIP](/books/insurance/flood-and-the-nfip#risk-rating-2-0)).
- **Coverages A to F:** the homeowners coverages: dwelling, other structures, personal property, loss of use, personal liability and medical payments to others ([Homeowners & Renters](/books/insurance/homeowners-and-renters#forms-and-coverages)).
- **Designated Responsible Producer (DRP):** the licensed individual a limited lines travel insurance producer names as responsible for compliance ([Pet & Travel Insurance](/books/insurance/pet-and-travel#travel-insurance)).
- **Emergency medical condition:** the Florida PIP finding that raises the medical benefit limit from $2,500 to $10,000 ([Personal Auto](/books/insurance/personal-auto#no-fault-and-personal-injury-protection)).
- **Federal Policy Fee:** the NFIP's per-policy fee, $47 for most policies, funding mapping and floodplain management ([Flood & the NFIP](/books/insurance/flood-and-the-nfip#risk-rating-2-0)).
- **Financial responsibility:** a state's requirement that a driver carry liability insurance or equivalent security at minimum limits ([Personal Auto](/books/insurance/personal-auto#compulsory-insurance-and-minimum-limits)).
- **Force-placed (lender-placed) insurance:** coverage a mortgage servicer buys and charges to a borrower whose own coverage lapsed ([Homeowners & Renters](/books/insurance/homeowners-and-renters#mortgagees-escrow-and-lender-requirements)).
- **HFIAA surcharge:** the NFIP's annual surcharge of $25 for primary residences and $250 for other properties ([Flood & the NFIP](/books/insurance/flood-and-the-nfip#risk-rating-2-0)).
- **Holdback:** the depreciation withheld from an ACV-first property payment until repair or replacement ([Homeowners & Renters](/books/insurance/homeowners-and-renters#replacement-cost-acv-and-roofs)).
- **Hurricane deductible:** a homeowners deductible, often a percentage of Coverage A, that applies to hurricane losses, in Florida across all hurricanes in a calendar year ([Homeowners & Renters](/books/insurance/homeowners-and-renters#wind-hail-and-hurricane-deductibles)).
- **Limited lines travel insurance producer (LLTIP):** a business entity licensed to sell travel insurance and to register travel retailers that offer it ([Pet & Travel Insurance](/books/insurance/pet-and-travel#travel-insurance)).
- **Mandatory purchase requirement:** the federal rule that loans on buildings in special flood hazard areas carry flood insurance ([Flood & the NFIP](/books/insurance/flood-and-the-nfip#mandatory-purchase)).
- **Negative option:** selling coverage by making the buyer deselect it, such as a pre-checked box, which the travel model act prohibits ([Pet & Travel Insurance](/books/insurance/pet-and-travel#travel-insurance)).
- **No-fault:** a system in which each person's own auto policy pays their injuries through PIP regardless of fault, with suits limited by a threshold ([Personal Auto](/books/insurance/personal-auto#no-fault-and-personal-injury-protection)).
- **Personal injury protection (PIP):** first-party auto coverage for medical costs, lost income and other expenses in no-fault states ([Personal Auto](/books/insurance/personal-auto#no-fault-and-personal-injury-protection)).
- **Pet insurance:** "a property insurance policy that provides coverage for accidents and illnesses of pets," in the NAIC model act's definition ([Pet & Travel Insurance](/books/insurance/pet-and-travel#pet-insurance)).
- **Preexisting condition (pet):** a condition with veterinary advice, treatment, or signs or symptoms before the policy's effective date or during a waiting period ([Pet & Travel Insurance](/books/insurance/pet-and-travel#pet-insurance)).
- **Private flood insurance:** flood coverage from a private insurer that lenders must accept if it is at least as broad as the NFIP's standard policy and meets the statute's terms ([Flood & the NFIP](/books/insurance/flood-and-the-nfip#mandatory-purchase)).
- **Replacement cost:** the cost to repair or replace with like kind and quality, without deduction for depreciation ([Homeowners & Renters](/books/insurance/homeowners-and-renters#replacement-cost-acv-and-roofs)).
- **Reserve Fund assessment:** the NFIP's 18% charge on premium funding its reserve ([Flood & the NFIP](/books/insurance/flood-and-the-nfip#risk-rating-2-0)).
- **Risk Rating 2.0:** the NFIP's building-level pricing method, fully in effect from April 1, 2023 ([Flood & the NFIP](/books/insurance/flood-and-the-nfip#risk-rating-2-0)).
- **SR-22:** a certificate an insurer files with a state proving a driver's future financial responsibility, with a duty to notify the state if coverage ends ([Personal Auto](/books/insurance/personal-auto#sr-22-and-fr-44-filings)).
- **Stacking:** adding together the UM or UIM limits of several vehicles or policies ([Personal Auto](/books/insurance/personal-auto#uninsured-and-underinsured-motorists)).
- **State insurance verification system:** a state system (TexasSure and others) to which insurers report auto coverage or answer inquiries ([Personal Auto](/books/insurance/personal-auto#state-insurance-verification)).
- **Travel protection plan:** a plan combining travel insurance, travel assistance services and cancellation fee waivers sold at one price ([Pet & Travel Insurance](/books/insurance/pet-and-travel#travel-insurance)).
- **Travel retailer:** a business that offers travel insurance under a limited lines travel insurance producer's license ([Pet & Travel Insurance](/books/insurance/pet-and-travel#travel-insurance)).
- **Underinsured motorists (UIM):** coverage for the insured's injuries when the at-fault driver's liability limits are insufficient, paid on an excess or limits-less basis ([Personal Auto](/books/insurance/personal-auto#uninsured-and-underinsured-motorists)).
- **Uninsured motorists (UM):** coverage for the insured's injuries caused by an uninsured or hit-and-run driver ([Personal Auto](/books/insurance/personal-auto#uninsured-and-underinsured-motorists)).
- **Waiting period (flood):** the 30 days before a new NFIP policy takes effect, with exceptions for loans, map revisions and post-wildfire flooding ([Flood & the NFIP](/books/insurance/flood-and-the-nfip#coverage-limits-and-the-waiting-period)).
- **Waiting period (pet):** time before some coverage begins; the NAIC model caps illness waiting periods at 30 days and bans them for accidents ([Pet & Travel Insurance](/books/insurance/pet-and-travel#pet-insurance)).
- **Wellness program:** a pet health subscription separate from insurance that may not be marketed as insurance or required for eligibility ([Pet & Travel Insurance](/books/insurance/pet-and-travel#pet-insurance)).
- **Write Your Own (WYO):** the NFIP arrangement under which private insurers sell and service federal flood policies in their own names as fiscal agents ([Flood & the NFIP](/books/insurance/flood-and-the-nfip#write-your-own)).

## Commercial and Specialty Lines

- **Additional insured:** a party other than the named insured covered by an endorsement on the policy (in GL, ISO's CG 20 10, CG 20 37 and their blanket versions); a certificate saying so confers nothing without the endorsement ([Certificates of Insurance](/books/insurance/certificates-of-insurance#additional-insured-endorsements)).
- **Approved Insurance Provider (AIP):** a private insurer that sells, services and adjusts federal crop insurance policies and shares their gains and losses with FCIC under the Standard Reinsurance Agreement ([Crop Insurance](/books/insurance/crop-insurance#the-standard-reinsurance-agreement)).
- **Business owners policy (BOP):** a pre-packaged property and liability policy for small eligible businesses, rated by one algorithm on one form set ([The Commercial Lines Map](/books/insurance/commercial-lines#bop-versus-package)).
- **Certificate of insurance (COI):** a form (usually ACORD 25) showing that policies exist, issued for information only; it cannot amend, extend or alter the coverage or confer rights beyond the policy ([Certificates of Insurance](/books/insurance/certificates-of-insurance#what-a-certificate-is)).
- **Circular 570:** the US Treasury's list of companies certified as sureties and reinsurers on federal bonds, with each company's underwriting limitation ([Surety Bonds](/books/insurance/surety#circular-570-and-the-underwriting-limitation)).
- **Claims-made policy:** a policy that covers claims first made during the policy period (or an extended reporting period) for wrongful acts after the retroactive date ([Professional & Management Liability](/books/insurance/professional-and-management-liability#claims-made-mechanics)).
- **Claims-made relationship:** the period from the first claims-made policy with an insurer through its renewals, during which the retroactive date may not change in New York ([Professional & Management Liability](/books/insurance/professional-and-management-liability#claims-made-mechanics)).
- **Commercial general liability (CGL):** liability coverage for bodily injury and property damage (Coverage A), personal and advertising injury (B) and medical payments (C), on ISO's CG 00 01 occurrence or CG 00 02 claims-made forms ([The Commercial Lines Map](/books/insurance/commercial-lines#the-lines)).
- **Defense within limits:** a policy whose defense costs reduce the limit of liability, regulated in New York by minimum limits and a 50% cap ([Professional & Management Liability](/books/insurance/professional-and-management-liability#defense-within-limits)).
- **Diligent search:** the surplus lines broker's documented attempt to place a risk with admitted insurers (three declinations in New York and California) before placing it non-admitted ([Excess & Surplus Lines](/books/insurance/excess-and-surplus-lines#diligent-search)).
- **Excess and surplus lines (E&S):** insurance placed by a licensed surplus lines broker with an insurer not admitted in the home state but eligible there, free of rate and form filing ([Excess & Surplus Lines](/books/insurance/excess-and-surplus-lines)).
- **Extended reporting period (tail):** time after a claims-made policy ends during which claims for earlier wrongful acts may still be made, automatic for 60 days in New York and offered for longer ([Professional & Management Liability](/books/insurance/professional-and-management-liability#claims-made-mechanics)).
- **Federal Crop Insurance Corporation (FCIC):** the federal corporation, managed by USDA's Risk Management Agency, that offers, subsidizes and reinsures federal crop insurance ([Crop Insurance](/books/insurance/crop-insurance)).
- **General indemnity agreement:** the contract by which a surety's principal and its owners agree to repay the surety for every loss and cost under its bonds and to post collateral on demand ([Surety Bonds](/books/insurance/surety#indemnity-agreements)).
- **Loss run:** an insurer's report of an insured's claims, with paid, reserved and incurred amounts as of a valuation date, used by other insurers to underwrite the account ([The Commercial Lines Map](/books/insurance/commercial-lines#loss-runs)).
- **Miller Act:** the federal law requiring performance and payment bonds on federal construction contracts above a threshold ($150,000 under the FAR) ([Surety Bonds](/books/insurance/surety#federal-bonds-and-the-miller-act)).
- **Nonadmitted and Reinsurance Reform Act (NRRA):** the 2010 federal law making the insured's home state the only state that may tax, license the broker for and regulate a surplus lines placement ([Excess & Surplus Lines](/books/insurance/excess-and-surplus-lines#the-home-state-rule-worked)).
- **Occurrence policy:** a policy that covers injury or damage happening during the policy period, whenever the claim is made ([The Commercial Lines Map](/books/insurance/commercial-lines#occurrence-versus-claims-made)).
- **Penal sum:** the maximum amount a surety is liable for under a bond ([Surety Bonds](/books/insurance/surety)).
- **Retroactive date:** the date before which wrongful acts are not covered by a claims-made policy ([Professional & Management Liability](/books/insurance/professional-and-management-liability#claims-made-mechanics)).
- **Revenue protection:** a federal crop plan whose guarantee is the production guarantee times the greater of the projected and harvest prices, with production to count valued at the harvest price ([Crop Insurance](/books/insurance/crop-insurance#plans-and-subsidy)).
- **Schedule rating:** a filed plan that credits or debits a commercial premium for risk characteristics within set maximums, chosen and justified by the underwriter ([The Commercial Lines Map](/books/insurance/commercial-lines#how-commercial-rating-differs)).
- **Silent cyber:** exposure to cyber losses under policies that neither grant nor exclude them ([Cyber Insurance](/books/insurance/cyber-insurance#silent-cyber)).
- **Stamping office:** the body in a state that receives, checks and records every surplus lines transaction and its tax ([Excess & Surplus Lines](/books/insurance/excess-and-surplus-lines#stamping-offices-and-filing)).
- **Standard Reinsurance Agreement (SRA):** the annual agreement under which FCIC reinsures and subsidizes AIPs, with fund designations, retentions, loss-ratio bands and A&O subsidy ([Crop Insurance](/books/insurance/crop-insurance#the-standard-reinsurance-agreement)).
- **State-backed cyber-attack exclusion:** a clause, mandatory in Lloyd's standalone cyber policies since March 31, 2023, excluding attacks attributed to a state that significantly impair its functioning or security ([Cyber Insurance](/books/insurance/cyber-insurance#war-and-state-backed-attack-exclusions)).
- **Surety bond:** a three-party guarantee in which a surety promises an obligee that a principal will perform an obligation, with the principal liable to repay the surety ([Surety Bonds](/books/insurance/surety)).
- **Underwriting limitation:** the largest single risk a Treasury-certified surety may write on its own, 10% of its paid-up capital and surplus ([Surety Bonds](/books/insurance/surety#circular-570-and-the-underwriting-limitation)).

## Workers' Compensation

- **D-ratio (discount ratio):** the share of a class's expected losses expected to be primary, used to split expected losses in the experience mod ([Rating Workers' Comp & Premium Audit](/books/insurance/rating-workers-comp#the-experience-mod)).
- **EDI Claims (IAIABC):** the standard for electronic first and subsequent reports of injury from insurers to state agencies, now Release 3.1 ([Workers' Comp Claims](/books/insurance/workers-comp-claims#edi-reporting-to-the-state)).
- **Employers liability:** Part Two of the workers' compensation policy, covering employee injury suits outside the exclusive remedy, with stated limits ([Workers' Compensation](/books/insurance/workers-compensation#the-policy)).
- **Experience modification (mod):** a bureau-computed factor comparing an employer's actual losses to expected losses for its classes and payroll, applied to manual premium ([Rating Workers' Comp & Premium Audit](/books/insurance/rating-workers-comp#the-experience-mod)).
- **First Report of Injury (FROI):** the EDI transaction reporting a workers' compensation claim and its facts to the state ([Workers' Comp Claims](/books/insurance/workers-comp-claims#edi-reporting-to-the-state)).
- **Large deductible plan:** a standard workers' compensation policy under which the employer reimburses the insurer for each claim up to a deductible, secured by collateral ([Rating Workers' Comp & Premium Audit](/books/insurance/rating-workers-comp#retrospective-rating-and-deductible-plans)).
- **Medicare set-aside (WCMSA):** money allocated in a settlement for future Medicare-covered care, reviewed by CMS above set thresholds ([Workers' Comp Claims](/books/insurance/workers-comp-claims#medicare-secondary-payer)).
- **Monopolistic state fund:** a state fund that is the only source of primary workers' compensation insurance in its state (North Dakota, Ohio, Washington, Wyoming) ([Workers' Compensation](/books/insurance/workers-compensation#state-funds)).
- **Multiple coordinated policy:** a PEO workers' compensation structure with a separate policy for each client, so each keeps its own experience ([Workers' Compensation](/books/insurance/workers-compensation#peos-and-master-policies)).
- **Non-subscriber:** a Texas employer that has elected not to carry workers' compensation and so loses its common law defenses ([Workers' Compensation](/books/insurance/workers-compensation#texas-non-subscription)).
- **Pay-as-you-go:** workers' compensation premium computed and paid from each payroll run rather than an annual estimate ([Rating Workers' Comp & Premium Audit](/books/insurance/rating-workers-comp#pay-as-you-go)).
- **Premium audit:** the post-term review of actual payroll by class and state that replaces the estimate and sets the final premium ([Rating Workers' Comp & Premium Audit](/books/insurance/rating-workers-comp#premium-audit)).
- **Primary and excess losses:** the parts of each claim below and above the experience rating split point, weighted fully and partly in the mod ([Rating Workers' Comp & Premium Audit](/books/insurance/rating-workers-comp#the-experience-mod)).
- **Retrospective rating:** a plan that recomputes a policy year's premium from that year's own losses, within a minimum and maximum ([Rating Workers' Comp & Premium Audit](/books/insurance/rating-workers-comp#retrospective-rating-and-deductible-plans)).
- **Section 111 reporting:** the duty of workers' compensation insurers and self-insurers to report claims involving Medicare beneficiaries to CMS ([Workers' Comp Claims](/books/insurance/workers-comp-claims#medicare-secondary-payer)).
- **Split point:** the per-claim amount dividing primary from excess losses in the experience mod, now state-specific (for example $25,000 in North Carolina) ([Rating Workers' Comp & Premium Audit](/books/insurance/rating-workers-comp#the-experience-mod)).
- **Statutory coverage:** Part One of the workers' compensation policy, which pays whatever benefits each listed state's law requires ([Workers' Compensation](/books/insurance/workers-compensation#the-policy)).
- **Subsequent Report of Injury (SROI):** an EDI transaction reporting a benefit event on a claim (initial payment, change, suspension, final) to the state ([Workers' Comp Claims](/books/insurance/workers-comp-claims#edi-reporting-to-the-state)).
- **Waiting period:** the first days of disability for which no indemnity is paid, refunded once disability lasts past a state's retroactive period ([Workers' Comp Claims](/books/insurance/workers-comp-claims#indemnity-benefits)).

## Life and Annuities

- **Accelerated death benefit:** part of a life policy's face paid before death on terminal (and, with some riders, chronic) illness, treated as paid by reason of death for tax when the insured is certified as expected to die within 24 months ([Life Products as Data](/books/insurance/life-products#riders)).
- **Accelerated underwriting:** issuing life coverage without an exam or fluids when data pulled in seconds (MIB, prescription history, MVR, a mortality score) and a rules engine clear the applicant ([Life Underwriting](/books/insurance/life-underwriting#accelerated-and-fluidless-underwriting)).
- **Account value:** the policy value of a universal life policy or deferred annuity: premiums in, charges out, interest or index credits added, recomputed every cycle ([Life Products as Data](/books/insurance/life-products#universal-life)).
- **AG 49-A, AG 49-B:** NAIC actuarial guidelines interpreting Model #582 for indexed universal life, limiting the maximum illustrated rate, indexed loan benefits and, from May 1, 2023, illustration above the benchmark index account ([Illustrations](/books/insurance/illustrations#ag-49-and-indexed-ul)).
- **AG 55:** NAIC Actuarial Guideline LV, requiring asset adequacy testing and an attribution analysis for large reinsurance transactions since 2016, first reported April 1, 2026 ([Life Reserves, Capital & Private Equity](/books/insurance/life-reserves-and-capital#asset-adequacy-testing-and-ag-55)).
- **Annuitant:** the person whose life measures an annuity's payments, who may differ from the owner ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#the-anatomy-of-an-annuity)).
- **Benefit base:** a notional amount on a guaranteed withdrawal or death benefit rider, grown by roll-ups and step-ups, on which guaranteed payments are computed; never paid as cash ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#variable-annuities)).
- **Block reinsurance, flow reinsurance:** ceding all or part of a block of previously issued contracts, versus ceding a share of each new policy as it is written ([Life Reserves, Capital & Private Equity](/books/insurance/life-reserves-and-capital#life-reinsurance)).
- **Buffer, floor (RILA):** downside protection on a registered index-linked annuity: with a buffer the insurer absorbs the first loss; with a floor the owner absorbs losses only down to a set level ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#registered-index-linked-annuities)).
- **Cap, participation rate, spread:** the crediting parameters of an indexed strategy: the maximum credit, the share of the index change credited, and the amount subtracted from it ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#fixed-indexed-annuities)).
- **Cash value accumulation test (CVAT):** the 7702 test under which cash surrender value may never exceed the net single premium for the contract's future benefits ([Life Insurance & the Tax Code](/books/insurance/life-insurance-and-tax#the-definition-of-life-insurance)).
- **Cash value corridor:** the 7702(d) minimum death benefit as a percentage of cash value, 250% through age 40 grading to 100% at 95, required with the guideline premium test ([Life Insurance & the Tax Code](/books/insurance/life-insurance-and-tax#the-cash-value-corridor)).
- **Certificate of coverage:** the document a group insurer issues for each covered person, stating the coverage under the employer's master policy ([Group Life & Disability](/books/insurance/group-life-and-disability#group-term-life-and-ad-d)).
- **Contestable period:** the period, two years from issue under the New York, Texas and Florida statutes, during which the insurer may void a life policy for material misrepresentation; it never ends if the insured dies within it ([Death Claims & Unclaimed Benefits](/books/insurance/death-claims-and-unclaimed-benefits#the-contestable-period)).
- **Conversion privilege:** the right to exchange term or group life coverage for an individual permanent policy without evidence of insurability before a deadline ([In-Force Administration](/books/insurance/in-force-administration#conversions)).
- **Cost of insurance (COI):** the monthly mortality charge on a universal life policy, the net amount at risk in thousands times the COI rate for the insured's class and age ([Life Products as Data](/books/insurance/life-products#universal-life)).
- **CTE 70:** conditional tail expectation at 70, "the average of the highest 30% modeled outcomes," the measure that sets stochastic reserves under the Valuation Manual ([Life Reserves, Capital & Private Equity](/books/insurance/life-reserves-and-capital#vm-21-and-vm-22-annuities)).
- **Death Master File (DMF):** the Social Security Administration's file of reported deaths, which insurers compare against their in-force books to find unclaimed benefits; recent deaths are in the Limited Access DMF, open only to certified users ([Death Claims & Unclaimed Benefits](/books/insurance/death-claims-and-unclaimed-benefits#death-master-file-matching)).
- **Deductible sources of income:** the other income an LTD certificate subtracts from the gross benefit, chiefly Social Security disability ([Group Life & Disability](/books/insurance/group-life-and-disability#short-and-long-term-disability)).
- **Disciplined current scale:** the scale of non-guaranteed elements an illustration actuary certifies annually as supported by recent experience, the limit on what an illustration may show ([Illustrations](/books/insurance/illustrations#model-582-what-an-illustration-is)).
- **Escheatment:** turning unclaimed money over to the state after a dormancy period ([Death Claims & Unclaimed Benefits](/books/insurance/death-claims-and-unclaimed-benefits#unclaimed-property-and-escheat)).
- **Evidence of insurability (EOI):** individual underwriting evidence required for group coverage above guaranteed issue limits or for late entrants ([Group Life & Disability](/books/insurance/group-life-and-disability#group-term-life-and-ad-d)).
- **Financed purchase:** buying a new policy with values withdrawn, surrendered or borrowed from an existing one, treated as a replacement under Model #613 ([Best Interest, Replacements & 1035 Exchanges](/books/insurance/annuity-best-interest#replacements-under-model-613)).
- **Fixed indexed annuity (FIA):** a fixed annuity whose interest is computed from an index change subject to a cap, participation rate or spread, with a floor usually at 0% ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#fixed-indexed-annuities)).
- **Free look:** the period after delivery in which an owner may return a policy or annuity for a refund; its length and refund depend on state law, replacement status and disclosure timing ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#free-look)).
- **GLWB, GMWB for life:** a guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit rider letting the owner withdraw a percentage of a benefit base every year for life even after the account value is exhausted ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#variable-annuities)).
- **Guideline premium test:** the 7702 test limiting cumulative premiums to the greater of the guideline single premium and the sum of guideline level premiums, paired with the cash value corridor ([Life Insurance & the Tax Code](/books/insurance/life-insurance-and-tax#the-definition-of-life-insurance)).
- **Illustration actuary:** the actuary the insurer's board appoints to certify annually that illustrations follow Model #582 and the disciplined current scale ([Illustrations](/books/insurance/illustrations#the-illustration-actuary)).
- **Interim value:** the daily value of a RILA segment before its term ends, computed by a prospectus formula approximating the fair value of its options ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#registered-index-linked-annuities)).
- **Investment in the contract:** premiums paid less amounts received tax-free, the owner's basis in a life policy or annuity ([Life Insurance & the Tax Code](/books/insurance/life-insurance-and-tax#loans-withdrawals-and-surrenders)).
- **Lapse-supported illustration:** an illustration that fails the self-support test when persistency after year five is assumed to be 100%, prohibited by Model #582 for policies with nonforfeiture values ([Illustrations](/books/insurance/illustrations#the-self-support-and-lapse-support-tests)).
- **Market value adjustment (MVA):** a positive or negative adjustment to an early withdrawal from a fixed guarantee period that passes interest rate changes since issue through to the owner ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#surrender-charges-free-withdrawals-and-mvas)).
- **MIB:** the member database of coded findings from individually underwritten life, health and disability applications in the past seven years, a consumer reporting agency under the FCRA ([Life Underwriting](/books/insurance/life-underwriting#accelerated-and-fluidless-underwriting)).
- **Minimum nonforfeiture amount:** the floor on a deferred annuity's cash surrender value, 87.5% of gross considerations accumulated at the nonforfeiture rate less withdrawals, a $50 annual charge, premium tax and loans ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#the-nonforfeiture-floor)).
- **Modified coinsurance (modco):** life reinsurance in which the cedent keeps the assets and reserves and passes the reinsurer its share through a periodic reserve adjustment ([Life Reserves, Capital & Private Equity](/books/insurance/life-reserves-and-capital#life-reinsurance)).
- **Modified endowment contract (MEC):** a life policy that fails the 7-pay test, whose withdrawals and loans are taxed income first with a 10% additional tax before 59½ ([Life Insurance & the Tax Code](/books/insurance/life-insurance-and-tax#the-7-pay-test-and-modified-endowment-contracts)).
- **Multi-year guaranteed annuity (MYGA):** a fixed deferred annuity guaranteeing one rate for its whole surrender charge period ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#fixed-and-multi-year-guaranteed-annuities)).
- **Net amount at risk (NAR):** the death benefit minus the account value (discounted a month at the guaranteed rate in many contracts), the amount the COI charge and YRT reinsurance are computed on ([Life Products as Data](/books/insurance/life-products#universal-life)).
- **Nonforfeiture options:** the cash surrender value, reduced paid-up insurance and extended term insurance a permanent policy must offer on lapse under Model #808 ([Life Products as Data](/books/insurance/life-products#nonforfeiture-value-on-lapse)).
- **Paid-up additions:** small single-premium whole life additions bought with participating dividends, adding face and cash value ([Life Products as Data](/books/insurance/life-products#whole-life)).
- **Pension risk transfer (buy-out, buy-in):** moving a pension plan's obligations to an insurer through a group annuity that pays retirees directly (buy-out) or is held as a plan asset (buy-in) ([Life Reserves, Capital & Private Equity](/books/insurance/life-reserves-and-capital#pension-risk-transfer)).
- **Principle-based reserving (PBR):** statutory reserving under the Valuation Manual at the higher of a prescribed floor and a reserve modeled over economic scenarios with the company's experience ([Life Reserves, Capital & Private Equity](/books/insurance/life-reserves-and-capital#from-formulas-to-principle-based-reserves)).
- **Registered index-linked annuity (RILA):** an index-linked deferred annuity with a buffer or floor instead of a 0% floor, registered with the SEC because the owner can lose principal ([Annuities & Riders](/books/insurance/annuities#registered-index-linked-annuities)).
- **Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI):** Rule 15l-1, requiring a broker-dealer recommending a security, strategy or account to a retail customer to act in the customer's best interest, through disclosure, care, conflict of interest and compliance obligations ([Best Interest, Replacements & 1035 Exchanges](/books/insurance/annuity-best-interest#regulation-best-interest-and-the-safe-harbor)).
- **Replacement:** a purchase of a new policy or annuity that lapses, surrenders, converts, reduces or finances from an existing one, with producer and insurer duties under Model #613 ([Best Interest, Replacements & 1035 Exchanges](/books/insurance/annuity-best-interest#replacements-under-model-613)).
- **Retained asset account:** settling death proceeds into an insurer-held account with check or draft writing, included in Death Master File comparisons under the NCOIL model ([Death Claims & Unclaimed Benefits](/books/insurance/death-claims-and-unclaimed-benefits#who-gets-paid)).
- **Section 1035 exchange:** a tax-free exchange of a life, endowment, annuity or qualified LTC contract for another allowed kind, with carryover basis ([Life Insurance & the Tax Code](/books/insurance/life-insurance-and-tax#1035-exchanges-and-basis)).
- **Self-supporting illustration:** an illustration whose policy cash flows, on the disciplined current scale's experience, cover all owner values from the 15th anniversary (20th for second-to-die) on ([Illustrations](/books/insurance/illustrations#the-self-support-and-lapse-support-tests)).
- **Seven-pay test:** the 7702A test comparing premiums paid in the first seven contract years with seven level premiums that would pay up the policy; failing it makes a MEC ([Life Insurance & the Tax Code](/books/insurance/life-insurance-and-tax#the-7-pay-test-and-modified-endowment-contracts)).
- **Sidecar (life):** a separate, usually fully collateralized vehicle through which third-party investors share in reinsured life and annuity business for a share of premiums ([Life Reserves, Capital & Private Equity](/books/insurance/life-reserves-and-capital#bermuda-cayman-and-sidecars)).
- **Suitability (best interest) record:** the consumer profile, options considered, rationale, disclosures and insurer review behind an annuity recommendation under Model #275 ([Best Interest, Replacements & 1035 Exchanges](/books/insurance/annuity-best-interest#model-275-the-best-interest-standard)).
- **Universal life secondary guarantee (ULSG):** a no-lapse guarantee keeping a universal life policy in force while a shadow account or premium test is met, even at zero account value ([Life Products as Data](/books/insurance/life-products#universal-life)).
- **Valuation Manual:** the NAIC's annual manual of statutory reserve requirements, operative January 1, 2017, with VM-20 for life, VM-21 for variable annuities and VM-22 for non-variable annuities ([Life Reserves, Capital & Private Equity](/books/insurance/life-reserves-and-capital#from-formulas-to-principle-based-reserves)).
- **Yearly renewable term (YRT) reinsurance:** reinsurance of a share of the net amount at risk for a yearly premium per thousand, with the cedent keeping reserves and cash values ([Life Reserves, Capital & Private Equity](/books/insurance/life-reserves-and-capital#life-reinsurance)).

## Embedded and Service Contracts

- **Administrator (service contracts):** the person responsible for administering service contracts and for the filings a state's service contract law requires; registers with the commissioner and keeps the records ([Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#the-parties)).
- **Attach rate:** the share of eligible purchases on which the buyer adds the embedded coverage offered; the demand curve of an embedded program ([Embedded Insurance](/books/insurance/embedded-insurance#the-money-premium-compensation-and-revenue-share)).
- **Contractual liability insurance:** a policy covering a service contract or home warranty company's obligations to holders, which in Florida can replace the unearned premium reserve when it covers 100% of claim exposure ([Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#financial-security-the-three-options)).
- **Dealer obligor:** a vehicle dealer that is itself the provider obligated on the service contracts it sells, so the program's security depends on the dealer ([Vehicle Service Contracts & GAP](/books/insurance/vehicle-service-contracts-and-gap#dealer-obligor-and-third-party-obligor-programs)).
- **Debt cancellation contract:** a loan term under which a lender agrees to cancel all or part of the debt on a specified event, regulated for national banks as a banking product rather than insurance ([Vehicle Service Contracts & GAP](/books/insurance/vehicle-service-contracts-and-gap#what-the-products-are)).
- **Embedded insurance:** coverage offered inside the purchase of another product (a trip, a rental, an order, a lease, a car), by a partner who is licensed, holds a limited lines license, only refers, or sells a product that isn't insurance ([Embedded Insurance](/books/insurance/embedded-insurance)).
- **GAP waiver:** a term of a credit contract under which the creditor forgives the difference between the balance owed and the vehicle's value after a total loss; the waiver form of GAP, as opposed to GAP insurance ([Vehicle Service Contracts & GAP](/books/insurance/vehicle-service-contracts-and-gap#what-the-products-are)).
- **Home warranty:** a contract to repair or replace a home's systems and appliances (and in Florida, structural components) for a fee, regulated as its own line of business in some states ([Home Warranties & Product Protection](/books/insurance/home-warranties-and-protection-plans#home-warranty-licensing)).
- **Limited lines license:** a narrow producer license for coverage incidental to another sale, such as travel, rental car, credit, self-storage or portable electronics insurance ([Embedded Insurance](/books/insurance/embedded-insurance#who-may-sell-it-four-licensing-models)).
- **Mechanical breakdown insurance:** a policy issued by an authorized insurer covering the same failures as a service contract; excluded from the NAIC model's service contract definition ([Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#warranty-service-contract-or-insurance)).
- **Offer presentation:** the record of an embedded offer as shown to a buyer (content version, disclosures, unselected default, the buyer's choice), kept for sold and declined offers ([Embedded Insurance](/books/insurance/embedded-insurance#how-to-build-it)).
- **Provider fee:** the consideration paid for a service contract (in the NAIC model, the amount in excess of the reimbursement insurance premium); not subject to premium tax ([Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#what-the-contract-must-say)).
- **Provider (obligor):** the person contractually obligated to perform under a service contract, which must meet the state's financial security rule ([Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#the-parties)).
- **Reimbursement insurance policy:** a policy issued to a service contract provider under which the insurer discharges the provider's obligations to holders, including refunds, if the provider doesn't perform ([Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#the-parties)).
- **Repair authorization:** the administrator's approval of a covered repair, with an amount and an authorization number, given before a facility repairs a vehicle or product under a service contract ([Vehicle Service Contracts & GAP](/books/insurance/vehicle-service-contracts-and-gap#claims-and-repair-authorization)).
- **Service contract:** a contract for a separately stated price and a specific duration to repair, replace or maintain property after failure from defects or wear and tear; not insurance in most states when it meets the state's regime ([Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#warranty-service-contract-or-insurance)).
- **Service dispatch:** one technician visit on one item of a home warranty or protection plan claim, with its diagnosis and repair, replace or cash decision ([Home Warranties & Product Protection](/books/insurance/home-warranties-and-protection-plans#claims-as-repair-or-replace-logistics)).
- **Service warranty:** Florida's term for a consumer product service contract regulated under chapter 634, Part III ([Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#financial-security-the-three-options)).
- **Vehicle service contract (VSC):** a service contract covering repairs to a vehicle after the factory warranty, usually sold and financed at the dealer ([Vehicle Service Contracts & GAP](/books/insurance/vehicle-service-contracts-and-gap)).
- **Warranty (Magnuson-Moss):** a promise given with the product at no extra charge as part of the basis of the bargain; one that costs extra or comes after the sale is a service contract ([Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#warranty-service-contract-or-insurance)).

## Running the Platform

- **ACORD:** the insurance industry's standards body, publishing forms, the AL3 batch format, P&C and life XML message standards and newer API standards ([ACORD & Data Standards](/books/insurance/acord-and-data-standards)).
- **AL3:** ACORD's batch format for carrier-to-agency policy, claim and commission data, under much of the agency download traffic ([ACORD & Data Standards](/books/insurance/acord-and-data-standards#al3)).
- **Big bang conversion:** converting every in-force policy to a new system at one cutoff, midterm ([Replacing a Core System](/books/insurance/replacing-a-core-system#conversion-strategies)).
- **Conversion transfer:** a pair of entries moving a policy's unearned premium from the old system's ledger to the new one at conversion, excluded from written premium, premium tax, statistics and commissions ([Replacing a Core System](/books/insurance/replacing-a-core-system#migrating-in-force-policies)).
- **Core platform:** the system of record for an insurer's policies, billing and claims, bought from a vendor or built ([Core Platforms](/books/insurance/core-platforms)).
- **Data call:** a regulator's request for data the routine reports don't contain, answered under the call's own definitions and deadline ([Regulatory & Statistical Reporting](/books/insurance/regulatory-and-statistical-reporting#data-calls-and-the-mcas)).
- **Data collection agent:** a contractor designated by a state agency to receive reports on its behalf, such as Verisk for Texas workers' comp claims EDI ([ACORD & Data Standards](/books/insurance/acord-and-data-standards#iaiabc-claims-edi)).
- **FROI and SROI:** the First and Subsequent Reports of Injury, the IAIABC EDI transactions workers' comp insurers send to states ([ACORD & Data Standards](/books/insurance/acord-and-data-standards#iaiabc-claims-edi)).
- **Maintenance type code (MTC):** the code on an IAIABC EDI transaction naming the event it reports, such as 00 (original FROI) or IP (initial indemnity payment) ([ACORD & Data Standards](/books/insurance/acord-and-data-standards#iaiabc-claims-edi)).
- **Parallel run:** operating both systems on the same work to compare results; in a policy conversion, the premium, bills, notices, claim postings and reports, before cutover ([Replacing a Core System](/books/insurance/replacing-a-core-system#parallel-runs)).
- **Premium tax:** a state tax on insurance premiums written on risks in the state, computed from direct premium by state with deductions, credits and prepayments ([Regulatory & Statistical Reporting](/books/insurance/regulatory-and-statistical-reporting#premium-tax)).
- **Renewal rollover:** converting each policy to a new system at its renewal, so the old system runs off over one term ([Replacing a Core System](/books/insurance/replacing-a-core-system#conversion-strategies)).
- **Retaliatory tax:** a tax on a foreign insurer equal to the excess of what its home state would charge a company from this state over this state's own charges on the same premium ([Regulatory & Statistical Reporting](/books/insurance/regulatory-and-statistical-reporting#premium-tax)).
- **Statistical agent:** an organization designated by states to receive insurers' statistical plan data, such as Verisk ISO or NISS ([Regulatory & Statistical Reporting](/books/insurance/regulatory-and-statistical-reporting#statistical-plans-and-agents)).
- **Statistical plan:** the rules and codes under which an insurer reports premium and loss transactions to a statistical agent, by line, coverage, class and territory ([Regulatory & Statistical Reporting](/books/insurance/regulatory-and-statistical-reporting#statistical-plans-and-agents)).
- **Subledger:** a ledger fed by one system (premium, receivables, commissions, taxes, losses, reinsurance) that rolls up to the general ledger and feeds the statutory statement ([Insurance System Architecture](/books/insurance/insurance-system-architecture#the-insurance-ledger)).
- **System of record:** the one system whose copy of a record wins when systems disagree; during a conversion it differs policy by policy and claim by claim ([Core Platforms](/books/insurance/core-platforms#how-to-build-it)).
- **TXLife:** ACORD's life, annuity and health XML message envelope, carrying requests with a TransRefGUID and TransType ([ACORD & Data Standards](/books/insurance/acord-and-data-standards#acord-life-txlife)).
