A car sale is the largest embedded insurance moment in consumer life. In the finance office, after the price is agreed, the buyer is offered a vehicle service contract (repairs after the factory warranty), GAP (the difference between the loan balance and the car's value if it's totaled), and a list of smaller products (tire and wheel, key replacement, paintless dent repair, windshield, appearance protection). Most of them are financed into the retail installment contract, so they are bought with borrowed money, priced with a dealer markup, and outlive the sale by years. Each one has a legal form (insurance, service contract, or a term of the loan), and the form decides who regulates it, who may sell it and how much comes back when the loan is paid off early.

This chapter covers the insurance and service contract side: how vehicle service contracts are regulated (Florida's motor vehicle service agreement law, New York and Texas), dealer-obligor and third-party-obligor programs, how claims and repair authorization work, GAP as insurance versus waiver, and refunds on payoff, repossession and total loss, worked. Selling these products in the finance office (the deal, Regulation Z, the CARS Rule's history) is Commerce's [F&I: Financing, Leases and Add-Ons](/books/commerce/automotive-retail#f-i-financing-leases-and-add-ons); the lender's obligations once it owns the contract (refunds applied to the account, CFPB findings, state GAP statutes such as Virginia's) are Banking & Lending's [GAP and Add-On Products](/books/banking-and-lending/auto-lending#gap-and-add-on-products). The general service contract regime is in [Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts).

The pattern: the product is sold by the dealer, administered by a company like Allstate or Assurant, obligated either on the administrator's affiliate (third-party obligor) or on the dealer itself (dealer obligor), and backed by insurance. Assurant's naming of dealer obligors as a credit risk is the point of the distinction: when the dealer is the obligor, the program's security depends on the dealer.

## What the Products Are

| Product | Typical legal form | Who regulates it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vehicle service contract (VSC)** | Service contract; in some states mechanical breakdown insurance when an insurer issues it | State service contract law (Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation under ch. 634 Part I; New York's superintendent under Ins. Law Art. 79; Texas's Department of Licensing and Regulation under Occ. Code ch. 1304) |
| **Tire and wheel road hazard, windshield chip repair, paintless dent repair, key and key fob replacement, excess lease wear** | Service contract where the state's definition includes it; New York lists all five in its definition (Ins. Law 7902(k)) | Same as VSCs where included; otherwise insurance |
| **GAP insurance** | Insurance; the NAIC model counts "guaranteed automobile protection (gap) insurance" as limited line credit insurance | Insurance department; sold by a licensed producer or a creditor under a credit limited license (Florida 626.321(1)(e) includes GAP and lets a lender sell to its own borrowers) |
| **GAP waiver (debt cancellation)** | A term of the credit contract: the creditor agrees to cancel the shortfall | Credit law: state GAP waiver statutes; for national banks, the OCC's debt cancellation rule |
| **Depreciation benefit or vehicle protection products** | Varies; Texas defines a "depreciation benefit optional member program" as a service contract paying a credit after a total constructive loss (Occ. Code 1304.003(a)(3)); Florida requires contractual liability insurance for "vehicle protection agreements" and sells them only to holders with comprehensive coverage in force (634.041) | State law by product |

GAP's two forms carry the same economic promise. As insurance, an insurer pays the shortfall to the lender and the premium is insurance premium, taxed, filed and sold under a license. As a waiver, the creditor simply forgives the shortfall, the charge is a finance charge or a contract term, and the creditor typically buys contractual liability insurance to cover its own cost. A national bank's version is a **debt cancellation contract**: "a loan term or contractual arrangement" under which the bank "agrees to cancel all or part of a customer's obligation to repay an extension of credit" on a specified event (12 CFR 37.2(f)), regulated as a banking product.

## Vehicle Service Contract Regulation

**Florida (ch. 634, Part I).** A motor vehicle service agreement company is licensed by the Office of Insurance Regulation and must keep net assets of at least $500,000, and then choose a security basis (634.041):

- an unearned premium reserve in unencumbered assets of at least 50% of the unearned gross written premium on each agreement, amortized pro rata over its term, with gross written premium in force no more than 10 times net assets and a securities deposit equal to 15% of the reserve; or
- contractual liability insurance covering 100% of claim exposure from an insurer or qualifying risk retention group with at least $15 million of surplus, which must pay the holder's claims if the company doesn't, and which can't be cancelled or nonrenewed without 90 days' written notice to the office; or
- for a company with at least $10 million of net assets and audited GAAP statements, either of the above for designated blocks of new agreements.

A motor vehicle manufacturer qualifies with lighter requirements (management competence and information requests, no fingerprints except for officers and directors). Florida's form rules (634.121) are some of the most specific anywhere, and they double as a list of fields:

- **Free cancellation for 60 days.** "Any service agreement is cancelable by the purchaser within 60 days after purchase," with a refund of the full gross premium less claims paid, and an administrative fee of up to 5% of the gross premium.
- **Limited company cancellation after 60 days:** only for material misrepresentation or fraud at sale, failure to maintain the vehicle as the manufacturer prescribes, an odometer tampered with or disabled and not repaired, or nonpayment (with notice by certified mail).
- **Refunds after 60 days:** not less than 100% of the unearned pro rata premium, less claims paid, when the company cancels; not less than 90% when the holder cancels, paid directly to the holder.
- **Lender cancellations** are "only valid if authorized by the terms of the service agreement."
- **The commission comes back too:** "the salesperson and agent are responsible for the refund of the unearned pro rata commission," and if refunds are routed through them they must keep proof and produce it within 45 days of a request.
- **Assignment:** a conspicuous statement of the right to assign the agreement to a later buyer of the vehicle, an assignment period of at least 15 days after the sale, and a fee of no more than $40.
- **Disclosures:** limitations in conspicuous boldface, used or remanufactured parts disclosed, the name, address and Florida license number of the insurer or company, and a notice that "the rate charged for the service agreement is not subject to regulation by the office."

**New York.** Vehicle service contracts are service contracts under Article 79, with the general provider fee filing, financial security and free-look rules ([Financial Security: the Three Options](/books/insurance/service-contracts#financial-security-the-three-options)), and the five vehicle ancillary products written into the definition.

**Texas.** Vehicle service contract providers are regulated under Occupations Code ch. 1304 with the general three options, but the trust deposit scales for dealers: a dealer licensed under ch. 2301 that sells contracts only on vehicles it sells posts $25,000 if its prior-year Texas service contract revenue was $1,125,000 or less, $50,000 up to $2,500,000, and $75,000 above, against a general minimum of $250,000 (1304.151). Texas's definition of normal wear "may include minor and reasonable wear and tear," with small dents repairable by paintless dent removal and worn tire tread among its examples (1304.003(c)).

## Dealer-Obligor and Third-Party-Obligor Programs

Who is obligated on a VSC decides almost everything about the program's data:

| | Third-party obligor | Dealer obligor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who owes the repair** | The administrator's affiliate or an independent obligor | The selling dealer |
| **Who holds the security** | The obligor (usually through reimbursement or contractual liability insurance) | The dealer, through its own reserve and deposit (Texas's dealer amounts) or, more often, reimbursement insurance bought through the administrator |
| **Where the money goes** | The dealer keeps its markup and remits the rest (the obligor's cost) to the administrator | The dealer keeps the contract price; the reserve or the reimbursement premium is set aside from it |
| **Credit risk** | The obligor's | The dealer's: Assurant names "dealer obligors" as a counterparty risk |
| **Who answers a regulator** | The obligor and administrator | The dealer, as a licensed or registered provider |

Many dealer programs add a dealer participation layer, in which the dealer shares in the underwriting results of the contracts it sells (Assurant's 10-K describes "client profit sharing adjustments" in its results, and its risk factors describe up-front payments to clients secured by letters of credit and guarantees). ## Claims and Repair Authorization

A VSC claim is a repair order with a coverage decision in the middle. The NAIC model's disclosure rules describe the shape: if prior approval of repair work is required, the contract must "conspicuously state the procedure for obtaining prior approval and for making a claim, including a toll-free telephone number for claim service and a procedure for obtaining emergency repairs performed outside of normal business hours" (Section 5F), state any deductible, name the covered parts and exclusions, and say when non-original parts may be used. The steps a repair facility and administrator go through:

1. **The car arrives with a complaint.** The repair facility (often the selling dealer's service department, sometimes any licensed facility) confirms the contract, the term and mileage, and the deductible.
2. **Diagnosis.** The facility identifies the failed part and the cause. Teardown to find the cause is a cost the contract may or may not cover if the failure turns out not to be covered, and the contract should say which.
3. **Authorization request.** The facility calls or submits to the administrator before repair, with the failed component, cause, part numbers and prices, and labor hours.
4. **Coverage decision.** The administrator checks the component against the coverage schedule, the cause against exclusions (wear beyond the contract's definition, lack of maintenance, preexisting conditions, modifications), and may require maintenance records or send an inspector. Florida's list of grounds for company cancellation (no maintenance as prescribed, odometer tampering) shows what the administrator will look for.
5. **Authorization with an amount.** An approved repair gets an authorization number and an approved amount for parts and labor; anything above it needs another authorization.
6. **Repair, invoice and payment.** The facility repairs, invoices against the authorization, and is paid directly (usually by card or electronic payment at the time of repair, or on invoice); the holder pays the deductible and anything not covered.
7. **Incidental benefits.** Rental, towing and trip interruption, which the model allows as incidental payments "under limited circumstances," are separate exposures with their own limits.

The claim is a `claims` row with one or more exposures (the repair, the rental, the tow), and the authorization is the record that the repair was approved, by whom, for how much and under which contract term.

## GAP: How a Claim Is Computed

My contract terms and numbers. A buyer finances $31,000 on a 72-month retail installment contract and buys a GAP waiver for $900 (2.9% of the amount financed, inside California's 4% cap). In month 20 the car is totaled.

| Step | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Payoff balance on the loss date | $24,860 |
| Less amounts the waiver excludes under its terms: two past-due payments and late fees | −$1,120 |
| Balance the waiver applies to | $23,740 |
| Less the primary insurer's payment to the lienholder: actual cash value $19,200 less the $500 deductible | −$18,700 |
| Less refunds of the buyer's other cancelled add-ons, credited to the balance (my figure) | −$1,000 |
| **GAP waiver amount** (these terms cover the primary deductible up to $1,000, so it stays in the shortfall) | **$4,040** |
| Buyer still owes | $1,120 (the excluded past-due amounts and fees) |

Check: $18,700 + $1,000 + $4,040 + $1,120 = $24,860, the payoff. Under terms that don't cover the deductible, the waiver would pay $3,540 and the buyer would owe $1,620.

The items that move the number are contract terms, not law: whether past-due payments and fees are excluded, whether the primary deductible is covered and up to what amount, whether a maximum loan-to-value or a dollar cap applies, and whether refunds of other products are credited first. California's statute adds sale-time limits: a seller may not sell a GAP waiver where the amount financed exceeds the waiver's maximum, or where the loan-to-value exceeds the waiver's maximum unless that limit is conspicuously disclosed and the buyer acknowledges in writing that the waiver won't cover the full amount, or where the amount financed is less than 70% of the MSRP (new) or average retail value (used) (Civ. Code 2982.12(a)(5)(B)). The claim system needs the waiver's terms version, the primary settlement, the payoff statement with its components, and the refunds of other products, which is why GAP claims wait for the primary insurer's total loss settlement ([Auto Claims & Total Loss](/books/insurance/auto-claims-and-total-loss#valuation-rules)).

## Refunds on Payoff, Repossession and Total Loss

Every add-on on a car loan has a refund event: early payoff, trade-in, repossession, total loss, or the buyer simply cancelling. The rules come from the product's own law and the contract.

**California GAP waiver.** The buyer may cancel "at any time without penalty," and no cancellation fee may be charged. Within 30 days of purchase, a termination refunds the full charge plus attributable finance charges. After 30 days, the refund is pro rata: total charges multiplied by the days remaining to the original full term date, counting the termination date, divided by the total days in the original term. The holder must pay the refund, or instruct the administrator to, within 60 business days of termination, and keep refund records for four years (Civ. Code 2982.12(b)). Virginia's GAP waiver statute, with its 90-day window to request a refund after early termination of the finance agreement, is covered in Banking & Lending.

**Florida VSC.** After the 60-day free cancellation, a holder who cancels gets at least 90% of the unearned pro rata premium, less claims paid; the commission is refunded pro rata by the salesperson and agent; and a lender may cancel only if the agreement authorizes it (634.121).

### Worked Numbers: A Payoff at Month 24

My numbers. The buyer above bought, on January 15, 2026, a VSC for $2,400 (a 60-month contract, 1,826 days) and the $900 GAP waiver (72 months, 2,191 days). On January 15, 2028 (day 731) the buyer trades in the car and pays off the loan. One VSC claim of $300 was paid.

| Product | Rule | Calculation | Refund |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GAP waiver (California) | Pro rata by days remaining, counting the termination date | $900 × 1,461 / 2,191 | **$600.14**, due within 60 business days |
| VSC (Florida rule, holder cancels) | 90% of unearned pro rata, less claims | $2,400 × 1,096 / 1,826 = $1,440.53; × 90% = $1,296.47; − $300 | **$996.47** |
| VSC dealer commission returned | Unearned pro rata commission | If the dealer kept $900 of the $2,400 as markup and commission: $900 × 1,096 / 1,826 | **$540.20** owed back by the dealer and agent |

Three facts shape the system. The refund is computed on the full retail price the buyer paid, including the dealer's markup, so the dealer owes back its share; that is a receivable from the dealer, tracked against a dealer that may be slow or gone. The lender usually triggers the refund (it sees the payoff), the administrator computes it, and the money goes to the lender to reduce a balance or to the buyer, which is the reconciliation the CFPB found servicers failing at (Banking & Lending). And the 60-business-day clock in California starts at termination, which the administrator may learn about weeks later unless the lender's payoff event reaches it.

## How to Build It

These are deltas against [The Core Data Model](/books/insurance/insurance-business#the-core-data-model) and the service contract tables in [Service Contracts & Warranties](/books/insurance/service-contracts#how-to-build-it): each product is a `policies` row with `legal_form` (`vsc`, `service_contract`, `insurance` for GAP insurance, `debt_cancellation` for a GAP waiver), its `obligor_id` and `backing_policy_id`; the vehicle is a `risks` row of kind `vehicle`; the lender is a `party_roles` row (`lienholder`); a dealer obligor is an `obligors` row of kind `dealer`; repairs, rentals and GAP payments are `claims` and `exposures`. Refunds are `policy_transactions` of kind `cancellation` with `premium_entries`, and commission returns are `commissions` rows of kind `chargeback`. This chapter adds the repair authorization and the add-on's link to the credit contract:

```sql
CREATE TABLE repair_authorizations (       -- the approval behind every covered repair
  id                 UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  exposure_id        UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES exposures (id),
  facility_party_id  UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),
  odometer           INT NOT NULL,
  complaint          TEXT NOT NULL,
  cause              TEXT NOT NULL,         -- failed component and why
  covered_component  TEXT,                  -- the coverage schedule line, or null if denied
  parts              JSONB NOT NULL,        -- [{part_no, qty, price}]
  labor_hours        NUMERIC(6, 2) NOT NULL,
  labor_rate         BIGINT NOT NULL,
  approved_amount    BIGINT,                -- null until decided
  deductible         BIGINT NOT NULL,
  decision           TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (decision IN ('pending', 'approved', 'partial',
                       'denied', 'inspection_required', 'supplemented')),
  denial_reason      TEXT,                  -- 'not_covered', 'maintenance', 'preexisting', ...
  authorization_no   TEXT UNIQUE,
  supersedes_id      UUID REFERENCES repair_authorizations (id),  -- a supplement
  decided_by         UUID,
  decided_at         TIMESTAMPTZ
);

CREATE TABLE credit_contract_links (       -- an add-on financed into a loan
  policy_id          UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES policies (id),
  lender_party_id    UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),
  contract_ref       TEXT NOT NULL,         -- the retail installment contract number
  amount_financed    BIGINT NOT NULL,
  financed_price     BIGINT NOT NULL,       -- this product's price inside the contract
  dealer_retained    BIGINT NOT NULL,       -- markup and commission kept at sale
  term_end           DATE NOT NULL,         -- the loan's original full term date
  terminated_on      DATE,                  -- payoff, repossession, total loss
  termination_kind   TEXT CHECK (termination_kind IN ('payoff', 'trade_in', 'repossession',
                       'total_loss', 'buyer_cancel')),
  refund_due_on      DATE,                  -- CA: 60 business days after termination
  refund_paid_to     TEXT CHECK (refund_paid_to IN ('lender', 'buyer')),
  PRIMARY KEY (policy_id)
);
```

Design choices that matter:

- **One add-on, one contract, one legal form.** A deal with four products is four `policies` rows linked to the same credit contract, because each has its own obligor, refund rule and regulator.
- **Authorizations are versioned** (`supersedes_id`): a supplement for a second failed part is a new row, so the approved amount history is visible.
- **The termination event starts every refund clock.** Payoff and repossession come from the lender, total loss from the insurer, so the system subscribes to those events and computes `refund_due_on` per product and state.
- **Dealer receivables are first-class.** The dealer's share of a refund is a receivable with an owner and an aging, because the administrator often pays the buyer or lender before the dealer pays it back.

## Auditing

- **Every product's legal form, obligor and backing** on the sale date, and the seller's license where the product is insurance (GAP insurance under a credit limited license or a producer license).
- **Authorizations before repairs:** every paid repair has an authorization with an amount at or above the payment, decided by someone with authority.
- **Denials with reasons:** each denial cites the coverage schedule or exclusion and the evidence (maintenance records, inspection).
- **Refunds on time:** every terminated credit contract produced refunds for each add-on, computed by the product's rule and paid within the state's deadline, to the lender or buyer as required.
- **GAP limits at sale:** for California waivers, the price at or under 4% of the amount financed and the LTV and 70% rules checked at sale.
- **Commission returned:** for Florida VSCs, the unearned pro rata commission recovered from the salesperson and agent.

## What This Means for the System You Build

- **Model each add-on separately** with its legal form, obligor, backing, regulator and refund rule.
- **Link every financed add-on to the credit contract** and subscribe to payoff, repossession and total loss events.
- **Compute GAP from documented parts:** payoff components, primary settlement, deductible terms and credited refunds.
- **Make repair authorization the gate for payment,** versioned for supplements, with denial reasons tied to the coverage schedule.
- **Track dealer refund shares as receivables,** not as a footnote to the buyer's refund.
