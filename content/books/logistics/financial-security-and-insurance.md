Federal law makes a motor carrier's authority conditional on money being there when something goes wrong. A for-hire carrier of general freight in a truck of 10,001 pounds or more must carry at least $750,000 of public liability coverage (49 CFR 387.9), and its insurer files proof with FMCSA; a broker or freight forwarder must keep a $75,000 surety bond or trust fund in effect to pay carriers and shippers it fails to pay (387.307, 13906). Registration stays in effect only while the security requirements are met (49 U.S.C. 13906(a)(1)), so when an insurer cancels, the authority goes inactive with it. Cargo coverage is a different story: outside household goods there is no federal cargo insurance minimum, and the market's requirement of $100,000 comes from brokers' and shippers' contracts, not the regulations.

Building for this is hard because three records describe the same coverage and they disagree. The **federal filing** is what the insurer told FMCSA, on a BMC form, for the minimum the law requires. The **certificate of insurance** is what the carrier's agent sends a broker, showing the policies, limits and dates, issued "as a matter of information only." The **policy** itself, with its endorsements, exclusions and scheduled vehicles, is what pays a claim, and nobody outside the carrier sees it. A broker's system that reads one and assumes the others misses the cancellation that has already been filed or the cargo exclusion for the commodity on the load. This chapter covers the liability minimums and their proof, the filings and cancellations, the broker and forwarder security and the drawdown rule that took full effect January 16, 2026, cargo insurance in practice, and the records for monitoring coverage. Insurance products themselves (commercial auto, motor truck cargo, contingent and excess cover, how they're rated) belong to Insurance's [Commercial Lines](/books/insurance/commercial-lines#the-lines), certificates to [Certificates of Insurance](/books/insurance/certificates-of-insurance), and surety bonds as a product to [Surety Bonds](/books/insurance/surety#bond-types).

## How the Regulators Do It

Who must carry what, as of November 2026, from the regulations and statutes, with two large brokers' published requirements for comparison:

| Who | Requirement | Amount | Proof filed with FMCSA | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **For-hire carrier, nonhazardous property, fleet only under 10,001 lb GVWR** | Public liability | $300,000 | As below | 387.303T(b)(1) |
| **For-hire carrier, nonhazardous property, 10,001 lb GVWR or more** | Public liability | $750,000 | BMC-91 or BMC-91X by the insurer; BMC-82 for a surety bond | 387.9, 387.313T |
| **For-hire or private carrier of oil and of hazmat not in the higher rows** | Public liability | $1,000,000 | As above | 387.9 |
| **For-hire or private carrier of bulk hazmat, certain explosives and highway route controlled radioactive material** | Public liability | $5,000,000 | As above | 387.9 |
| **Household goods carrier** | Cargo | $5,000 per vehicle, $10,000 per occurrence | BMC-34 certificate or BMC-83 bond | 387.303T(c), 387.313T |
| **Property broker** | Surety bond or trust fund | $75,000 | BMC-84 (bond) or BMC-85 (trust) | 387.307, 13906(b)(3) |
| **Freight forwarder** | Financial security for unpaid charges | $75,000 "regardless of the number of branch offices or sales agents" | As for brokers | 13906(c)(4) |
| **C.H. Robinson's carriers** | Contract requirement | $1,000,000 auto liability, $100,000 cargo | Certificate from the carrier's agent to Highway | Carrier FAQ |
| **TQL's carriers** | Contract requirement | $1,000,000 auto liability (more where local law requires), $100,000 cargo | Verified with the carrier's agent, monitored for lapses | Carrier vetting page |

Ocean intermediaries have their own FMC bonds ($50,000 for an ocean freight forwarder, $75,000 for an NVOCC; [Who's Who](/books/logistics/logistics-business#who-s-who)), which Freight Forwarders & NVOCCs covers; customs bonds belong to Customs Brokers & Bonds.

Three patterns:

- **The regulation sets a floor for the public, not for the freight.** The 387.9 minimums protect people a truck hurts; they say nothing about the cargo, and the $75,000 broker bond protects carriers and shippers a broker doesn't pay. Brokers and shippers set what protects them (auto liability above the minimum, cargo limits) in contracts, and both large brokers above land on the same figures.
- **The insurer, not the carrier, files.** Every federal filing and cancellation comes from the insurer or surety, so a carrier can't fake an FMCSA filing, but a certificate in a PDF is only as good as its source. Both brokers above refuse certificates from the carrier: C.H. Robinson's must come "directly from your insurance agent," and TQL verifies "directly with the carrier's insurance agent."
- **Cancellation is a dated event with notice.** A policy or bond on file can't be cancelled until 30 days after FMCSA receives the notice (387.313T(d)), so a monitoring system that reads cancellations sees the lapse coming about a month ahead.

## Public Liability Minimums

The minimums in 387.9's schedule of limits, by row:

| Carriage | Commodity | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| For-hire, interstate or foreign, 10,001 pounds GVWR or more | Property (nonhazardous) | $750,000 |
| For-hire and private, interstate, foreign or intrastate, 10,001 pounds or more | Hazardous substances in bulk in cargo tanks, portable tanks or hopper-type vehicles; certain bulk hazard divisions; certain Class 7 quantities | $5,000,000 |
| For-hire and private, interstate or foreign in any quantity, or intrastate in bulk only, 10,001 pounds or more | Oil listed in 172.101; hazardous waste, materials and substances not in the other rows | $1,000,000 |
| For-hire and private, interstate or foreign, under 10,001 pounds | Certain bulk Division 1.1, 1.2 or 1.3 explosives; certain bulk Division 2.3 and 6.1; highway route controlled quantities of Class 7 | $5,000,000 |

The 387.9 table has no general freight row below 10,001 pounds. The filing schedule does: 387.303T, the temporary section that sets the security a registered carrier files for, requires $300,000 for a "Fleet including only vehicles under 10,001 pounds (4,536 kilograms) GVWR" hauling nonhazardous property ((b)(1)(i)), and repeats the $750,000, $1,000,000 and $5,000,000 rows for heavier vehicles and hazmat ((b)(2)). A cargo van carrier with authority therefore files $300,000, and a fleet with a single truck over 10,001 pounds files $750,000.

Three rules sit around the numbers:

- **No coverage, no operation.** "No motor carrier shall operate a motor vehicle until the motor carrier has obtained and has in effect" the required coverage (387.7(a)), and the coverage "shall remain in effect continuously until terminated" (387.7(b)), with 35 days' advance written notice to cancel the endorsement.
- **The proof is an endorsement or a bond.** Acceptable proof is the MCS-90 endorsement on the policy, an MCS-82 surety bond, or FMCSA authorization to self-insure, kept "at the motor carrier's principal place of business" (387.7(d)); a carrier domiciled in a contiguous foreign country carries an English copy in the vehicle. The endorsement "must be in the form prescribed by the FMCSA" (387.15). What the MCS-90 does to the insurer's obligations, and why it isn't coverage for the carrier, is Insurance's question ([Commercial Lines](/books/insurance/commercial-lines#the-lines)).
- **The minimum is per vehicle class, the hazmat row per load.** A carrier that hauls a placardable hazmat load once needs the higher limit in force for that load; a system that checks "insured: yes" without the limit lets a $750,000 carrier take a $1,000,000 load.

## Filings and Cancellations

Proof to FMCSA is a set of standard forms, filed with FMCSA by the insurer or surety (387.313T(a) and (b) for the carrier forms; 387.307 for the broker's bond and trust):

| Form | What it is | Filed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **BMC-91** | Certificate of insurance for the full public liability limit | The insurer |
| **BMC-91X** | Certificate of insurance "to represent full coverage or any level of aggregation," where more than one insurer layers coverage to reach the limit, each filing its own | Each insurer in the layer |
| **BMC-82 / MCS-82** | Surety bond for public liability | The surety |
| **BMC-34** | Certificate of cargo insurance (household goods) | The insurer |
| **BMC-83** | Surety bond for cargo (household goods) | The surety |
| **BMC-84** | Broker or forwarder surety bond | The surety |
| **BMC-85** | Broker or forwarder trust fund agreement | The trustee |
| **BMC-35** | Notice of cancellation of a motor carrier insurance policy | The insurer |
| **BMC-36** | Notice of cancellation of a motor carrier or broker surety bond | The surety |

"Surety bonds, certificates of insurance and other securities or agreements" can't be cancelled or withdrawn "until 30 days after" written notice on the prescribed form, counted from "the date such notice on the prescribed form is actually received by the FMCSA" (387.313T(d)). As with registration ([The Rules in Flux](/books/logistics/operating-authority#motus-and-the-rules-in-flux)), Cornell's 387.313 carries notes that the section was suspended in 2017, amended in 2019 and "again suspended indefinitely," so the operative text is 387.313T, and the forms above are the ones it and the registration notice still use. The BMC-91X matters most in practice: a carrier whose $1,000,000 auto liability is a $750,000 primary and a $250,000 excess layer shows two insurers' filings, and both have to be in force for the minimum to be met.

A filing is not a certificate. A certificate of insurance (usually an ACORD 25) lists the carrier's policies (auto liability, cargo, general liability, sometimes trailer interchange), their limits, dates and the certificate holder, "AS A MATTER OF INFORMATION ONLY" ([What a Certificate Is](/books/insurance/certificates-of-insurance#what-a-certificate-is)); the federal filing is the insurer's representation to the government that the minimum exists. A broker needs both: the filing says the carrier may operate, the certificate says what it carries above that and for cargo.

## Cargo Insurance in Practice

For general freight, a carrier's cargo liability comes from the Carmack Amendment and its tariff or contract, not from an insurance mandate: the carrier is liable "for the actual loss or injury to the property" unless a released rate limits it ([Cargo Liability & Claims](/books/logistics/cargo-claims)). Cargo insurance (motor truck cargo coverage) is how a small carrier pays that liability, and brokers require it because an uninsured carrier's liability is worth only its balance sheet. Three things go wrong often enough to model:

- **Limit below the load.** A $100,000 cargo limit is the common contract floor, but a load of electronics, pharmaceuticals or tobacco can exceed it, and the broker either asks for a higher limit or carries contingent cargo cover itself.
- **Exclusions by commodity or circumstance.** Policies exclude or sublimit classes of goods and conditions (unattended vehicles, reefer breakdown, theft from an unlocked trailer); a certificate doesn't show them. The broker's control is the shipper contract and the carrier agreement, which say who bears an excluded loss, not the certificate.
- **Scheduled vehicles and drivers.** A policy that schedules units covers only those units; a carrier that swaps in an unscheduled truck can be uninsured on that load. The load's tractor and trailer, as dispatched, are worth recording against the coverage.

How these coverages are priced, endorsed and claimed against is Insurance's [Commercial Lines](/books/insurance/commercial-lines#the-lines); this volume records only what the logistics side needs to decide whether to tender.

## Broker and Forwarder Security

A broker "must have a surety bond or trust fund of $75,000 in effect," and FMCSA won't register a broker until it is (387.307(a)). The security pays shippers or motor carriers when the broker fails to pay for transportation it arranged, and the statute limits when: with the broker's consent, when the surety finds the claim valid after the broker doesn't respond, or on a judgment (13906(b)(2)). A trust fund's assets must be convertible to cash within 7 calendar days, and "Acceptable assets included in any trust fund filed under this section are limited to cash," irrevocable letters of credit from federally insured depository institutions, and Treasury bonds (387.307(b)).

The rule that changed the market is the **financial responsibility final rule** of November 16, 2023 (88 FR 78656), effective January 16, 2024, which applies equally to forwarders, removed loan and finance companies as BMC-85 trustees, and created immediate suspension on a drawdown. FMCSA extended the compliance date for the drawdown and suspension provisions by a year, to January 16, 2026, because its new registration system, not the legacy one, would handle the notices (89 FR 107021, December 31, 2024); no further extension appears in the Federal Register through November 2026. The mechanics, from 387.307(e) to (g):

1. **A triggering payment or determination.** The surety pays a claim with the broker's consent, pays a claim the broker "does not respond" to within 7 business days and the surety finds valid, pays a judgment, or determines the broker is in financial failure; in each case, the payment takes the fund below $75,000.
2. **Notice within 2 business days.** The surety or trustee notifies FMCSA electronically and in writing, with the broker's MC or USDOT number, the reason and proof of the payments or a list of pending claims.
3. **Suspension within 7 business days.** FMCSA notifies the broker, whose authority is suspended within 7 business days of service unless it shows the notice was in error, restores the fund to $75,000, or shows the claims were paid without using the security. A broker that doesn't respond is suspended and told in writing.
4. **Financial failure.** A drawdown not cured is "financial failure or insolvency"; the surety starts cancellation, FMCSA publishes it in the FMCSA Register, and claims must be accepted for 60 calendar days after publication. The statute adds that the surety pays claims within 30 days after that period, in full if uncontested or pro rata if claims exceed the security (13906(b)(6)).
5. **Bankruptcy is not failure by itself.** A Title 11 filing alone isn't financial failure (387.307(f)(2)), and the drawdown notice rule doesn't apply once the broker has filed (387.307(e)(2)).
6. **Sureties are policed too.** A surety or trustee that violates the section loses its ability to file financial responsibility instruments for 3 years, after notice and 30 days to contest (387.307(g)); the statute also allows a civil penalty of up to $10,000 (13906(b)(7)).

For a carrier, the practical meaning is that a broker that stops paying now loses its authority within days of the surety's first payment rather than months later, and the 60-day claims window after publication is the deadline to file against the bond. A $75,000 fund split across every unpaid carrier of a failed broker pays little, which is why Paying Carriers, Quick Pay & Factoring treats a broker's payment behavior as a credit question, not an insurance one.

## Monitoring Coverage

Coverage is checked at onboarding and then watched, because every element can change mid-relationship:

- **Federal filing status.** Read with the registration check ([Operating Authority & Registration](/books/logistics/operating-authority#how-to-build-it)): the filing on file, its limit and any pending cancellation with its effective date. A pending cancellation 30 days out is the signal to stop tendering future-dated loads past that date.
- **Certificates from the agent.** Received from the agent or a vetting network (C.H. Robinson routes them through Highway), parsed into coverage lines, and checked against the broker's requirement and the shipper's (a shipper contract can require more than the broker's default).
- **Expiry dates.** Policies renew yearly; a certificate whose policy expires next week is valid today and useless on a load delivering after it. The check compares expiry with the load's delivery window, not today.
- **The load's specifics.** Commodity (hazmat needs the higher liability row, high-value goods may need a higher cargo limit), equipment (a reefer load needs reefer breakdown cover where the shipper requires it) and units (scheduled vehicles).

Vendors sell this monitoring as part of carrier onboarding; Carrier Onboarding & Vetting compares them. The records below are the same whichever vendor feeds them.

## How to Build It

Coverage facts live in four places: the federal filings, the certificates and their coverage lines, the broker's or forwarder's own security, and the requirements each counterparty sets. `documents` already has a `certificate_of_insurance` kind in the core model ([The Core Data Model](/books/logistics/logistics-business#the-core-data-model)), and the certificate's PDF lands there.

```sql
CREATE TABLE insurance_filings (         -- a BMC filing on record with FMCSA, as read
  id                UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  carrier_party_id  UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),  -- the carrier, broker or forwarder
  form              TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (form IN ('BMC-91', 'BMC-91X', 'BMC-82', 'MCS-82',
                      'BMC-34', 'BMC-83', 'BMC-84', 'BMC-85')),
  coverage          TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (coverage IN ('public_liability', 'cargo',
                      'broker_security', 'forwarder_security')),
  insurer_party_id  UUID REFERENCES parties (id),  -- the insurer, surety or trustee
  policy_number     TEXT,
  limit_amount      BIGINT NOT NULL CHECK (limit_amount > 0),  -- minor units; this filer's layer
  attaches_at       BIGINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,     -- for a 91X layer: where it sits in the tower
  currency          CHAR(3) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'USD',
  effective_on      DATE NOT NULL,
  cancellation_received_on  DATE,       -- BMC-35 or BMC-36 received by FMCSA
  cancellation_effective_on DATE,       -- at least 30 days later (387.313T(d))
  source_check_id   UUID REFERENCES carrier_registration_checks (id),
  CHECK (cancellation_effective_on IS NULL OR cancellation_received_on IS NULL
         OR cancellation_effective_on >= cancellation_received_on + 30)
);
CREATE INDEX ON insurance_filings (carrier_party_id, coverage);
CREATE INDEX ON insurance_filings (cancellation_effective_on)
  WHERE cancellation_effective_on IS NOT NULL;

CREATE TABLE insurance_certificates (    -- a certificate of insurance, as received
  id                 UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  insured_party_id   UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),
  holder_party_id    UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),  -- the broker or shipper it was issued to
  producer_party_id  UUID REFERENCES parties (id),  -- the agent who issued it
  received_via       TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (received_via IN ('agent_email', 'vetting_network',
                       'agent_portal', 'carrier_upload')),
  verified_with_agent BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE,
  issued_on          DATE NOT NULL,
  document_id        UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES documents (id),
  superseded_by      UUID REFERENCES insurance_certificates (id),
  received_at        TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW()
);

CREATE TABLE insurance_coverages (       -- one line on a certificate
  id                 UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  certificate_id     UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES insurance_certificates (id),
  line               TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (line IN ('auto_liability', 'motor_truck_cargo',
                       'general_liability', 'excess_umbrella', 'trailer_interchange',
                       'physical_damage', 'workers_compensation', 'contingent_auto',
                       'contingent_cargo', 'other')),
  insurer_party_id   UUID REFERENCES parties (id),
  insurer_naic       TEXT,                -- the NAIC company code as printed
  policy_number      TEXT NOT NULL,
  limit_amount       BIGINT CHECK (limit_amount >= 0),  -- per occurrence, minor units
  deductible         BIGINT CHECK (deductible >= 0),
  currency           CHAR(3) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'USD',
  coverage_during    DATERANGE NOT NULL,  -- effective to expiry
  scheduled_units    TEXT[],              -- VINs or unit numbers, where the policy schedules them
  additional_insured BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE  -- the holder named, per the certificate
);
CREATE INDEX ON insurance_coverages USING gist (coverage_during);

CREATE TABLE insurance_requirements (    -- what a broker or shipper requires, as data
  id                 UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  owner_party_id     UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),  -- the broker's default or a shipper's contract
  applies_to         JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}',  -- {"commodity": "hazmat"}, {"equipment": "reefer"}
  line               TEXT NOT NULL,       -- an insurance_coverages line
  minimum_limit      BIGINT NOT NULL CHECK (minimum_limit >= 0),
  currency           CHAR(3) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'USD',
  valid_during       TSTZRANGE NOT NULL,
  source_document_id UUID REFERENCES documents (id)  -- the shipper contract that sets it
);

CREATE TABLE financial_security_events ( -- a broker's or forwarder's own bond or trust
  id                 UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  principal_party_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),  -- the broker or forwarder
  filing_id          UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES insurance_filings (id),  -- the BMC-84 or 85
  kind               TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('claim_received', 'claim_paid',
                       'judgment_paid', 'drawdown_notice', 'suspension_notice', 'cured',
                       'suspended', 'financial_failure', 'register_published',
                       'claims_window_closed', 'replaced', 'cancelled')),
  claimant_party_id  UUID REFERENCES parties (id),
  amount             BIGINT,              -- minor units, for claims and payments
  currency           CHAR(3) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'USD',
  occurred_on        DATE NOT NULL,
  respond_by         DATE,                -- 7 business days, computed and stored
  document_id        UUID REFERENCES documents (id),
  recorded_at        TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW()
);
```

`DATERANGE` is right for policy terms because policies run by date, while `valid_during` on requirements is an instant range like the rest of the model. The `respond_by` column follows the playbook rule that deadlines are computed once from the business calendar and stored ([Deadlines Are Computed and Stored](/docs/building-blocks/rules-as-data#deadlines-are-computed-and-stored)), since "7 business days" depends on federal holidays.

The question a tender asks: does this carrier's coverage meet every requirement that applies to this load, for the whole time the load is in its hands? A sketch for the auto liability and cargo lines, with the load's delivery date as the date that matters:

```sql
WITH load_window AS (
  SELECT l.id AS load_id, l.carrier_party_id,
         max(upper(s.appointment))::DATE AS last_day
  FROM loads l JOIN stops s ON s.load_id = l.id
  WHERE l.id = $1
  GROUP BY l.id, l.carrier_party_id
), required AS (
  SELECT r.line, max(r.minimum_limit) AS minimum_limit
  FROM insurance_requirements r
  WHERE r.owner_party_id = $2                     -- the broker, and the shipper's contract rows
    AND r.valid_during @> now()
    AND r.applies_to <@ $3::JSONB                  -- the load's facts: commodity, equipment
  GROUP BY r.line
), held AS (
  SELECT c.line, max(c.limit_amount) AS limit_amount
  FROM load_window w
  JOIN insurance_certificates ic ON ic.insured_party_id = w.carrier_party_id
                                AND ic.superseded_by IS NULL
                                AND ic.verified_with_agent
  JOIN insurance_coverages c ON c.certificate_id = ic.id
  WHERE c.coverage_during @> w.last_day
  GROUP BY c.line
)
SELECT r.line, r.minimum_limit, h.limit_amount,
       COALESCE(h.limit_amount, 0) >= r.minimum_limit AS meets
FROM required r LEFT JOIN held h USING (line);
```

Design notes:

- **Filings and certificates are separate tables on purpose.** One comes from FMCSA's records, the other from an agent; joining them on policy number is a useful cross-check (a certificate policy with no filing, or a filing cancelled while the certificate still shows it in force) and never a merge.
- **Layers sum, by filer.** For a BMC-91X tower, the public liability in force is the sum of active layers, each with its own cancellation clock; one layer cancelling drops the carrier below the minimum.
- **Requirements are rules as data.** Broker defaults, shipper contract overrides and commodity rules are rows with validity, so a new shipper contract that requires $250,000 cargo for its loads is a row, not a code change ([The Shape of a Rule](/docs/building-blocks/rules-as-data#the-shape-of-a-rule)).
- **Emit the events.** `insurance.cancellation_pending`, `insurance.certificate_expiring` and `broker_security.drawdown` drive the tender gate, the carrier manager's queue, and for a carrier the decision whether to keep hauling for a broker whose bond is being drawn.

## Auditing

- **What coverage was on file and certified when the carrier was chosen,** for each load: the filing check, the certificate and coverage lines, the requirements in force and the result. A negligent selection claim against a broker turns on what the broker knew or should have checked when it chose the carrier (How Freight Brokerage Works covers the case law), and this record is the answer.
- **Every certificate as received,** with how it arrived and whether it was verified with the agent, kept beyond the policy term for the claim window.
- **Every cancellation notice and when it was seen,** against loads tendered after it.
- **The broker's own bond events,** claims, payments and notices, with the response deadlines met or missed ([Audit Trails](/docs/building-blocks/audit-trails)).

## What This Means for the System You Build

- **Keep federal filings, certificates and requirements as three records,** and cross-check them rather than trusting one.
- **Check coverage against the load,** its commodity, equipment, value and delivery date, not against the carrier in general.
- **Accept certificates only from the agent or a vetting network,** and record how each arrived.
- **Read cancellation notices daily** and stop tendering loads that deliver after the effective date.
- **Model insurance requirements as data** with owners and validity, so shipper contracts override broker defaults without code.
- **For a broker or forwarder, track your own bond's claims and deadlines:** since January 16, 2026, a drawdown suspends authority within 7 business days of notice.
