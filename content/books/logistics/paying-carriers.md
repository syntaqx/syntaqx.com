Most trucking companies are small, they pay for fuel and drivers every week, and their customers pay in a month. That gap is the business that sits between a delivered load and a carrier's bank account: brokers offer **quick pay** for a discount, **factors** buy the carrier's invoices for cash now and collect from the broker later, and leased owner-operators are paid on settlements a federal regulation times. Triumph's factoring business, which buys "invoices generated by small to medium sized trucking fleets," purchased $11.699 billion of them in 2025, and says those invoices "are typically paid 30 to 60 days after delivery." TQL's published terms are 28 days, with 1-day pay at 5% off and 7-day pay at 3%; C.H. Robinson's quick pay fee is "standardized at 2.0%." A broker paying thousands of carriers a week is therefore paying many of them through someone else.

It is hard because who is owed changes while the amount doesn't. A carrier that signs with a factor sends a notice of assignment, and from then on paying the carrier doesn't discharge the debt: the broker that pays the wrong party pays twice. Carriers change factors, factors release carriers, notices arrive for some invoices and not others, and fraud aims at exactly this seam with forged notices, hijacked email and new bank accounts. Meanwhile the carrier wants its money on time, by the rail it chose, with a remittance that says which loads it covers. This chapter covers how brokers and factors do it, pay terms and quick pay, owner-operator settlement timing, factoring from the payer's side (notices, releases and proof), resolving the payee for each invoice (with a resolver in Go and Rust), the controls against payment diversion, the rails, and the broker's own credit. Factoring as a financial product (sale of receivables, recourse, UCC filings, state disclosure laws) is [Invoice Factoring and Financing](/books/banking-and-lending/small-business-lending#invoice-factoring-and-financing); the payment rails are [Vendor Payouts](/books/payments/vendor-payouts) and [ACH & Bank Transfers](/books/payments/ach). The fraud schemes themselves are Freight Fraud & Cargo Theft's.

## How the Brokers and Factors Do It

As of November 2026, from carrier pages, a published broker-carrier agreement, a factor's guide and filings:

Three patterns:

- **Speed is priced as a discount on the payable.** Quick pay is a percentage off the carrier's invoice for paying in days instead of weeks: 2.0% at one broker, 3% to 5% at another depending on the speed. It's the carrier's choice per load or per account, and it belongs on the payable as its own line.
- **Factors become the payee, and the paperwork goes to them.** Once a carrier factors, its documents and its money both route through the factor (TQL says so outright), and the broker's obligation runs to the factor.
- **Brokers write their own notice rules on top of the UCC.** Flock Freight's 15-day delay and its rule for conflicting notices are contract terms layered on the statute's rule that, after notification, paying the carrier no longer discharges the debt. Whether a contractual delay holds against a factor is a legal question the contract can't settle on its own (below).

## Terms, Quick Pay and Offsets

**Terms** are the contract's: the broker-carrier agreement or the shipper's carrier contract sets them, usually from receipt of an undisputed invoice with its documents, and the rate confirmation can override them per load. The regulatory credit rules in 49 CFR 377 run the other way (a carrier extending credit to its shipper, [Presentment, Credit and the Clocks](/books/logistics/freight-invoicing#presentment-credit-and-the-clocks)); nothing federal sets how fast a broker pays a carrier, which is why the broker's own record of "the date it paid the carrier" (49 CFR 371.3) and its bond are what a carrier has.

**Quick pay** is modeled as an election and a line:

- The **election** records that the carrier chose quick pay for this invoice or for every invoice, the offer it accepted (the speed and the percentage in force that day), and when. A standing election is a carrier setting with a validity range, not a flag on each load.
- The **fee** is a payable `freight_charges` row of kind `quick_pay_fee`, negative, computed once from the approved amount and the offer's percentage ([A Brokered Truckload](/books/logistics/logistics-business#a-brokered-truckload) shows $48.48 on a $2,424.00 payable at 2%). It's computed on the approved amount, after audit, and it is never folded into the linehaul: the rate confirmation's price stays what was agreed.
- The **due date** moves: the invoice's due date is recomputed from the quick pay offer's days, from the event the offer names (approval, or receipt of complete documents).

**Offsets** are amounts the broker withholds: a cargo claim charged back under the agreement, an overpayment on another load, an advance already paid (a fuel advance or a lumper paid by the broker on the carrier's behalf). Each is a line on the payment with what it offsets, under the agreement's setoff clause (Flock Freight's gives the broker the right "to withhold and/or set off any payment owing to Carrier"), and an offset against a factored invoice is an offset against the factor's money, which is why factoring agreements and NOAs address it.

## Owner-Operators on Lease

A leased owner-operator isn't a carrier the broker pays; it's paid by the motor carrier it leases to, on a settlement, and the regulation sets the clock: payment "within 15 days after submission of the necessary delivery documents," and the carrier may not set time limits for submitting them (49 CFR 376.12(f)). When pay is a percentage of revenue, the owner-operator is entitled to the rated freight bill, and every deduction (fuel advances, insurance, escrow) is listed with its basis ([Owner-Operators and Leases](/books/logistics/truckload#owner-operators-and-leases)). The settlement statement and its lines are Carrier Dispatch & Asset Management's `driver_settlements`; what this chapter adds is that the settlement's payment is a carrier payment like any other, to a payee who may also have factored, on a rail, with the same payee controls.

## Factoring From the Payer's Side

Factoring is a sale of the carrier's receivable to the factor, with or without recourse ([Invoice Factoring and Financing](/books/banking-and-lending/small-business-lending#invoice-factoring-and-financing)). The broker (or shipper) whose invoice was sold is the **account debtor**, and three provisions of UCC 9-406 decide what it must do:

- **After notification, pay only the factor.** The account debtor may pay the carrier "until, but not after," it receives a notification "authenticated by the assignor or the assignee" that "the amount due or to become due has been assigned," and after that it "may not discharge the obligation by paying the assignor" (9-406(a)).
- **The notice must identify what was assigned.** A notification is ineffective "if it does not reasonably identify the rights assigned" (9-406(b)(1)). A blanket notice covering all of a carrier's receivables is common; one for specific invoices covers only those.
- **The payer can ask for proof.** On request, the factor must "seasonably furnish reasonable proof that the assignment has been made," and if it doesn't, the account debtor "may discharge its obligation by paying the assignor" despite the notice (9-406(c)).

From these the operating rules follow:

- **A notice applies from when it was received,** to every invoice it identifies that hasn't been paid. An invoice paid to the carrier before the notice arrived was discharged; one paid after it was not, and Apex's guide warns that "this could result in the invoice being paid twice."
- **A broker's contractual delay is a risk position.** Flock Freight's agreement says an assignment "shall not take effect until fifteen (15) days after Broker receives the Notice of Release." The statute's discharge rule turns on the account debtor's receipt of notification, and 9-406(d) makes many contract terms restricting assignment of a receivable ineffective, so a broker relying on a delay should have counsel's view of it; my default is to apply a verified notice on receipt and hold the carrier's payments in the meantime, which is safe under either reading.
- **Releases end a notice, and only the factor can give one.** When a carrier leaves a factor, the factor sends a release letter ("An NOA release letter is issued when a trucking company ends its relationship with a factoring company"); a new factor's notice for a carrier whose current factor hasn't released is a conflict to resolve before paying anyone, not a replacement.
- **Conflicting notices freeze the payee.** Two factors claiming the same carrier's invoices, or a notice and a release that disagree, put the invoice on hold until the factors and carrier agree; Flock Freight's agreement releases the broker if it complies with one of several conflicting notices, which is a contract position, not a guarantee against a factor's claim.
- **The documents go to the factor.** A factored carrier submits its paperwork through the factor, so the broker's invoice intake must accept a factor as the submitter of a carrier's invoice, and match it to the same load.

## Resolving the Payee

The core model puts the payee on the invoice, not on the carrier ([The Core Data Model](/books/logistics/logistics-business#the-core-data-model)'s design notes), because the right payee depends on the invoice and on what the payer had received when. My rule: resolve the payee when an invoice is approved for payment, from the notice history as it stood at that moment, record which notice decided, and resolve again (never silently) if a notice or release arrives before the money leaves.

The rule reads a carrier's notice history, each with its factor, scope, received time, verification state and any release:

```go tab="Go"
// Notice is a notice of assignment as recorded: who it assigns to, which
// invoices it covers, and when it began and stopped applying.
type Notice struct {
	ID         string
	FactorID   string
	Scope      string          // "all_receivables" or "listed_invoices"
	InvoiceIDs map[string]bool // for "listed_invoices"
	ReceivedAt time.Time
	VerifiedAt *time.Time // confirmed with the factor through a contact on file
	ReleasedAt *time.Time // the factor's release letter, received
}

type Payee struct {
	PartyID  string
	NoticeID string // empty: paid to the carrier
	Hold     string // non-empty: don't pay; why
}

// ResolvePayee decides who an invoice is paid to at time at. A notice received
// by then applies until its release; an unverified one holds payment; two that
// apply at once are a conflict.
func ResolvePayee(carrierID, invoiceID string, notices []Notice, at time.Time) Payee {
	var applying []Notice
	for _, n := range notices {
		if n.ReceivedAt.After(at) {
			continue
		}
		if n.ReleasedAt != nil && !n.ReleasedAt.After(at) {
			continue
		}
		if n.Scope == "listed_invoices" && !n.InvoiceIDs[invoiceID] {
			continue
		}
		applying = append(applying, n)
	}
	switch {
	case len(applying) == 0:
		return Payee{PartyID: carrierID}
	case len(applying) > 1:
		return Payee{Hold: "conflicting_notices"}
	case applying[0].VerifiedAt == nil || applying[0].VerifiedAt.After(at):
		return Payee{PartyID: applying[0].FactorID, NoticeID: applying[0].ID, Hold: "notice_unverified"}
	default:
		return Payee{PartyID: applying[0].FactorID, NoticeID: applying[0].ID}
	}
}
```

```rust tab="Rust"
use chrono::{DateTime, Utc};
use std::collections::HashSet;

/// A notice of assignment as recorded: who it assigns to, which invoices it
/// covers, and when it began and stopped applying.
pub struct Notice {
    pub id: String,
    pub factor_id: String,
    pub listed_invoices: Option<HashSet<String>>, // None: all receivables
    pub received_at: DateTime<Utc>,
    pub verified_at: Option<DateTime<Utc>>, // confirmed with the factor through a contact on file
    pub released_at: Option<DateTime<Utc>>, // the factor's release letter, received
}

#[derive(Debug, PartialEq)]
pub enum Payee {
    Carrier(String),
    Factor { party_id: String, notice_id: String },
    Hold { reason: &'static str, factor_id: Option<String> },
}

/// Decides who an invoice is paid to at time `at`. A notice received by then
/// applies until its release; an unverified one holds payment; two that apply at
/// once are a conflict.
pub fn resolve_payee(carrier_id: &str, invoice_id: &str, notices: &[Notice], at: DateTime<Utc>) -> Payee {
    let applying: Vec<&Notice> = notices
        .iter()
        .filter(|n| n.received_at <= at)
        .filter(|n| n.released_at.map_or(true, |r| r > at))
        .filter(|n| n.listed_invoices.as_ref().map_or(true, |l| l.contains(invoice_id)))
        .collect();
    match applying.as_slice() {
        [] => Payee::Carrier(carrier_id.to_string()),
        [n] if n.verified_at.map_or(true, |v| v > at) => Payee::Hold {
            reason: "notice_unverified",
            factor_id: Some(n.factor_id.clone()),
        },
        [n] => Payee::Factor { party_id: n.factor_id.clone(), notice_id: n.id.clone() },
        _ => Payee::Hold { reason: "conflicting_notices", factor_id: None },
    }
}
```

The two versions encode the same decisions: a notice counts from receipt (not from verification, because the statute's clock runs from receipt), an unverified notice holds payment rather than paying the carrier, a release counts from when it was received, and two applying notices stop everything. What they leave to policy: whether a notice older than the invoice but outside its listed scope applies (here, no), and how long an unverified hold may last before someone calls the factor again.

## Payment Diversion Controls

The fraud aims at the payee: a spoofed email from the carrier's "accounting" changing the bank account, a forged notice naming a factor the carrier never used, a real factor's notice ignored so the carrier is paid twice. The general controls on changing a payee's bank details (verify out of band, validate the account, hold the first payment, separate duties) are Payments' ([Stopping the Changed Bank Account](/books/payments/vendor-payouts#stopping-the-changed-bank-account)). What freight adds:

- **Verify every notice with the factor, not the sender.** Call or message the factor through contact details already on file for that factor (from its own website or a prior relationship), never the phone number or email on the notice. A factor the system has never seen is verified as a party first: its legal name, its address and its own bank account ownership.
- **Know the factors.** Factors are a small, repeat population; keep them as parties with the `factor` role and verified remit accounts, so a notice naming an unknown "factor" stands out.
- **Pin bank accounts to parties, with history.** A remit account belongs to the carrier or a factor, with the date it was verified and how; an invoice is paid only to an account that belongs to its resolved payee, and a change to an account is a new row, never an edit ([Amend, Don't Overwrite](/docs/building-blocks/audit-trails#amend-don-t-overwrite)).
- **Watch the timing.** A remit change, a new notice or a new contact arriving between pickup and payment of a high-value load, or across several loads at once, is a signal; hold and verify.
- **Pay to the load's real carrier.** A payee resolution starts from the carrier the load was tendered to and picked up by; an invoice from another carrier for the same load is a double-brokering question to resolve before anyone is paid.
- **Notify on change.** Tell the carrier's verified contact and the previous payee when payment instructions change, so the legitimate party learns before the money moves.

## Rails and Remittance

Carriers are paid by ACH most often, by check where they still ask for it, by card or instant rails where the payer offers them for a fee, and through network wallets such as Triumph's LoadPay, which funds approved invoices into "a digital bank account developed for Carriers." The rails, their cutoffs, returns and fees are Payments' ([The Methods](/books/payments/vendor-payouts#the-methods), [Returns: When Money Is Really Yours](/books/payments/ach#returns-when-money-is-really-yours)). Freight-specific requirements:

- **Remittance by load.** One payment usually covers many invoices; the remittance must list each with the carrier's invoice number and the payer's load number, the approved amount, any quick pay fee, short pay with its reason and offset with its reference, because the carrier (or its factor) applies cash by load ([Remittance and Reconciliation](/books/payments/vendor-payouts#remittance-and-reconciliation)).
- **A return reopens the invoice.** An ACH return to a carrier's account means the invoice is unpaid again, and the account is suspect until re-verified; the payment row moves to `returned` and the invoice back to `approved`, never deleted.
- **Tax reporting follows the payee.** Information returns are the payer's obligation to the party it paid and are Payments' ([Tax: W-9s, TIN Matching and 1099s](/books/payments/vendor-payouts#tax-w-9s-tin-matching-and-1099s)); a factor that collects a carrier's invoice is a payee with its own tax identity, so W-9s are kept per party, not per carrier.

## The Broker's Credit

A carrier's protection when a broker doesn't pay is narrow: the broker's $75,000 bond or trust fund, claims on which now trigger suspension of the broker's authority within days of a drawdown ([Broker and Forwarder Security](/books/logistics/financial-security-and-insurance#broker-and-forwarder-security)). A $75,000 fund split among a failed broker's unpaid carriers pays little, so carriers and factors treat a broker's payment behavior as credit risk: factors underwrite the account debtor ("account debtors represent the substantive underlying credit risk," in Triumph's words), and carriers check how fast a broker pays. For the broker's system the consequence is that payment timeliness is a measured, exposed fact: days from complete documents to payment, by carrier and by month, and the date of every payment in the record 49 CFR 371.3 requires, open to each party to the transaction.

## How to Build It

The deltas add terms and quick pay elections, notices of assignment and releases, remit accounts with history, and the payments themselves. `carrier_payments` is one row per invoice paid, so the broker's transaction record can read each load's payment date directly (How Freight Brokerage Works builds that view):

```sql
CREATE TABLE carrier_payment_terms (           -- how and when this payer pays this carrier
  id                UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  payer_party_id    UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),
  carrier_party_id  UUID REFERENCES parties (id),  -- null: the payer's default
  standard_days     SMALLINT NOT NULL CHECK (standard_days BETWEEN 0 AND 120),
  starts_from       TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (starts_from IN ('invoice_received', 'documents_complete',
                      'delivery', 'approval')),
  source_document_id UUID REFERENCES documents (id),  -- the agreement version that says so
  valid_during      TSTZRANGE NOT NULL
);

CREATE TABLE quick_pay_offers (                -- a published speed and price
  id              UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  payer_party_id  UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),
  days            SMALLINT NOT NULL CHECK (days BETWEEN 0 AND 30),
  fee_bp          INT NOT NULL CHECK (fee_bp BETWEEN 0 AND 10000),  -- 200 = 2.0%
  valid_during    TSTZRANGE NOT NULL
);

CREATE TABLE quick_pay_elections (             -- the carrier's choice
  id                 UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  quick_pay_offer_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES quick_pay_offers (id),
  carrier_party_id   UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),
  freight_invoice_id UUID REFERENCES freight_invoices (id),  -- null: a standing election
  valid_during       TSTZRANGE,                -- for a standing election
  elected_at         TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  elected_via        TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (elected_via IN ('portal', 'app', 'api', 'phone', 'email')),
  CHECK ((freight_invoice_id IS NULL) = (valid_during IS NOT NULL))

CREATE TABLE assignment_notices (              -- notices of assignment and their releases
  id                  UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  carrier_party_id    UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),  -- the assignor
  factor_party_id     UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),  -- the assignee
  account_debtor_id   UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES parties (id),  -- this payer
  scope               TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (scope IN ('all_receivables', 'listed_invoices')),
  listed_invoice_ids  UUID[],                  -- for 'listed_invoices'
  received_at         TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,    -- 9-406(a) runs from here
  document_id         UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES documents (id),  -- the notice as received
  verified_at         TIMESTAMPTZ,
  verified_via        TEXT,                    -- 'call_to_factor_on_file', 'factor_portal'
  verified_by         TEXT,
  proof_requested_at  TIMESTAMPTZ,             -- 9-406(c)
  proof_received_at   TIMESTAMPTZ,
  released_at         TIMESTAMPTZ,             -- the factor's release, received
  release_document_id UUID REFERENCES documents (id),
  status              TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (status IN ('received', 'verified', 'disputed',
                        'released', 'rejected')),
  CHECK ((scope = 'listed_invoices') = (listed_invoice_ids IS NOT NULL)),
  CHECK (released_at IS NULL OR release_document_id IS NOT NULL),
  CHECK (verified_at IS NULL OR (verified_via IS NOT NULL AND verified_by IS NOT NULL))

CREATE INDEX ON assignment_notices (carrier_party_id, account_debtor_id, received_at);

  currency              CHAR(3) NOT NULL,
  scheduled_on          DATE NOT NULL,

  CHECK (net_amount = approved_amount - quick_pay_fee - offset_amount),
  CHECK (status <> 'held' OR hold_reason IS NOT NULL)
);
CREATE INDEX ON carrier_payments (load_id);
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX carrier_payments_one_live_per_invoice ON carrier_payments (freight_invoice_id)

CREATE TABLE carrier_payment_offsets (         -- what was withheld, and why
  id                 UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
  carrier_payment_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES carrier_payments (id),
  kind               TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('cargo_claim', 'overpayment', 'advance',
                       'lumper_paid', 'other')),
  amount             BIGINT NOT NULL CHECK (amount > 0),
  reference_id       UUID,                     -- the claim, payment or charge it offsets
  agreement_clause   TEXT NOT NULL             -- the setoff clause relied on
```

Invoices approved for payment whose payee needs another look before the next run, because a notice or release arrived after the payee was resolved:

```sql
SELECT fi.id, fi.number, fi.payee_party_id, n.id AS notice_id, n.factor_party_id,
       n.received_at, n.released_at
FROM freight_invoices fi
JOIN assignment_notices n
  ON n.carrier_party_id = fi.issuer_party_id
 AND n.account_debtor_id = fi.bill_to_party_id
WHERE fi.side = 'payable'
  AND fi.status = 'approved'
  AND (n.received_at > fi.payee_resolved_at OR n.released_at > fi.payee_resolved_at)
  AND (n.scope = 'all_receivables' OR fi.id = ANY (n.listed_invoice_ids))
  AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM carrier_payments p
                  WHERE p.freight_invoice_id = fi.id AND p.status IN ('sent', 'settled'));
```

Design notes:

- **The payee is resolved, recorded and re-resolved.** `freight_invoices.payee_party_id` is set by the resolver with the notice that decided it; any later notice or release sends the invoice back through it before payment, never after.
- **One live payment per invoice.** The partial unique index stops a second payment while the first is live; a returned or cancelled payment frees the invoice for a new one, and both rows stay.
- **Account numbers live in a vault.** `party_remit_accounts.account_ref` points at it; the row keeps the last four digits, the validated owner name and how it was verified ([Validating the Account](/books/payments/ach#validating-the-account)).
- **Fees and offsets are lines, not discounts.** The quick pay fee is also a `quick_pay_fee` charge on the load and offsets are their own rows, so margin, the carrier's statement and the remittance agree.
- **Emit the events.** `assignment_notice.received`, `assignment_notice.verified`, `assignment_notice.released`, `party_remit_account.changed`, `carrier_payment.held`, `carrier_payment.sent` and `carrier_payment.returned` feed payment runs, carrier notifications, fraud review and the broker's record.

## Auditing

- **Every notice and release as received,** with its received time, verification (who, how, through which contact) and proof requests.
- **Every payee decision:** which notice decided it, when, and every re-resolution.
- **Every remit account and its history,** with the request that created it, validation and out-of-band verification.
- **Every payment** with its payee, account, rail, quick pay fee, offsets and their clauses, sent and settled times, and returns.
- **Days to pay,** by carrier and month, from complete documents to payment, and the 371.3 payment date for every brokered load.

## What This Means for the System You Build

- **Put the payee on the invoice,** resolved from notice history at approval, with the deciding notice recorded.
- **Apply a notice from receipt,** hold until it's verified with the factor through a contact on file, and freeze the payee on conflicts.
- **Model quick pay as an election and a negative line,** computed on the approved amount, with the offer that priced it.
- **Keep remit accounts as history per party,** validated, verified out of band, and paid only when they belong to the resolved payee.
- **Pay one invoice once:** one live payment per invoice, returns reopen it, and every offset names its clause.
- **Send remittance by load,** because carriers and factors apply cash by load.
- **Measure and expose how fast you pay,** since carriers and factors price your credit by it.
